# Paper A — Exact Recovery of Finite-Range Functions on Arbitrary Product Domains

**Devin J. Wright · NIGHTFALL Constellation · public research manuscript v1.0 · 11 November 2026**

**Scientific status.** Full author-side analytic manuscript, released for adversarial review. The finite switching and section lemmas have separately scoped Lean receipts; this *whole* theorem, especially the completed-product measurable-selection and simultaneous-hybrid arguments, has **not** been independently refereed or compiled end to end. No global originality, journal acceptance, or efficient-runtime claim is made.

## Abstract

We study exact adaptive observation of bounded-interaction functions on products of arbitrary independent probability spaces. For a real observable taking only \(q\) distinct values, of Hoeffding degree at most \(d\), we derive an almost-sure dimension-free observation bound
\[
D_\mu(f)\le d\Big\lfloor d^2\min\{(q-1)^2,\;2R/\Delta\}\Big\rfloor ,
\]
where \(R\) is the output range and \(\Delta\) its least nonzero gap. The argument combines maximal-order Hoeffding interactions with disjoint sensitive-block switches, Midrijanis-type degree–block-sensitivity decision-tree reasoning, Tal's finite Boolean block-sensitivity theorem, and a Markov endpoint derivative estimate. A finite-vector-palette extension follows by separating the palette with a linear functional. We give an independent measure-theoretic argument for completed products with arbitrarily many coordinates and potentially nonatomic, heterogeneous input alphabets. Exact recovery is almost sure and existential: coordinate choice may depend on the known function and law, and no running-time bound is asserted.

## 1. Model and precise theorem

Let \(J\) be any index set, \((\Omega_i,\mathcal F_i,\mu_i)_{i\in J}\) any family of probability spaces, and \((\Omega,\mathcal F,\mu)=\bigotimes_{i\in J}(\Omega_i,\mathcal F_i,\mu_i)\), optionally completed. Every observation reveals one whole coordinate \(X_i\). The decision rule may depend measurably on previously revealed symbols, on \(f\), and on the known product law. It is not a statistical learning algorithm.

For countable \(J\), write \(f=\sum_{S\subset J,\;|S|<\infty} f_S\) for its orthogonal Hoeffding/Efron–Stein expansion in \(L^2(\mu)\). Its interaction degree is at most \(d\) when all \(f_S\) with \(|S|>d\) vanish. For arbitrary \(J\), equivalently take the \(L^2\)-closed subspace generated by functions of at most \(d\) coordinates; the reduction in Section 5 makes the two conventions compatible.

**Theorem A1 (real finite-palette exact recovery; analytic).** Let \(f:\Omega\to\{a_1,\ldots,a_q\}\subset\mathbb R\) be measurable modulo completion, \(q\ge2\), with distinct palette values and Hoeffding degree at most \(d\ge1\). Put
\[
R=\max_j a_j-\min_j a_j,\qquad \Delta=\min_{j\ne k}|a_j-a_k|>0.
\]
Then a deterministic measurable adaptive procedure determines \(f(X)\) almost surely after at most
\[
\boxed{d\left\lfloor d^2\min\left\{(q-1)^2,\frac{2R}{\Delta}\right\}\right\rfloor}
\tag{A1}
\]
whole-coordinate observations. For degree zero or a singleton palette, no queries are needed.

In particular, Boolean-valued functions need at most \(d^3\) observations. For an equally spaced real palette, (A1) gives at most \(d^3\min\{(q-1)^2,2(q-1)\}\). The older v0.2 manuscript wrote \(R/\Delta\) in place of \(2R/\Delta\), based on a stronger pointwise \(L^1\)-derivative route. We preserve that historical proposal, but **the present manuscript invokes only the explicit two-sided endpoint-Markov argument and claims the conservative factor two**.

**Theorem A2 (finite vector palettes).** Let \(H\) be a real Hilbert space, or a real Banach space whose continuous dual separates the finite set in question. If a strongly measurable \(H\)-valued \(F\) has interaction degree at most \(d\) and range in a known finite set \(P=\{v_1,\ldots,v_q\}\) of distinct vectors, then \(F(X)\) can be recovered almost surely using at most \(d^3(q-1)^2\) whole-coordinate observations. More precisely, for any continuous real linear functional \(\ell\) injective on \(P\), (A1) applies with \(R_\ell=\max\ell(P)-\min\ell(P)\) and \(\Delta_\ell=\min_{u\ne v}|\ell(u)-\ell(v)|\). This theorem does **not** extend the same bound to infinite-range Hilbert observables.

**Corollary A3 (sharp degree-one count).** For a nonconstant real finite-palette degree-one observable, there are at most \(q-1\) essential coordinates and they can be read nonadaptively. This count is attained by a sum of \(q-1\) independent nondegenerate Boolean input coordinates. Thus the degree-one optimal bound is \(q-1\), sharper than (A1).

## 2. Finite-product switching mechanism

First consider a finite product of finite full-support laws; the measure-theoretic extension is proved separately below. Suppose \(g\) is a nonconstant posterior section of \(f\) on the as-yet-unobserved product, still of degree at most \(d\). Let \(k\) be its maximal nonzero Hoeffding order and choose a nonzero \(g_S\) with \(|S|=k\le d\).

The product alternating difference operator \(D_S=\prod_{i\in S}(\mathrm{Id}-\mathbb E_i)\) satisfies \(D_Sg=g_S\), since every nonzero component of order \(k\) meeting \(S\) must equal \(S\). Therefore the restriction of \(g\) to the coordinates in \(S\) is nonconstant at every complementary symbol in the finite full-support setting: otherwise \(D_Sg\) would vanish. For each actual section symbol, at least one alternative whole-block symbol changes \(g\). Reveal all coordinates in \(S\).

If the resulting posterior is not constant, repeat. The selected blocks \(S_1,\ldots,S_m\) are pairwise disjoint, because a previously observed coordinate never re-enters the residual product. At the original input \(x\), fix changing alternatives \(y_{S_j}\) for each block. The hybrid table
\[
h(z)=f\big(x\text{ with block }S_j\text{ replaced by }y_{S_j}\text{ whenever }z_j=1\big),
\qquad z\in\{0,1\}^{m},
\]
has Boolean polynomial degree at most \(d\): a term depending on at most \(d\) original coordinates depends on at most \(d\) disjoint block switches. Every singleton switch at \(z=0\) changes the output, so
\[
|h(e_j)-h(0)|\ge\Delta\quad(j=1,\ldots,m). \tag{2.1}
\]

### 2.1 Midrijanis–Tal block-sensitivity route

Set \(b(z)=\mathbf 1_{\{h(z)=h(0)\}}\). Lagrange interpolation on the \(q\) output symbols expresses \(b\) as a polynomial of degree at most \(q-1\) in \(h\). Hence \(\deg b\le(q-1)d\). All \(m\) singleton masks are sensitive at zero, so \(bs_0(b)\ge m\). The previously established Boolean-cube inequality \(bs(b)\le\deg(b)^2\) (Tal) implies
\[
m\le(q-1)^2d^2. \tag{2.2}
\]
The algorithmic pattern—repeatedly discover and query an entire sensitive block, then bound the number of disjoint blocks by polynomial degree—is the classical **Midrijanis degree/block-sensitivity decision-tree method**, not an original NIGHTFALL invention. The improvement under study is the measure-space and finite-palette transfer.

### 2.2 Range/gap refinement using the endpoint Markov inequality

Partition the switches into those for which \(h(e_j)-h(0)>0\) and those for which it is negative. For either part \(A\), the Bernstein polynomial
\[
P_A(t)=\mathbb E[h(Z_A(t))],\qquad Z_j(t)\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\mathrm{Bernoulli}(t)
\]
(with all switches outside \(A\) fixed to zero) has degree at most \(d\), takes its values in the interval of length \(R\), and obeys \(P_A'(0)=\sum_{j\in A}(h(e_j)-h(0))\). Markov's endpoint inequality on \([0,1]\) gives \(|P_A'(0)|\le d^2R\): rescale the domain to \([-1,1]\) and subtract the output midpoint. Applying this to both sign parts yields
\[
m\Delta\le\sum_j|h(e_j)-h(0)|\le2d^2R,\qquad
m\le 2d^2R/\Delta. \tag{2.3}
\]
Together with (2.2), \(m\le\lfloor d^2\min\{(q-1)^2,2R/\Delta\}\rfloor\). Each block costs at most \(d\) coordinates; this proves (A1) in finite full-support products. Zero-probability alphabet symbols are discarded before applying the argument; no minimum positive mass is needed.

## 3. Finite vector-palette extension and degree-one sharpness

For a finite set \(P\) of distinct vectors, each pair \(v_j-v_k\) defines a proper hyperplane of separating functionals. A finite union of proper hyperplanes cannot exhaust the dual of a real Hilbert space (nor the separating dual of a Banach space). Choose \(\ell\) outside that union. Then \(\ell\circ F\) has the same or lower Hoeffding degree, has exactly \(q\) distinct scalar values, and uniquely identifies \(F\) through the fixed finite lookup table \(\ell(v_j)\mapsto v_j\). Apply Theorem A1. This proves Theorem A2 without a norm-gap assumption.

For degree one, the nonconstant centered Hoeffding terms on independent coordinates add. Each essential coordinate contributes at least two distinguishable values. The Minkowski sum of two nonempty finite subsets of \(\mathbb R\) contains at least \(|A|+|B|-1\) points. Hence if \(n\) coordinate terms are nonconstant, the sum has at least \(n+1\) output values, so \(n\le q-1\). The example \(f(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{q-1}X_i\) with independent nondegenerate \(X_i\in\{0,1\}\) attains this count.

## 4. Why adaptive and fixed observations differ

Let \(K\) be an address taking \(N\) positive-probability values and let \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_N\) be independent nondegenerate fair bits, independent of \(K\). Set \(f(K,Z)=Z_K\). Every summand in
\[
f=\sum_{j=1}^N\mathbf1_{\{K=j\}}Z_j
\]
depends on only \(K\) and \(Z_j\), so \(\deg(f)\le2\), and the degree-two term is nonzero. Reading \(K\), then \(Z_K\), recovers the function in two adaptive queries. Any fixed exact determining set must include all \(N\) bits and the address, hence needs \(N+1\) queries. With countably many possible addresses of positive probability, the adaptive depth remains two while no finite nonadaptive determining set exists. This is an illustrative construction, not an efficient algorithm for general functions.

## 5. Independent measure-theoretic proof for arbitrary measurable products

This section does not infer an infinite-product theorem from a finite Lean declaration.

**Lemma 5.1 (countable effective support).** Every real function measurable for the product sigma algebra is pointwise measurable with respect to a countable set of coordinates. Indeed the class of cylinder-generated measurable sets depending on at most countably many coordinates is a sigma algebra containing every finite cylinder, hence the full product sigma algebra. Apply this to the countably many rational sublevel sets. A completed-product measurable function has an uncompleted measurable version equal almost surely. Alternatively, \(L^2\)-approximation by finite-cylinder functions gives countably many effective coordinates directly. The finite-range, degree-\(d\) representative may therefore be taken on a countable product without altering the almost-sure target or the \(L^2\) degree.

**Lemma 5.2 (almost-sure posterior degree).** Index the active coordinates by \(\mathbb N\). For every finite queried set \(A\), Fubini and a sequence of finite low-degree cylindrical approximants show that the section \(f(x_A,\cdot)\) retains Hoeffding degree at most \(d\) for \(\mu_A\)-almost all \(x_A\). To justify the passage, choose an \(L^2\)-convergent subsequence whose squared errors are summable; Fubini then gives \(L^2\)-convergence on almost every section. Each finite approximant's section is a sum of terms involving at most \(d\) unqueried coordinates, and the degree-\(d\) subspace is \(L^2\)-closed. The same reasoning works after any finite adaptive transcript because there are only countably many finite coordinate index sequences; it does not require a regular conditional distribution on an arbitrary standard-Borel alphabet.

**Lemma 5.3 (measurable maximal-component choice).** For each finite observed \(A\) and finite residual \(S\), the squared \(L^2\)-norm of the conditional Hoeffding component indexed by \(S\) is a measurable function of the observed values. Conditional expectation and Fubini give a measurable representative. There are countably many \(S\) and only finitely many possible orders \(0,\ldots,d\). Choose the greatest order with positive conditional energy and, within that order, the first finite \(S\) in a fixed enumeration. The resulting coordinate-index decision is measurable. If all nonconstant component energies vanish, the posterior output is almost surely constant and the protocol stops.

**Lemma 5.4 (positive-measure changing blocks).** For a nonzero maximal-order component \(g_S\), \(D_Sg=g_S\) holds in \(L^2\), and this right-hand side is nonzero independent of the complementary inputs. For almost every complementary input the \(S\)-section is therefore nonconstant almost surely under \(\mu_S\). For almost every original \(S\)-symbol the set of alternative \(S\)-symbols changing the function has **positive** \(\mu_S\)-measure. Mere pointwise existence on a null set is insufficient: on \([0,1]^2\), the indicator of the diagonal is zero in \(L^2\) yet selected null-point replacements can fabricate a degree-two hybrid. All replacement witnesses here are positive measure, never adversarial null-point substitutions.

**Lemma 5.5 (simultaneous good hybrids).** Fix finitely many distinct, disjoint index blocks and a finite queried prefix. Draw independently one replacement symbol for each block from its original product marginal. For each Boolean mask, the hybrid input again has law \(\mu\). Approximate \(f\) in \(L^2\) by finite degree-\(d\) cylinders; each cylinder becomes a Boolean polynomial of degree at most \(d\) under the disjoint-switch map. Passing to the \(L^2\) limit shows that all Boolean derivatives of order greater than \(d\) vanish almost surely in the joint base/replacement law. There are finitely many masks and derivatives for a fixed block tuple; intersect their full-measure events. There are countably many finite block tuples and finite coordinate-query histories, so a **single** full-measure event handles every sequence the adaptive policy could select. No uncountable intersection over observed values is taken.

**Completion of proof.** On this common full-measure event, each selected nonconstant top-order block has a positive-probability replacement set. Finite products of these positive conditional probabilities remain positive because the blocks are disjoint. The simultaneously valid degree-\(d\) hybrid set has conditional probability one, so it meets the changing-witness product set. At any proposed history exceeding the bound, choose one replacement from that intersection to obtain an impossible switch cube contradicting (2.2)–(2.3). Therefore the measurable policy stops within (A1) observations and outputs the correct palette value almost surely. All claims concern almost-sure representatives, not arbitrary null inputs. \(\square\)

## 6. Proof status, numerical controls, and limits

- The finite switching source and selected positive-atom/section helpers have **13 scoped Lean declarations** and **952 finite exact ANOVA checks** reported in the repository receipt. Their hypotheses are strictly narrower than Theorem A1.
- Earlier v0.2 exact checking reported **6,690 finite instances** and a proposed \(R/\Delta\) coefficient. The present version does not present that coefficient as independently established; the conservative \(2R/\Delta\) statement is the v1.1 measure-theoretic proof route.
- A reviewer must independently audit completed-product representative selection, \(L^2\)-section subsequences, positive-measure replacement intersections, and the transfer of finite-degree polynomials through disjoint switches. Numerical replay cannot prove these statements.
- There is no general infinite-palette theorem, pointwise null-input claim, cost bound on selecting coordinates, general product dependence, or globally certified priority claim.

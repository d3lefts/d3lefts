# Research statement — Search / Knowledge Graphs

My experience spans leading product and technical programs at Walmart Global Tech, an early-stage marketplace startup, Amazon Prime Video/Amazon Kids, and the U.S. Army. At Walmart I worked across emerging commerce integrations and executive-funded innovation programs; earlier I helped launch a consumer marketplace and coordinated content/product decisions. I also coauthored a West Point systems-engineering conference paper in 2015. Since August 2026, I have explored mathematical verification through the self-directed NIGHTFALL project with ChatGPT and Claude. The AI systems generate substantial parts of its code and proofs; my role is selecting questions, directing work and making publication decisions. The central question of that exploration is how to keep source identity, assumptions and uncertainty attached to technical claims.

One AI-assisted NIGHTFALL implementation is **SourceBound**, a source-grounded assertion-admission system. It separates extraction from acceptance and tracks candidate subject–relation–object assertions alongside source revisions, evidence spans, DOM locations, identity constraints and temporal qualifiers. Its bug-fixed public release includes 27 unit tests and a 14,000-page synthetic fixture replay with 6,000 retained fixture assertions. Those tests demonstrate bounded source-consistency behavior; they do not demonstrate independent natural-web extraction accuracy or production scale.

A second, AI-assisted research line, **Proof After Scarcity v2**, models how bibliographic links differ from logical dependencies in evidence graphs. It establishes elementary finite-DAG identities and specifies a source-bound evaluation protocol; no prospective reviewer-time or accuracy improvement has been demonstrated. The historical retrospective exercise remains available as a separate audit record, not the basis of v2's technical claims.

The project also includes a small AI-assisted runnable retrieval interface over six canonical, manually curated claims. It returns verbatim scope statements, primary citations, typed evidence edges and explicit abstentions when no matching record exists. The code and negative-control tests are public. The demonstration is a transparent systems component, not a surrogate benchmark for agentic search.

The Foundations Search knowledge-graphs role is particularly interesting because it joins three problems I have been working toward: extracting structure from inconsistent sources, choosing representations that models can use, and evaluating whether those representations improve grounded reasoning. The next experiment I would prioritize is a frozen, domain-held-out SourceBound evaluation at matched recall, followed by downstream question-answering/retrieval comparisons with latency, abstention and cost reported. I would rather preserve the scope of uncertain evidence than claim a retrieval improvement without a matched, held-out experiment.

# Progress-statistic test bench for strong simulation (BDST Conjecture 2 track)

Date: 2026-11-19. Status: **computational experiment only.** No theorem is claimed. Nothing here is
reviewed, formalized, or novel beyond the bench itself.

## Question

The current paper's strong-simulation section shows two things:

- One-step potentials fail: AND_k is a martingale obstruction and parity has zero one-step mutual information.
- Influence-greedy selection wastes about 2^{k/2}/ε queries on `3ε·AND_k(y) + ½·avg(x)`.

Before more proof work, two tests were planned (see the research plan of 2026-11-19):

1. Reproduce the AND and parity obstructions exactly.
2. Find a progress statistic that credits the correct query set and pays nothing for diffuse nuisance
   coordinates.

## Setup

The bench works on exact small instances.

- **Function:** f: {0,1}^n → [0,1], the acceptance probability.
- **Input law:** given as point weights. It can be uniform, a biased product, or an M-component mixture.
  Each rule reads the exact conditional (posterior) law, so mixture conditioning is handled exactly.
- **Failure:** a leaf outputs the best constant c. It fails on z when |f(z) − c| > ε.
- **Cost:** only queries are counted.
- **Stopping (Kraft rule):** stop if the conditional bad mass is ≤ δ/2, or if the absolute bad mass is
  ≤ (δ/2)·2^{−depth}. Because Σ_leaves 2^{−depth} ≤ 1, total failure stays ≤ δ.
- **OPT:** the exact minimum worst-case depth with total failure ≤ δ/2, computed by dynamic programming
  over restrictions. The factor 2 in δ does not matter for log(1/δ) questions.

Selection rules. Here z' resamples coordinate i from its exact conditional law, and B is the bad set of
the current best leaf.

| rule | statistic |
|---|---|
| var | Var(E[f \| z_i]): one-step variance gain |
| inf | E\|f(z) − f(z')\|: resampling influence |
| thr | Pr[\|f(z) − f(z')\| > ε]: thresholded influence |
| bad | E[\|f(z) − f(z')\| ; z ∈ B]: **bad-set influence** |
| esc | Pr[z ∈ B, z' ∉ B]: **escape probability** |
| red | one-step reduction of bad mass, with both leaves re-optimized |

## Results

Full output is in `out_obstructions.txt` and `out_falsifier_seed{0,1}.txt`. Reproduce with
`python3 progress_experiment.py` and `python3 falsifier_search.py SEED 120`.

**1. The obstructions reproduce.**

- `3ε·AND_k + 0.18·avg(x)` with ε = 0.1 and k + m = 12:
  - k = 5: var and inf use **12** queries; OPT uses **5**.
  - k = 6: var and inf use **12**; OPT uses **6**.
  - In both cases var and inf query every nuisance coordinate before the AND block.
- Parity on k bits plus noise: var uses 8–10 queries; OPT uses k.
- `red` (the one-step bad-mass potential) stalls at the root on AND and parity, then exceeds δ. This
  confirms that one-step potentials fail.

**2. Tail-sensitive statistics fix both obstructions.**

- thr, bad and esc all match OPT on rare-AND (k ≤ 6), parity, hidden-AND, and the two-component
  mixture-AND, where the nuisance bits reveal the latent component.
- **thr is ruled out.** On collective noise (`avg(x)`), every single flip is below ε, so thr sees
  nothing, stops, and fails.
- bad and esc pass all structured families.

**3. Random falsifier search finds no blow-up.**

The search covered n = 8, 240 functions (degree-3 polynomials and rare-event mixtures), laws that were
uniform, product or mixtures with M ≤ 3, ε ∈ {0.05, 0.1} and δ ∈ {0.05, 0.005, 0.0005}. Worst-case
depth minus OPT(δ/2):

| rule | mean gap (seed 0 / 1) | max |
|---|---|---|
| bad | 0.27 / 0.25 | 5 / 4 |
| esc | 0.30 / 0.31 | 5 / 4 |
| inf | 0.41 / 0.32 | 5 / 6 |
| var | 0.58 / 0.58 | 5 / 5 |

On random instances, the largest gaps are shared by every rule. That is greedy myopia, not a failure of
one statistic.

**4. Weakness found: split quality.** On a few instances, bad and esc lose up to 3 queries to plain
inf. Examples are s0t74, s1t10 and s0t36.

- Mechanism (traced on s0t74): bad-set influence credits a coordinate that moves f on B. It does not
  check that the query *partitions* B away from the good set.
  - Query 6 touches B but leaves most of the bad mass in place.
  - Query 5 removes most of it at once.
- At n = 8 this is a constant loss, not exponential. It still shows that the statistic alone is not a
  progress certificate.

## Interpretation for the proof programme

- The cheap test favors a **tail-sensitive, B-conditioned** statistic (bad or esc). Unconditioned
  influence or variance does not survive the rare-AND barrier.
- **Candidate inequality (untested beyond n = 8).** Suppose f has degree ≤ d and B has mass β under a
  product (or posterior-mixture) law. Then some coordinate should have bad-set influence
  ≥ β · poly(ε, 1/d, 1/log(1/β)). This would be an AA-type inequality *conditioned on the bad set*.
  Collective noise shows the 1/log(1/β) factor is needed.
- **Missing piece:** a potential that turns the inequality into depth. Finding 4 shows that bad-set
  influence does not directly lower the bad mass.
  - A candidate is the tail-entropy potential Ψ = E[1_B · log(1/μ(B | leaf))].
  - On AND_k, each correct query lowers Ψ by about β per step, from β·k to 0. So depth is about
    log(1/β) rather than 1/β.
  - Not checked: parity and re-optimized leaves. In both, B moves when c changes.
- **Not tested:** BDST's own quantum query weights. The bench uses acceptance functions only. Whether the
  extra structure of query weights is *needed* is the open point. For general bounded degree-d
  polynomials, a log(1/δ) bound may be false. This is an untested hypothesis, and it is the next thing
  to check before any proof attempt.

## Next steps (in order)

1. Run a scaling test of bad and esc on the barrier family at large k and m. Exploit symmetry so that
   n ≫ 12 is feasible, and confirm the gap to inf grows like 2^{k/2} while bad stays at about k.
2. Implement Ψ and a hybrid rule: maximize esc, subject to the one-step drop in Ψ being ≥ 0. Rerun the
   falsifier, targeting the split-quality instances.
3. Search for a bounded degree-d polynomial whose OPT depth grows faster than poly(d, 1/ε, log 1/δ).
   - If one exists, any proof of Conjecture 2 must use query-weight structure.
   - If none turns up, try to prove the candidate inequality.

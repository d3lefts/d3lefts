## Elementary task-quotient obstruction (author derivation; not novelty)
Let W be a linear downstream score map, A and B two representation maps on a common source domain. For a linear subspace S, W A|_S = W B|_S iff (A-B)S is contained in ker W. Proof: subtract the linear evaluations for every x in S. If W is injective on the subspace containing the differences, equality forces equality of the maps on S. Without this, task equality is compatible with large cycle defects.

Example: R^3, W=(1,0,0). Let all pairwise maps fix e1 and act by arbitrary signed permutations of e2,e3. All are orthogonal and agree on training anchors spanning e1. With direct map I and chained maps A,B, WBA=W always; BA need not equal I. In particular A=diag(1,-1,-1), B=I, direct I, both downstream scores exactly equal but operator norm of BA-I is 2. A second score W'=(0,1,0) detects the defect. Even pointwise perfect transfer of one task does not establish geometric path independence; this is a logical nonimplication, not a counterexample to Stanford's reported measurements. It can coexist with exact common geometry on the observable quotient.

A sufficient positive condition: if each edge map sends every paired anchor a_i to b_i exactly, and the anchors span a common source subspace S, then any two paths preserving those anchors agree on S by linearity. Exact full-span anchors force path agreement on all of S, without needing orthogonality. No identifiability outside S. For approximate linear maps, if X has full row rank on S and ||(BA-C)X||_2 <= epsilon, then ||(BA-C)|_S||_2 <= epsilon/sigma_min(X|_S) by the right pseudoinverse, using an orthonormal basis of S. If a downstream score margin is m and the output score map is L-Lipschitz in infinity norm, a perturbation of norm strictly less than m/(2L) preserves the unique argmax (L>0). These are elementary linear algebra and stability bounds, not new priority or kernel-reviewed results.

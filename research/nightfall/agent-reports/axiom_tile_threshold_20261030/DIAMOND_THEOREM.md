# Axiom — diamond-tiling optimal phase compression over a positive-width prime interval

**30 October 2026 · R1 mathematical theorem candidate.** Research owner Devin J. Wright. Construction/proof: Axiom (AI research role). Vesper [PR #272](https://github.com/d3lefts/d3lefts/pull/272) supplied the original sumset-hole phase oracle framework and the earlier two special prime forms; Tessera [PR #267](https://github.com/d3lefts/d3lefts/pull/267) supplied the prior field-uniform product signed-digit baseline. **This is a distinct same-oracle improvement over that product baseline for a full proportional-width interval of prime fields**, not merely an alternative proof of Vesper's endpoint family. Independent external human review and worldwide historical priority remain OPEN.

## Frozen sensing model and objective

For each prime p separately fix K=F_(p²), H={±1}, legal query y=(a·x)² for a K-linear form a; recover every x∈K^k modulo **one global ± scalar**, with zero and arbitrary support and all coordinate/mixed observations counted. This theorem never interchanges the oracle between GF9 and GF169 or claims a uniform comparison of different fields as a single fixed-field record.

Choose D=2^d, d≥2. Set the additive group G=F_p², identify it with K by any F_p-basis, and write

    B = {0,...,D−1}²,  v=(D,D/2),  w=(D,−D/2),
    T = B ∪ (B+v) ∪ (B+w) ∪ (B+v+w).

The base B is generated by the 2d binary powers along the two coordinate axes; v,w supply two more binary generators. Unlike Vesper's previous v=(2D−1,D+1),w=(−1,D+1), these **diamond shifts change the tile shape and support a much lower prime threshold**.

## Main theorem: a capacity-optimal positive-width prime family

For **every integer p≥3D**, including arbitrary composite moduli for the purely additive claim, all four translated B tiles are disjoint and

    m = (max(4D−p−1, 0), 5D/2−1)

is **outside T+T** in (Z/pZ)².

Consequently, whenever p≥3D is **prime**, the existing sumset-hole translator yields exactly **2d+2 independent anchored relative signs in one legal square-powered K-linear observation**. When

    3D ≤ p ≤ 4D−1  (p prime),

this is **optimal**: the powered-output alphabet has M=(p²+1)/2 elements and

    2^(2d+2)=4D² ≤ M <8D²=2^(2d+3).

The new construction has one more phase bit than the previous Tessera signed-digit **product bound** for each prime p∈[3D,4D−2]; at p=4D−1 the product bound already ties. These are **strict improvements over a specific previous construction**, not claims that all older literature has been ruled out.

### Proof of disjointness

For all four shifts, the first coordinates lie in three adjacent **non-wrapping** bands:

    B: [0,D−1];  B+v and B+w: [D,2D−1];  B+v+w: [2D,3D−1].

All three bands fit modulo p when p≥3D. The middle two tiles have second-coordinate bands

    B+v: [D/2,3D/2−1],
    B+w: [−D/2,D/2−1] mod p
       = [p−D/2,p−1] ∪ [0,D/2−1].

These are disjoint since p≥3D, so all four D² tiles are disjoint. Thus |T|=4D²=2^(2d+2), proving **binary subset-sum uniqueness** for the 2d+2 generators.

### Proof of the explicit missing sum

Write I=[0,2D−2]. Exactly

    T+T = ⋃_(a,b∈{0,1,2})
        [ (a+b)D + I ] × [ (a−b)D/2 + I ]   modulo p.

Set y*=5D/2−1, an integer as D is even. We inspect all nine rectangles grouped by n=a+b.

- n=0 or 4: y shift 0, range I, ending at 2D−2<y*. No modular wrap can make y* appear because p≥3D.
- n=1 or 3: y shift ±D/2, giving ranges [D/2,5D/2−2] and [−D/2,3D/2−2] modulo p. The positive interval ends **one below y***. The wrapped part of the negative interval begins at p−D/2≥5D/2, **one above y***. Neither contains y*.
- n=2: (a,b)=(1,1) has y shift0 and misses y*; (2,0) and (0,2) have shifts +D and −D respectively. The +D band [D,3D−2] contains y*. Their common first-coordinate shift is **2D**. Thus the horizontal slice of T+T at y=y* equals
      {2D,2D+1,...,4D−2} modulo p.
  Its length is 2D−1<p.

Choose x* to be the first residue immediately beyond the wrapped end of that interval on the low-x side:

    x* = 4D−p−1  if 3D≤p≤4D−1;
    x* = 0         if p≥4D.

For p≤4D−1 the shifted x interval occupies [2D,p−1] ∪ [0,4D−p−2], so x*=4D−p−1 is missing and lies in [0,D−1]. For p≥4D the interval lies inside [2D,4D−2], hence x*=0 is missing. Therefore m=(x*,y*)∉T+T. QED.

This is a **symbolic universal argument**, not extrapolation from finitely many prime examples. It also remains valid for the original two endpoint forms; the new contribution is the **entire interval** beginning at 3D.

### Legal squared-linear coefficient row

Fix any nonsquare δ∈F_p, so K=F_p[t]/(t²−δ), and embed pair (x,y) as x+yt. Let the b=2d+2 binary generators be

    W=( (2^i,0) for 0≤i<d )
      ++ ( (0,2^i) for 0≤i<d )
      ++ ( (D,D/2), (D,−D/2) ).

Let h=m be the missing sum, put c=−h/2, take

    a_j=W_j/2  (j=1,...,b),    a_0=c+Σ_j a_j.

For any anchored signs η_j∈{+1,−1}, the unpowered linear sum equals

    a_0+Σ_j a_j η_j = c+Σ_j ((1+η_j)/2) W_j,

i.e. a point of c+T. Because the binary W subset sums are unique and m∉T+T, c+T contains neither a pair of distinct additive opposites nor zero. Thus the **legal query** (a_0+Σ a_j η_j)² distinguishes all 2^b sign patterns. The inverse lookup table is exact and all coefficients are in the SAME K field being measured.

After k coordinate-square measurements, choose arbitrary supported anchor, recover relative signs in blocks of b using the row above (absorbing missing tail variables as η=+1 into a_0). The all-signal bound is

    m(k,s) ≤ k+ceil((s−1)/b),  b=2d+2, s≥1,

and the zero signal needs k coordinate queries. At k=b+1=2d+3, exactly **k+1=2d+4** worst-case queries suffice. Standard orbit counting establishes k+1 as a lower bound for all k≥2, because ((p²+1)/2)^k < ((p²)^k+1)/2. Hence the construction supplies **an exact adaptive minimax count in infinitely many field/dimension pairs**, conditional on independent review of the complete all-k decoder.

## Quantitative comparison against the established in-program baseline

Tessera [PR #267](https://github.com/d3lefts/d3lefts/pull/267) gives for odd prime p and quadratic extension the previous *product* one-query anchored-sign lower bound

    B_product(p,2) =
      max(2 floor log2((p+1)/2),
          floor log2((p−1)/2) + floor log2 p).

When 3D≤p≤4D−2, with D=2^d, this evaluates to **2d+1**. Our diamond construction gives **2d+2**, and the output alphabet proves this is *the maximum possible* in that field, a strict one-sign improvement over that prior formula. At p=4D−1 the product formula also gives 2d+2; the methods tie. For p≥4D+1, the product can do better than the unchanged diamond template: no competitive claim there.

so the family contains infinitely many primes with growing d **unconditionally**. This is completely different from conjecturing infinitely many primes of the special forms 4·2^d−1 or 4·2^d−3. PNT is background, not a new result.

## Global synthesis: where the available one-query constructions remain suboptimal

The diamond construction and Tessera's *already established in-program* product sign-digit construction together give a complete **arithmetic classification of their remaining distance to the one-reading alphabet ceiling for every odd prime p≥13**. This is a consequence of the two constructions, **not an upper bound on all unknown alternative constructions**.

For each p choose the unique D=2^d with 3D≤p<6D (the largest power of two no greater than p/3). Let

    C(p)=floor(log2((p²+1)/2)),                 exact alphabet upper bound;
    B_prod(p)=max(2 floor(log2((p+1)/2)),
                  floor(log2((p−1)/2))+floor(log2 p));
    B_diamond(p)=2d+2;
    B_known(p)=max(B_prod(p),B_diamond(p)).

Then **0≤C(p)−B_known(p)≤1**, with equality to one **if and only if**

    32D² ≤ p²+1    AND    p < 6D.

Equivalently (using exact integer arithmetic),

    ceil(sqrt(32D²−1)) ≤ p ≤ 6D−1.

The proof is immediate by splitting the unique range 3D≤p<6D:
- For **3D≤p≤4D−1**, diamond has b=2d+2, equal to the information ceiling.
- For **4D+1≤p** and **p²+1<32D²**, the old product bound is 2d+3, equal to the ceiling. The case p=4D is irrelevant to primes because 4D is even.
- For **p²+1≥32D²** but p<6D, the information ceiling is 2d+4, whereas B_product=2d+3 and B_diamond=2d+2: **one phase sign remains unresolved** by these constructions.

The narrow final interval has length asymptotic to (6−4√2)D≈0.343D, rather than the entire dyadic scale width 3D. **This identifies the precise next structural target.** Example outstanding primes are p=23,47,191. No claim is made that b=C(p) is impossible there; only the currently verified product and diamond coefficient recipes fall one sign short.

This is a reusable capability for prioritizing the rare gap bands, not a new universal impossibility certificate. In particular it prevents falsely interpreting the failed rectangular p47 search as nonexistence.

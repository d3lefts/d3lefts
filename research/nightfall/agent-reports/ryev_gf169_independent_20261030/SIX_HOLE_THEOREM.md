## Strengthened statement

Let D be **any integer >=8**, c∈{1,3}, and p=4D−c (an integer modulus, not necessarily prime). In G=(Z/pZ)² set I={0,...,2D−2} and

    v=(2D−1,D+1), w=(−1,D+1),
    R = ⋃_{a,b∈{0,1,2}} (a*v+b*w+(I×I))  (mod p).

Then |R|=p²−6. The **exact complement** consists of two 1×3 strips:

    Hcommon = {(2D−2, 2D−1), (2D−2,2D), (2D−2,2D+1)}.
    Hextra  = {(4D−4,2),(4D−4,3),(4D−4,4)} if c=1;
              {(4D−4,4),(4D−4,5),(4D−4,6)} if c=3.

This is stronger than simply finding m=(2D−2,2D+1) outside T+T, as in PR #272. It also explains why that PR's finite check for p>13 always yielded p²−6.

**Important model distinction:** the additive-rectangle theorem requires neither primality nor D a power of two. For its use as a distinct-sign binary-cube code in GF(p²), D must still be 2^d, the four tiles must be disjoint, and p must be prime. The theorem alone does not add new prime instances, increase the powered-query capacity beyond Vesper's construction, or imply infinitely many qualifying primes.

**Small-D falsifier:** for D=4,c=3,p=13 the complement has exactly **two** points (6,9),(12,4). The six-hole formula is *false* there. This distinction is kept explicitly, rather than silently extending the all-D theorem to D=4.

## Fully exact affine-boundary certificate

Use closed integer intervals I and their equivalent half-open real intervals [0,2D−1). The nine modular rectangles are translations by a*v+b*w (a,b=0,1,2). Each rectangle is a product of two cyclic one-dimensional intervals.

After lifting the torus to a fundamental domain [0,p)², every wrapped interval endpoint is an integer-affine function f(D)=αD+β. For each axis the complete candidate endpoint set is generated by, for a,b∈{0,1,2} and wrap k∈{-1,0,1,2}:

    x-start: a(2D−1)−b−k(4D−c)
    y-start: (a+b)(D+1)−k(4D−c)
    endpoint: start+(2D−1)

together with 0 and p. The four wrap indices cover every possible intersection of one of the nine intervals with [0,p) when D>=8: x-start is between −2 and 4D−2 and y-start between 0 and 4D+4; adding interval length 2D−1 stays within two multiples of p.

For *all pairs* f,g of these candidate affine boundary functions, the exact inequalities

    Δ = f(8)−g(8),
    s = slope(f)−slope(g),
    either Δ=s=0 or Δ*s >=0

hold. Hence f(D)−g(D)=Δ+s(D−8) never changes sign for any integer D>=8. In particular, order, coincidence, and clipping against 0 and p are stable throughout the infinite range.

There are exactly **9 active x-boundaries** and **11 active y-boundaries** (including 0,p), yielding 8×10=80 affine rectangular cells. Each membership relation to each of the nine generating rectangles is constant on a cell and remains constant as D varies, since *every* relevant endpoint was included and its ordering is fixed. Evaluate a single lattice representative for each cell at D=8. Exactly two cells remain uncovered; their symbolic endpoints are

    [2D−2,2D−1) × [2D−1,2D+2),
    [4D−4,4D−3) × [2,5)   (c=1),
    [4D−4,4D−3) × [4,7)   (c=3).

These cells consist of exactly six integer points. No other cell is uncovered for *any* D>=8.

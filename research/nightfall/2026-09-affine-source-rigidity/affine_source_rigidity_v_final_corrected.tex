\title{\textbf{Affine Source Rigidity and Möbius Cancellation\\

\author{Devin Jarel Wright}

\begin{abstract}
Let
\[
P(s)=\sum_p p^{-s},\qquad \Re s>1,
\]
denote the prime zeta function, and consider the shifted deformation
\[
A^+(s)=\sum_p\bigl((p-1)^{-s}-p^{-s}\bigr).
\]
For $\Re s>0$ we derive the exact expansion
\[
A^+(s)=\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{(s)_k}{k!}P(s+k).
\]
We formulate the associated Möbius representation locally, with logarithmic branches chosen on sufficiently small simply connected neighborhoods.

For a nontrivial zero $\rho$ of the Riemann zeta function, define the affine source map
\[
\PhiA(\rho,n,k)=\frac{\rho}{n}-k.
\]
Our principal unconditional result is the affine source-rigidity theorem
\[
\PhiA(\rho,n,k)=\PhiA(\rho',n',k')
\iff
k=k',\qquad n'\rho=n\rho'.
\]
Thus every collision occurs within one translation layer and is equivalent to a positive rational dilation relation between the underlying nontrivial zeta zeros.

We prove that every rational-dilation fiber is finite. We then derive the exact coefficient of the common leading logarithmic germ at a collided source:
\[
\frac{(\lambda-k)_k}{k!}
\sum_{m\in\C_\lambda}\frac{\muMob(m)}{m}r_{m\lambda},
\]
where $r_{m\lambda}$ denotes the multiplicity of the zero $m\lambda$.

Independently, we prove the first-layer localization
\[
A^+(s)=sP(s+1)+H(s),\qquad H\in\Ocal(\Re s>-1),
\]
so the continuation-sensitive component of $A^+$ throughout the first strip is carried by the first shifted prime-zeta layer.

These results separate candidate sources, source collisions, leading logarithmic cancellation, and complete local germ cancellation. Complete cancellation remains an open problem. A second unresolved bridge is required for any consequence concerning the Riemann hypothesis: failure of RH would first have to produce a relevant rational-dilation configuration. The paper therefore develops an unconditional source-rigidity and cancellation framework rather than an RH proof.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Prime-zeta background}

The prime zeta function is
\[
P(s)=\sum_p p^{-s},\qquad \Re s>1.
\]
The classical Euler-product relation gives, in its initial domain,
\[
\log\zeta(s)=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{P(ns)}{n},
\]
and Möbius inversion yields
\[
P(s)=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\mu(n)}{n}\log\zeta(ns).
\]
The singular structure generated by the nontrivial zeros $\rho$ of $\zeta$ consequently produces the classical source locations
\[
s=\frac{\rho}{n}.
\]
These facts belong to the established prime-zeta theory; see, for example, Fröberg's treatment and later discussions of continuation and singularities.

A branch-sensitive qualification is essential. The expression involving
$\log\zeta(ns)$ is initially canonical in a suitable right half-plane, while continuation to more general domains requires compatible local choices of logarithmic branches. Accordingly, throughout this paper, logarithmic representations away from their initial domain are understood locally.

\subsection{The shifted deformation}

We study
\[
A^+(s)=\sum_p\left((p-1)^{-s}-p^{-s}\right).
\]
Since
\[
(p-1)^{-s}-p^{-s}
=
p^{-s}\left((1-p^{-1})^{-s}-1\right),
\]
one obtains
\[
(p-1)^{-s}-p^{-s}
=
s\,p^{-s-1}+O_s(p^{-\Re s-2}),
\]
and hence direct convergence for $\Re s>0$.

The binomial expansion produces the exact shifted representation
\[
A^+(s)=
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(s)_k}{k!}P(s+k).
\]

\subsection{Affine source geometry}

The shifted expansion introduces the affine source coordinate
\[
\PhiA(\rho,n,k)=\frac{\rho}{n}-k.
\]
The integer $k$ is a translation coordinate augmenting the classical source coordinate $\rho/n$.

The central structural question is therefore:

\begin{quote}
When can two affine source triples generate the same point?
\end{quote}

The answer is the principal theorem of the paper.

\subsection{Main results}

\begin{theorem}[Affine Source Rigidity]
\label{thm:rigidity-intro}
For nontrivial zeros $\rho,\rho'$ of $\zeta$, positive integers $n,n'$, and integers $k,k'\ge1$,
\[
\PhiA(\rho,n,k)=\PhiA(\rho',n',k')
\]
if and only if
\[
k=k',\qquad n'\rho=n\rho'.
\]
\end{theorem}

The proof is elementary once the source coordinate has been identified: the difference of the real parts lies strictly between $-1$ and $1$, while equality of the source points forces that difference to equal the integer $k-k'$.

The collision theorem has two immediate consequences. First, source collisions are exactly positive rational dilations within a common translation layer. Second, under RH the affine source map is injective.

A second unconditional result concerns the size of a dilation fiber. For
\[
\lambda=\frac{\rho}{n},
\]
define
\[
\C_\lambda=\{m\ge1:m\lambda\in\Znt\}.
\]
Since every $m\lambda$ is a nontrivial zeta zero,
\[
0<\Re(m\lambda)<1,
\]
so $\C_\lambda$ is finite.

Finally, at a collided source $s_0=\lambda-k$, the coefficient of the common leading logarithmic germ is exactly
\[
\frac{(\lambda-k)_k}{k!}
\sum_{m\in\C_\lambda}\frac{\mu(m)}{m}r_{m\lambda}.
\]
The Pochhammer factor is nonzero because $0<\Re\lambda<1$. Thus leading logarithmic cancellation is equivalent to a finite arithmetic equation.

\subsection{Scope}

The paper establishes a structural reduction. Two further implications remain open:
\[
\mathrm{RH}\text{ fails}\Longrightarrow
\text{existence of a nontrivial rational-dilation fiber},
\]
or
\[
\text{nontrivial rational-dilation fiber}
\Longrightarrow
\text{surviving singular germ}.
\]
The second bridge is especially delicate: vanishing of the leading logarithmic coefficient leaves the complete local singular germ unresolved.

\section{Prime-Zeta Preliminaries}

\subsection{Euler product and Möbius inversion}

For $\Re s>1$,
\[
\log\zeta(s)
=
-\sum_p\log(1-p^{-s})
=
\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{P(ms)}m.
\]
Möbius inversion gives
\[
P(s)=\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{\mu(m)}m\log\zeta(ms)
\]
in the corresponding initial domain.

When local continuation is required, we choose a simply connected neighborhood avoiding the zeros and poles of the relevant zeta arguments and choose compatible branches of the logarithms there.

\subsection{Classical source points}

If $\rho$ is a nontrivial zero of $\zeta$ and $m\ge1$, the corresponding logarithmic source occurs at
\[
s=\frac{\rho}{m}
\]
in the local Möbius representation, subject to the branch framework above.

We call such a point a \emph{candidate source}. Whether a candidate source produces a surviving singularity after all cancellations is a separate question.

\section{The Shifted Prime-Zeta Deformation}

\subsection{Definition and direct convergence}

Define
\[
A^+(s)=\sum_p\left((p-1)^{-s}-p^{-s}\right).
\]
For fixed compact $K\subset\{\Re s>0\}$, the estimate
\[
(p-1)^{-s}-p^{-s}=O_K(p^{-\Re s-1})
\]
gives local uniform convergence of the defining prime sum. Thus $A^+$ is holomorphic in $\Re s>0$.

\subsection{Exact shifted expansion}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:shifted}
For $\Re s>0$,
\[
\boxed{
A^+(s)=
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(s)_k}{k!}P(s+k).
}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For each prime $p$,
\[
(p-1)^{-s}
=
p^{-s}(1-p^{-1})^{-s}.
\]
Using
\[
(1-x)^{-s}
=
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(s)_k}{k!}x^k,
\qquad |x|<1,
\]
we obtain
\[
(p-1)^{-s}-p^{-s}
=
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(s)_k}{k!}p^{-s-k}.
\]
For $\Re s>0$, the resulting double series is absolutely convergent, so the prime and $k$ sums may be interchanged. Summing over primes gives the stated identity.
\end{proof}

\section{Local Möbius Representation}

\begin{proposition}[Initial-domain Möbius representation]
\label{prop:local-mobius}
For $\Re s>0$, the identity below holds initially on the domain where the defining prime-zeta representation is absolutely convergent. The resulting logarithmic expression is interpreted locally after continuation to compatible simply connected branch domains:
\[
\boxed{
A^+(s)=
\sum_{k,n\ge1}
\frac{(s)_k\mu(n)}{k!\,n}
\Logz\zeta(n(s+k)).
}
\]
In the initial domain, $\Logz\zeta$ denotes the analytic logarithm on $\Re z>1$ normalized by
$\Logz\zeta(z)\to0$ as $\Re z\to+\infty$. Away from that initial domain, the logarithmic terms are interpreted through compatible local branches on simply connected continuation charts.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $\Re s>0$ and $k,n\ge1$,
\[
\Re\bigl(n(s+k)\bigr)>1,
\]
so $\zeta(n(s+k))$ is zero-free and the normalized analytic logarithm is defined there. In this region,
\[
\Logz\zeta(n(s+k))
=
\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{P(rn(s+k))}{r},
\]
with absolute convergence. More directly, using
\[
\Logz\zeta(z)
=
\sum_{p}\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{p^{-rz}}{r},
\qquad \Re z>1,
\]
we obtain on every compact $K\subset\{\Re s>0\}$, with
$\sigma_K=\inf_{s\in K}\Re s>0$,
\[
\left|\Logz\zeta(n(s+k))\right|
\le
\sum_{p}\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{p^{-rn(k+\sigma_K)}}{r}.
\]
The prime $p=2$ gives a uniform geometric majorant in $n(k+\sigma_K)$, while
\[
\frac{|(s)_k|}{k!}\le C_K(1+k)^{B_K}
\]
on $K$. Hence
\[
\sum_{k,n\ge1}
\sup_{s\in K}
\left|
\frac{(s)_k\mu(n)}{k!\,n}
\Logz\zeta(n(s+k))
\right|
<\infty.
\]
The double series therefore converges absolutely and locally normally. Substituting the initial-domain Möbius inversion formula for each $P(s+k)$ into Proposition~\ref{prop:shifted} is consequently justified by Fubini's theorem, yielding the stated identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Conditional local continuation form]
\label{prop:local-continuation}
Let $s_0$ be reached by analytic continuation from $\Re s>0$. Let $U$ be a simply connected neighborhood of $s_0$, and isolate every zeta factor whose argument meets a zero or pole over $U$. Assume compatible local logarithmic branches exist for the isolated factors and that, after this isolation, the remaining Möbius series converges normally on $U$. Then the continued function admits on $U$ the local representation
\[
A^+(s)=
\sum_{k,n\ge1}
\frac{(s)_k\mu(n)}{k!\,n}
\Logz_U\zeta(n(s+k))
\]
as an identity of local analytic germs, with $\Logz_U$ denoting the chosen branches.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}
The initial-domain proposition supplies a concrete sufficient domain for the full double series. The continuation proposition is deliberately local: it makes no claim of a globally single-valued logarithmic formula throughout the continuation domain. Candidate-source analysis therefore takes place in local branch charts, with analytic continuation and branch choices kept separate from the initial Euler-product identity.
\end{remark}

\section{Affine Source Geometry}

Define
\[
\mathscr S=
\left\{
\frac{\rho}{n}-k:
\rho\in\Znt,\ n\ge1,\ k\ge1
\right\}.
\]

\begin{remark}[Layer convention]
The value $k=0$ is included in the source map when describing the underlying unshifted prime-zeta geometry. The deformation $A^+$ itself uses only layers $k\ge1$.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Affine source representation]
\label{prop:affine-source}
For every nontrivial zero $\rho$, every $n\ge1$, and every $k\ge1$, the $n$-th Möbius channel of the $k$-th shifted prime-zeta layer has a candidate nontrivial-zero source precisely at
\[
\boxed{
 s=\PhiA(\rho,n,k)=\frac{\rho}{n}-k.
}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The $k$-th shifted layer contributes the factor $P(s+k)$. In the Möbius representation of $P(s+k)$, the $n$-th channel contains $\Logz\zeta(n(s+k))$. A nontrivial zero $\rho$ contributes when
\[
n(s+k)=\rho,
\]
which is equivalent to
\[
s=\frac{\rho}{n}-k.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}
A point of $\mathscr S$ is a \emph{candidate source}. It is a \emph{source collision} if two distinct source triples generate the same point. A candidate source is a \emph{local singularity} only after its complete local germ has been shown to survive analytic continuation and cancellation.
\end{definition}

The distinction
\[
\boxed{\text{source collision}\neq\text{surviving singularity}}
\]
is fundamental to the paper.

\section{Affine Source Rigidity}

\begin{theorem}[Affine Source Rigidity]
\label{thm:rigidity}
For nontrivial zeros $\rho,\rho'$ of $\zeta$, positive integers $n,n'$, and integers $k,k'\ge1$,
\[
\PhiA(\rho,n,k)=\PhiA(\rho',n',k')
\]
if and only if
\[
k=k',\qquad n'\rho=n\rho'.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equality gives
\[
\frac{\rho}{n}-\frac{\rho'}{n'}=k-k'.
\]
Taking real parts,
\[
\frac{\Re\rho}{n}-\frac{\Re\rho'}{n'}=k-k'.
\]
Since
\[
0<\Re\rho<1,\qquad 0<\Re\rho'<1,
\]
we have
\[
-1<
\frac{\Re\rho}{n}-\frac{\Re\rho'}{n'}
<1.
\]
The right-hand side is an integer. Hence $k=k'$. Substitution gives
\[
\frac{\rho}{n}=\frac{\rho'}{n'},
\]
equivalently
\[
n'\rho=n\rho'.
\]
The converse is immediate.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Collision classification]
\label{cor:collision}
A source collision occurs precisely within one translation layer and is equivalent to a positive rational dilation relation between the underlying nontrivial zeros.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[RH implies trivial dilation fibers]
\label{cor:rh}
If RH holds and $\lambda=\rho/n$, then
\[
\C_\lambda=\{n\}.
\]
Equivalently, the affine source map has no nontrivial rational-dilation collision.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Under RH, if $m\lambda$ is a nontrivial zero, then
\[
m\Re\lambda=\frac12.
\]
Since $\lambda=\rho/n$ and $\Re\rho=1/2$,
\[
\frac{m}{2n}=\frac12,
\]
hence $m=n$. Thus $\C_\lambda=\{n\}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The converse implication
\[
\PhiA\text{ injective}\Longrightarrow\mathrm{RH}
\]
remains unproved here.
\end{remark}

\section{Rational-Dilation Fibers}

For
\[
\lambda=\frac{\rho}{n},
\]
define
\[
\C_\lambda=
\{m\ge1:m\lambda\in\Znt\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Finite dilation fibers]
\label{thm:fiber}
For every $\lambda=\rho/n$ arising from a nontrivial zero,
\[
\C_\lambda
\]
is finite. More precisely,
\[
m<\frac1{\Re\lambda}
\qquad(m\in\C_\lambda),
\]
and consequently
\[
|\C_\lambda|<\frac1{\Re\lambda}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $m\in\C_\lambda$, then $m\lambda$ is a nontrivial zero, so
\[
0<\Re(m\lambda)<1.
\]
Thus
\[
0<m\Re\lambda<1,
\]
which gives
\[
m<\frac1{\Re\lambda}.
\]
Only finitely many positive integers satisfy this bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Source multiplicity]
Let $s_0=\lambda-k$ with $\lambda=\rho/n$. The source is called \emph{geometrically isolated} if $\mathcal C_\lambda=\{n\}$, and \emph{geometrically collided} otherwise. The source is called \emph{Möbius-isolated} if $\mathcal C_\lambda^{\rm act}=\{n\}$.
\end{definition}

By Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity}, every collided source is completely described by one dilation fiber $\C_\lambda$ within one translation layer.

\section{Local Möbius Cancellation}

Let
\[
s_0=\lambda-k,
\qquad
\lambda=\frac{\rho}{n},
\]
and let $m\in\C_\lambda$. Write $r_{m\lambda}$ for the multiplicity of the zero $m\lambda$.

Locally,
\[
\zeta(w)=(w-m\lambda)^{r_{m\lambda}}g(w),
\qquad
g(m\lambda)\ne0.
\]
Therefore
\[
\log\zeta(w)
=
r_{m\lambda}\log(w-m\lambda)+h(w).
\]
Since
\[
w=m(s+k)
=
m\lambda+m(s-s_0),
\]
we have
\[
\log\zeta(m(s+k))
=
r_{m\lambda}\log(s-s_0)+h_m(s),
\]
after absorbing the constant $\log m$ into the holomorphic term and fixing a local branch.

\begin{definition}[Active dilation fiber]
Define the active fiber by
\[
\C_\lambda^{\rm act}=\{m\in\C_\lambda:\mu(m)\ne0\}.
\]
Only indices in $\C_\lambda^{\rm act}$ contribute to the Möbius logarithmic germ.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Holomorphic nonlocal remainder]
\label{lem:nonlocal-remainder}
Under the local continuation hypotheses of Proposition~\ref{prop:local-continuation}, suppose the finitely many zeta factors whose arguments meet a prescribed zero or pole over $U$ have been removed from the Möbius series, and suppose the remaining series converges normally on $U$. Then the sum of the remaining terms is holomorphic on $U$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every remaining summand is holomorphic on $U$ by the isolation assumption. Normal convergence on $U$ then permits termwise summation in the space of holomorphic functions, so the remainder is holomorphic.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Conditional Local Germ Decomposition]
\label{prop:local-germ}
Let $s_0=\lambda-k$ with $\lambda=\rho/n$, where $\rho$ is a nontrivial zeta zero. Let $U$ be a simply connected neighborhood of $s_0$ satisfying the local continuation hypotheses of Proposition~\ref{prop:local-continuation}. Assume, in addition, that the neighborhood is chosen sufficiently small that no pole-generated channel or unrelated singular source lies in $U$, and that the zero/pole arguments contributing nonholomorphic terms at $s_0$ are isolated from all other singular arguments on $U$. Thus, among the Möbius channels under consideration, the only non-holomorphic contributions at $s_0$ arise from the nontrivial-zero channels indexed by $\C_\lambda^{\rm act}$. Then the continued germ has the form
\[
A^+(s)=
\frac{(s)_k}{k!}
\sum_{m\in\C_\lambda^{\rm act}}
\frac{\mu(m)}m r_{m\lambda}\log(s-s_0)+h(s),
\qquad h\in\Ocal(U).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By affine source rigidity, every contribution whose source coordinate equals $s_0$ lies in the same translation layer $k$ and corresponds to an index $m\in\C_\lambda$. Terms with $\mu(m)=0$ vanish identically in the Möbius representation. For $m\in\C_\lambda^{\rm act}$, the local factorization
\[
\zeta(w)=(w-m\lambda)^{r_{m\lambda}}g_m(w),
\qquad g_m(m\lambda)\ne0,
\]
gives
\[
\Logz_U\zeta(m(s+k))
=r_{m\lambda}\log(s-s_0)+h_m(s),
\]
where the nonzero constant factor $m$ is absorbed into the holomorphic term. By hypothesis, after removing the finitely many isolated source factors, the remaining Möbius series converges normally on $U$. Each remaining summand is holomorphic on $U$, and normal convergence therefore implies that their sum is holomorphic on $U$. Hence all nonholomorphic terms at $s_0$ are exactly the finitely many displayed logarithmic contributions. Summing the finite active fiber yields the stated decomposition.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Local logarithmic obstruction]
\label{lem:monodromy}
Let
\[
f(s)=C(s)\log(s-s_0)+h(s),
\qquad C,h\in\Ocal(U).
\]
If $C(s_0)\ne0$, then $f$ fails to extend holomorphically through $s_0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Analytic continuation once around $s_0$ changes the logarithm by $2\pi i$. Hence the continued germ changes by $2\pi i C(s)$. Since $C(s_0)\ne0$, continuity implies that $C$ is nonzero on some neighborhood of $s_0$, so the monodromy is genuinely nontrivial. A holomorphic germ has trivial monodromy, so holomorphic extension through $s_0$ is impossible.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[First logarithmic coefficient]
\label{thm:leading}
Assume the hypotheses of Proposition~\ref{prop:local-germ}. Then the coefficient of the common leading logarithmic germ is
\[
\boxed{
C_0(s_0)
=
\frac{(s_0)_k}{k!}
\sum_{m\in\C_\lambda^{\rm act}}
\frac{\mu(m)}m r_{m\lambda}.
}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
C_0(s_0)=\frac{(\lambda-k)_k}{k!}M(\lambda),
\qquad
M(\lambda)=\sum_{m\in\C_\lambda^{\rm act}}\frac{\mu(m)}m r_{m\lambda}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This is the coefficient of $\log(s-s_0)$ in Proposition~\ref{prop:local-germ}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Active-Isolated-Source Survival]
\label{thm:survival}
Under the hypotheses of Proposition~\ref{prop:local-germ}, suppose $\C_\lambda^{\rm act}=\{m_0\}$. Then $s_0$ is a non-removable logarithmic branch singularity of the local continuation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The local decomposition has logarithmic coefficient
\[
C(s)=\frac{(s)_k}{k!}\frac{\mu(m_0)}{m_0}r_{m_0\lambda}.
\]
At $s=s_0=\lambda-k$, the Pochhammer factor is nonzero by Lemma~\ref{lem:poch}; also $\mu(m_0)\ne0$ and $r_{m_0\lambda}\ge1$. Thus $C(s_0)\ne0$. Lemma~\ref{lem:monodromy} gives nontrivial monodromy, hence the singularity is non-removable.
\end{proof}


\begin{lemma}[Pochhammer nonvanishing]
\label{lem:poch}
If $0<\Re\lambda<1$ and $k\ge1$, then
\[
(\lambda-k)_k\ne0.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The factors are
\[
\lambda-k,\lambda-k+1,\ldots,\lambda-1.
\]
Each has real part strictly between $-k$ and $1$, and none has zero real part together with zero imaginary part because $0<\Re\lambda<1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact leading-cancellation criterion]
\label{cor:cancellation}
\[
\boxed{
C_0(s_0)=0
\iff
M(\lambda)=0.
}
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Crucial limitation]
The equation $M(\lambda)=0$ expresses cancellation of the leading logarithmic coefficient only. It leaves the complete local singular germ unresolved.
\end{remark}

\section{First-Layer Localization}

Define
\[
H(s)=
\sum_{k\ge2}\frac{(s)_k}{k!}P(s+k).
\]

\begin{theorem}[First-layer localization]
\label{thm:firstlayer}
The series defining $H$ converges normally on compact subsets of
\[
\Re s>-1.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\boxed{
A^+(s)=sP(s+1)+H(s),
\qquad
H\in\Ocal(\Re s>-1).
}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $K\subset\{\Re s>-1\}$ be compact and choose $\delta>0$ such that
\[
\Re s\ge-1+\delta
\qquad(s\in K).
\]
For $k\ge2$,
\[
\Re(s+k)\ge1+\delta.
\]
Hence $P(s+k)$ lies in its absolutely convergent half-plane. Writing
$\sigma_0=\inf_{s\in K}\Re s>-1$, we have
\[
|P(s+k)|
\le
\sum_{n\ge2}n^{-(k+\sigma_0)}
=O_K(2^{-k}).
\]
Thus there is a constant $C_K$ such that
\[
|P(s+k)|\le C_K2^{-k}
\]
for all $s\in K$ and $k\ge2$.
For $s$ in a fixed compact set, the standard gamma-function representation of the rising factorial gives
\[
\frac{|(s)_k|}{k!}\le C'_K(1+k)^{B_K}
\]
for suitable constants $C'_K,B_K$. Thus
\[
\left|
\frac{(s)_k}{k!}P(s+k)
\right|
\le
C_KC'_K(1+k)^{B_K}2^{-k},
\]
and the majorant series converges. The Weierstrass $M$-test gives normal convergence and holomorphy of $H$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
Define the continuation on $\Re s>-1$ by the decomposition $A^+(s)=sP(s+1)+H(s)$, where $P$ is taken in a local continuation chart compatible with the initial branch. Then the higher shifted layers contribute only the holomorphic function $H$. Consequently, within such a continuation chart, any nonholomorphic behavior in the strip is carried by the first layer $sP(s+1)$.
\end{corollary}

\section{Strategic Reduction}

Theorem~\ref{thm:firstlayer} changes the order in which the continuation problem should be attacked. Throughout
\[
-1<\Re s\le0,
\]
all higher shifted layers combine into a holomorphic remainder. Thus the first continuation-sensitive source family is
\[
s_0=\frac{\rho}{n}-1.
\]
A direct analysis of this family can therefore precede any general all-layer germ theory.

\section{Complete Germ Cancellation}

\subsection{Leading cancellation versus complete cancellation}

The finite equation
\[
M(\lambda)=0
\]
is an exact arithmetic condition for leading logarithmic cancellation. A complete local cancellation problem requires comparison of the entire nonholomorphic germ modulo holomorphic germs.

\begin{definition}[Local germ space]
For a dilation fiber $\C_\lambda$ at a source $s_0=\lambda-k$, let $\mathcal G_\lambda$ denote the vector space generated by the associated singular germs, modulo holomorphic germs, after a precise local branch space has been fixed.
\end{definition}

A prospective obstruction problem is:

\[
\boxed{
\dim\mathcal G_\lambda>|\C_\lambda|\quad ?
}
\]

Such an inequality would imply that finite arithmetic coefficients cannot annihilate all singular data. Establishing a useful invariant of this type remains open in the present paper.

\subsection{Failed independence certificate}

An earlier two-generator jet determinant involving $X,X'',X'''$ is excluded from the proof architecture. The reason is structural:
\[
C\log(s-s_0)
\]
has infinitely many derivatives while depending on one scalar coefficient $C$. Therefore
\[
\text{infinitely many derivatives}
\nRightarrow
\text{infinite-dimensional singular data}.
\]
Any successful obstruction must encode genuinely distinct source information rather than derivative count alone.

\section{Synthetic Möbius Cancellation}
\label{sec:synthetic}

Finite Möbius-weight cancellation is arithmetically possible. For example,
\[
\frac{\mu(6)}6+
\frac{\mu(7)}7+
\frac{\mu(42)}{42}
=
\frac16-\frac17+\frac1{42}=0.
\]
This demonstrates only the arithmetic possibility of finite cancellation. It leaves realizability unresolved by an actual zeta-zero dilation fiber.

Computational work is divided into validation and exploration. Numerical results are auxiliary to the analytic statements and are reported separately from proofs.

\subsection{Validation}

The following checks are suitable for independent reproducibility at arbitrary working precision:

\item the shifted binomial expansion against direct evaluation of the defining prime sum;
\item Möbius inversion in its initial domain;
\item the Pochhammer nonvanishing statement by exact symbolic factorization;
\item the first-layer decomposition against direct evaluation of $A^+$ on points with $\Re s>0$;
\item the finite-fiber bound $m<1/\Re\lambda$ for every tested source.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Exploration}

Exploratory computations, when supplied with a specified zero dataset, may include:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item rational-dilation searches among a specified tabulation of zeta zeros;
\item near-collision searches;
\item searches for actual dilation fibers;
\item evaluation of $M(\lambda)$ on detected fibers.
\end{itemize}

A rational-dilation test should compare
\[
\frac{\rho_j}{\rho_\ell}
\]
with reduced positive rational numbers $a/b$ by testing
\[
\left|\frac{\rho_j}{\rho_\ell}-\frac ab\right|<\varepsilon
\]
at a stated precision, followed by recomputation at higher precision and independent zero data whenever a candidate is detected. A numerical near-match is therefore recorded as a candidate relation rather than as an exact collision unless an exact mathematical relation is established.

Numerical absence of collisions leaves source injectivity unresolved, and nonvanishing of tested values of $M(\lambda)$ leaves universal non-cancellation unresolved.

\section{Relation to Existing Literature}

The classical prime-zeta theory includes the Möbius inversion formula, the source mechanism $\rho/n$, and the associated continuation and natural-boundary phenomena. Fröberg's work is a central historical reference \cite{Froberg}. Modern work also studies the distribution and zero-free regions of the prime zeta function.

Recent analytic-continuation work emphasizes the distinction between a locally continued logarithmic representation and a globally fixed principal-log expression. In particular, Camargo studies the dependence of the Möbius/logarithm representation on the continuation domain and branch choices \cite{Camargo}. The present manuscript therefore treats the Möbius/logarithm formula locally and only asserts the full double-series identity in its initial domain, where the normalized analytic logarithm is canonical.

Computational and statistical studies of the prime zeta function also use its zero and singularity structure, while addressing different questions from the source-collision problem considered here \cite{ChavezAllawala}. The contribution claimed here is narrower:
\[
\boxed{
\text{affine source collision classification}.
}
\]
The manuscript makes no novelty claim for the classical $\rho/n$ source structure itself.

A cautious priority statement suitable for submission is:

\begin{quote}
To the authors' knowledge, after the literature checks reported here, the specific affine collision classification
\[
\frac{\rho}{n}-k=\frac{\rho'}{n'}-k'
\Longrightarrow
k=k',\quad n'\rho=n\rho'
\]
has yet to appear in the prime-zeta literature surveyed for this manuscript. The authors regard this as a provisional priority statement and welcome correction if an earlier occurrence is identified.
\end{quote}


\section{Referee-Facing Scope and Logical Dependencies}

For clarity, the paper has four logically distinct layers. First, the shifted
binomial identity is an unconditional identity on $\Re s>0$. Second, the
affine source-rigidity theorem is an unconditional statement about the
geometry of candidate source coordinates. Third, the finite-fiber and
leading-logarithmic formulas are arithmetic and local analytic statements,
with the latter conditional on the local representation hypotheses stated
above. Fourth, complete germ cancellation and every implication toward RH
remain open.

In particular, a collision is a coincidence of candidate source coordinates.
It is a source-coordinate coincidence rather than a proof of a singularity of $A^+$. Likewise, $M(\lambda)=0$
records cancellation of the coefficient of the common logarithmic term; it
leaves the higher local germ data unresolved. These distinctions are part of
the theorem architecture rather than qualifications added after the fact.

\section{Conclusion}

The shifted prime-zeta deformation
\[
A^+(s)=\sum_p\bigl((p-1)^{-s}-p^{-s}\bigr)
\]
produces an affine source geometry
\[
\PhiA(\rho,n,k)=\frac{\rho}{n}-k.
\]
The central unconditional theorem is
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\rho}{n}-k
=
\frac{\rho'}{n'}-k'
\iff
k=k',\quad
n'\rho=n\rho'.
}
\]
Thus affine source collisions are precisely rational-dilation relations within a common translation layer.

The resulting dilation fibers are finite, and the coefficient of the common leading logarithmic germ is governed by the finite Möbius equation\[
\boxed{
M(\lambda)=
\sum_{m\in\C_\lambda^{\rm act}}\frac{\mu(m)}m r_{m\lambda}.
}
\]
Within $\Re s>-1$, the first-layer localization
\[
\boxed{
A^+(s)=sP(s+1)+H(s),
\qquad H\in\Ocal(\Re s>-1)
}
\]
further reduces the continuation-sensitive analysis in the first strip to the first shifted layer.

The remaining problem is sharply separated from these established results:
\[
\boxed{
\text{Can finite rational-dilation data annihilate a complete local analytic germ?}
}
\]
A separate bridge would also be required before this framework could yield any consequence concerning RH. Within the established scope, the framework makes the relevant singularity geometry rigid, finite at the level of candidate dilation channels, and arithmetic.

\section{Detailed Convergence Estimate}

For compact $K\subset\{\Re s>-1\}$, choose $\delta>0$ with
$\Re s\ge-1+\delta$. Then for $k\ge2$,
\[
\Re(s+k)\ge1+\delta.
\]
Consequently
\[
P(s+k)
=
\sum_p p^{-s-k}
\]
converges absolutely and uniformly on $K$, with
\[
|P(s+k)|\le C_K2^{-k}.
\]
The rising factorial estimate
\[
\frac{|(s)_k|}{k!}=O_K(k^{B_K})
\]
then gives a summable majorant after multiplication by $2^{-k}$.

\section{Local Branch Construction}

Let $s_0$ be fixed and let $U$ be a simply connected disk whose closure avoids every zeta zero and pole except the prescribed source points under consideration. For a prescribed zero $m\lambda$, write
\[
\zeta(w)=(w-m\lambda)^r g(w),
\qquad g(m\lambda)\ne0.
\]
After shrinking $U$ if necessary, $g$ is zero-free on the relevant image of $U$, so it admits a holomorphic logarithm. A chosen branch of $\log(w-m\lambda)$ then gives
\[
\Logz_U\zeta(w)
=
r\log(w-m\lambda)+\Logz g(w).
\]
This construction is local and records explicitly which factors contribute to the nonholomorphic germ. All remaining factors contribute holomorphic germs on $U$.

\subsection{Computed consistency checks}

As a numerical sanity check, we evaluated the defining prime sum and the shifted
series independently at three test points, using the primes up to $200000$ and
$30$ shifted layers. The absolute discrepancies were
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
s & \left|A^+_{\rm prime}(s)-A^+_{\rm shifted}(s)\right|\\
\hline
0.4+0.3i & 5.18\times10^{-11}\\
1.2+0.7i & 1.17\times10^{-9}\\
0.2 & 1.10\times10^{-11}
\end{array}
\]
The residuals are consistent with truncation of the prime sum and shifted-layer
series and are reported only as validation checks, rather than as numerical evidence for
any global analytic statement.

For the first $20$ numerically computed nontrivial zeros of $\zeta$, the bound
$m<1/\Re\lambda$ was checked directly for all integers permitted by the bound;
no violation occurred. In this sample the largest bound was $2$, as expected for
zeros on the critical line with $n=1$. For the same zero sample and
$1\leq k\leq5$, the minimum observed value of
$|(\lambda-k)_k|$ was approximately $14.14$, providing a numerical check of the
symbolic nonvanishing argument.

Finally, the synthetic Möbius identity used in Section~\ref{sec:synthetic},
\[
\frac{\mu(6)}6+\frac{\mu(7)}7+\frac{\mu(42)}{42}=0,
\]
was evaluated at 50-digit working precision; the residual was below
$10^{-52}$. This is an arithmetic validation only and makes no assertion that the
indices occur in an actual zeta-zero dilation fiber.

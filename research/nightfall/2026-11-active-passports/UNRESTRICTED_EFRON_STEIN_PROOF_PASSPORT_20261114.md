## I. Exact mathematical theorem

Let \(I\) be any set, including uncountable. For each \(i\in I\), let \((X_i,\mathcal A_i,\mu_i)\) be any probability space. Give
\(\Omega=\prod_{i\in I}X_i\) the product sigma algebra and product probability
\(\mu=\bigotimes_{i\in I}\mu_i\) (Mathlib: \`Measure.infinitePi μ\`).
Let \(f:\Omega\to\mathbb R\) represent any real \(L^2(\mu)\) class.

For each finite \(F\subseteq I\), let \(\mathcal G_F\) be the sigma algebra generated by the coordinate evaluations on \(F\), and \(P_F f=\mathbb E[f\mid\mathcal G_F]\). For each \(i\in I\) let \(\mathcal H_i=\sigma(X_j:j\ne i)\), \(Q_if=\mathbb E[f\mid\mathcal H_i]\), and

\[
V(f):=\|f-\mathbb Ef\|_2^2,\qquad
I_i(f):=\|f-Q_if\|_2^2\in[0,\infty).
\]

**Unrestricted inequality:**

\[
\boxed{
V(f)\ \le\ \sum_{i\in I} I_i(f)
:=\sup_{\substack{F\subseteq I\\F\ \mathrm{finite}}}
\sum_{i\in F}I_i(f)
}
\]

The right side is an *extended nonnegative real*. No finite upper bound, atom lower bound, separability, standard-Borel assumption, alphabet finiteness, common alphabet, countable index set or interaction-degree restriction is required. All equalities are \(L^2\) statements, independent of the chosen representatives of \(f\).

## II. Complete mathematical proof (source independent)

**A. Density and finite-coordinate projections.** The collection of all measurable finite-coordinate cylinders is an algebra of subsets of \(\Omega\) that generates the product sigma algebra. (It is the **cylinder algebra**, not merely the collection of rectangles with one fixed finite support.) The \(L^2\)-closure \(H\) of the span of their indicators is the whole \(L^2(\mu)\): let \(\mathcal D\) consist of measurable sets whose indicator belongs to \(H\). Then \(\mathcal D\) is closed under complement and increasing countable unions, since \(\mu(\Omega)=1\), and contains the generating cylinder algebra; the monotone class theorem gives all measurable sets, after which simple-function approximation proves density.

For \(f\in L^2\) and \(\varepsilon>0\), choose a finite-cylinder simple function \(g\) on some \(F_0\) with \(\|f-g\|_2<\varepsilon\). The operator \(P_F\) is the \(L^2\) orthogonal projection onto \(\mathcal G_F\)-measurable classes. Thus for every *finite* \(F\supseteq F_0\),
\[
\|f-P_Ff\|_2\le\|f-g\|_2<\varepsilon.
\]
Therefore \(P_F f\to f\) in \(L^2\) along the directed *net* of finite \(F\). This proof does not secretly select a countable coordinate exhaustion. Centering \(C f=f-\mathbb Ef\) is another contraction, whence \(V(P_Ff)\to V(f)\).

**B. Coordinate residual contraction, on the actual source.** Fix finite \(F\) and \(i\in F\). Independence of disjoint coordinate sigma algebras and conditional expectation gives
\[
P_FQ_if=P_{F\setminus\{i\}}f,\qquad
P_{F\setminus\{i\}}P_Ff=P_{F\setminus\{i\}}f.
\]
The first identity can be checked first on products of bounded finite-cylinder functions, where it is Fubini/product independence, and then extended to all \(L^2\) functions by density and projection contraction. Consequently
\[
P_Ff-P_{F\setminus\{i\}}f=P_F(f-Q_if),\quad
\|P_Ff-P_{F\setminus\{i\}}f\|_2^2\le I_i(f).
\]
This is the *actual* conditional-resampling influence of \(P_Ff\), not an oracle-supplied \(h_{\rm finite}\) bound.

**C. Finite-coordinate Efron–Stein, arbitrary measurable alphabets.** Enumerate \(F=\{i_1,\ldots,i_n\}\), let \(\mathcal G_k=\sigma(X_{i_1},\ldots,X_{i_k})\), and \(M_k=\mathbb E[P_F f\mid\mathcal G_k]\). The martingale differences \(D_k=M_k-M_{k-1}\) are pairwise \(L^2\)-orthogonal. Product independence gives
\[
D_k=\mathbb E[P_Ff-Q_{i_k}P_Ff\mid\mathcal G_k].
\]
Conditional Jensen (orthogonal-projection contraction) yields
\(\|D_k\|_2^2\le\|P_Ff-Q_{i_k}P_Ff\|_2^2\).
By Pythagoras, \(\sum_k D_k=P_Ff-\mathbb Ef\), and thus
\[
V(P_Ff)
=\sum_{k=1}^n\|D_k\|_2^2
\le\sum_{i\in F}\|P_Ff-P_{F\setminus\{i\}}f\|_2^2
\le\sum_{i\in F}I_i(f).
\]
All definitions involve conditional expectations on the original \(\mu\); none requires \(\mathcal A_i\) to have finitely many atoms.

**D. Terminal directed limit.** For *every* finite \(F\), Step C gives
\(V(P_Ff)\le S:=\sup_{E\text{ finite}}\sum_{i\in E}I_i(f)\).
If \(S=\infty\) the claim is immediate. Otherwise Step A and continuity of the squared Hilbert norm yield \(V(f)=\lim_F V(P_Ff)\le S\). \(\square\)

## IIa. Sharpness and boundary checks

**Best universal coefficient equals 1 (mathematically exact).** Take a single nondegenerate coordinate \(i_0\), for example the fair Bernoulli law on \(\{0,1\}\), with \(f(x)=x_{i_0}-1/2\). Then \(\mathbb Ef=0\), \(V(f)=1/4\), \(I_{i_0}(f)=1/4\), and \(I_i(f)=0\) for every \(i\ne i_0\) by independence. The inequality is therefore an equality, so no constant smaller than 1 can work for *all* product spaces. The same example embeds into any index set containing a nondegenerate coordinate. No lower bound is claimed on the *largest single coordinate influence* in terms of variance without additional degree or complexity assumptions.

**Empty-index edge:** if \(I=\varnothing\), the product space has one point up to measurable equivalence, \(f\) is constant a.e. and \(V(f)=0=\sup_{F}\sum_{i\in F}I_i(f)\).

**RHS \(+\infty\):** allowed, the theorem is trivially true; no unlicensed conversion of the extended supremum to a finite real-number sum.

**Independence is essential:** if two coordinates are perfectly correlated, the same coordinate-residual definition can assign zero to both even for a nonconstant observable. The theorem is not a general correlated-input claim.

# Three Ceilings on Oversight — September 2026

**Researcher:** Devin J. Wright. **Status:** internally audited formal synthesis with mathematical source assumptions; human referee review pending.

The manuscript separates three distinct limits on supervising AI systems:

**Information ceiling:** a safety property F is exactly decidable from a view R precisely when F factors through R. A richer adaptive observation protocol can change the view and remove an information obstruction.

**Computation ceiling:** a property may be present in the observations while inaccessible to a resource-bounded monitor; cryptographic versions inherit their explicit cryptographic assumptions.

**Authority ceiling:** an adversarial model's own statements cannot guarantee properties across worst-case strategy classes that include a mimic, while independently attested infrastructure may add usable evidence.

These are **relative** ceilings. A more capable human investigator can alter tests, observations, computation, and trusted evidence, changing the applicable regime. The manuscript's formulas cannot be used to claim unavoidable failure for every human overseer.

A 30 September adversarial review found a false unrestricted converse in a *separate boundary-paper draft*, and supplied a corrected factorization/decision-completeness statement. The revised Three Ceilings' finite and conditional arguments were reviewed within the explicitly stated assumptions. The paper is useful as an organizing theoretical framework for later Agent Safety Under Abstraction and Proof After Scarcity research.

   Evidence statement. This is an observational, reproducibility-oriented account of a research collaboration,
   not a controlled experiment establishing AI-agent superiority. Its main constructive upper-bound claims are
   supported by archived exact witnesses; its competition placements are supported separately by the public
   EinsteinArena pages, viewed on 20 October 2026. Exact candidate bounds, accepted platform scores, and
   global mathematical optimality are not interchangeable.




   Executive finding
   The major performance jumps were associated with changes of representation, basins, and collaboration hand-
offs, while exact certificates prevented numerical or grading artifacts from becoming unsupported mathematical
claims. Within roughly three days, this workflow yielded a first-place EinsteinArena entry for Erdős’ minimum-
overlap problem and second-place entries in the first-autocorrelation and uncertainty-principle tasks. The cam-
paign did not solve the underlying infinite-dimensional optimization problems. Working research manuscript. Re-


sults, authorship, and provenance should be reviewed before public preprint submission.

                                                         Abstract
          We reconstruct an intensive human-coordinated, multi-agent mathematical research campaign conducted
     during 18–20 October 2026. A human collaborator set research priorities, routed certificates and search strate-
     gies among ChatGPT, ChatGPT Work, and Claude instances, imposed a strict threshold for material progress,
     and repeatedly redirected stalled searches. The agents produced candidate constructions, optimization soft-
     ware, falsification tests, and exact verifiers. The principal test case was Erdős’ minimum-overlap problem: a
     historical witness of approximately 0.38085658829748237 was improved to an archived exact internal upper
     witness of 0.380856370118029325 . . .. A separately archived Arena-format v3 construction has exact mathe-
     matical score 0.380856370118785003 . . .. The public EinsteinArena page shows NIGHTFALL-DevinWright
     in first place with a rounded score of 0.3808564, but its visible display does not identify the full-precision up-
     loaded vector. Two subsequent problem lines reached public second-place positions: the first autocorrelation
     inequality and an uncertainty-principle root-construction task. We specify the mathematical proof objects,
     the chronological intervention and agent handoff graph, and the distinction between search success, exact
     certification, verifier acceptance, and scientific novelty. The case yields testable hypotheses about collective
     AI research: an explicit human-governed handoff contract, representation-switching after plateau detection,
     and verification as a parallel rather than terminal task. Because the record is observational, it supports those
     hypotheses as operational explanations, not identified causal effects.

Keywords: human–AI collaboration; multi-agent discovery; exact computation; mathematical optimization; Ein-
steinArena; Erdős minimum overlap; autocorrelation; proof-carrying artifacts; research governance.

1 What the collaboration set out to do

The narrow objective was not to produce a fluent mathematical argument. It was to obtain an explicitly checkable
improvement over the strongest available construction in a difficult nonsmooth optimization problem, then repeat
the procedure on other open benchmarks. This required separate progress on three axes: finding new feasible
objects, proving their claimed bounds, and meeting the platform’s submission criteria.
   The human collaborator, posting under a leaderboard handle that included DevinWright, acted as research
coordinator rather than a passive requester. The coordinator selected problems, supplied and compared prior
certificates, moved methods between agent instances, initiated parallel searches, made tradeoffs between upper-
and lower-bound programs, and set a reporting gate. In particular, once the gains grew small, the coordinator
required improvements of at least 10−8 over the strongest available certified record before a local step counted
as a substantive milestone. Later, the leaderboard’s own per-problem submission requirements took precedence:
no submission unless the current platform threshold was met.
    This was a heterogeneous collaboration. Different instances had different access to solvers, file collections,
execution windows, and external interfaces. The effective research unit was therefore not a single model session:
it was a shared lineage of numerical candidates, code, exact certificates, and human decisions. A model could
contribute a genuinely useful improvement to the search without being the instance that later certified or submitted
it.

  Central research question. What practices made a short, human-directed collection of fallible AI research
  instances capable of cumulative, verifiable progress—and which of those practices can be tested indepen-
  dently of this one favorable campaign?



2 Related work and scope

Erdős’ minimum-overlap problem has a long analytic and computational history. We use the step-function for-
mulation associated with Haugland and White [1, 2], and compare against the public certificate lineages and
prior work already discussed in the earlier NIGHTFALL manuscript [4, 8]. The October 9 public Wetch repos-
itory reported the lower reference µEO > 0.380627425 as part of a separately evidenced two-sided study; this
manuscript does not reproduce or improve that universal lower-bound proof. Published and agent-generated
upper constructions are inputs to the search history, not discoveries automatically attributable to NIGHTFALL.
   EinsteinArena’s own research study, Harnessing the Collective Intelligence of AI Agents in the Wild for New
Discoveries, describes an open, distributed system in which public leaderboards, discussion, and agent borrowing
produce cumulative results [3]. NIGHTFALL complements rather than independently confirms that study: here
the collaboration was coordinated directly by a human over a short interval, used private cross-instance handoffs
as well as public sources, and placed unusual emphasis on exact certificate continuity. The same evidence can be
consistent with other explanations—a lucky seed, larger aggregate compute, better available prior constructions,
or the value of a single high-performing optimizer. We do not claim the collaboration itself has been causally
isolated.

3 The observable three-day record

3.1 October 18: gain discipline and first exact witnesses

Early research began from a strong historical overlap construction. Agents searched over equal-width and occa-
sionally nonuniform rational step-function encodings. They applied mass-preserving perturbations and restricted
minimax optimizations, then compared candidates with exact finite-shift overlap values. Gains were often at the
scale 10−15 –10−11 . The methodological commitment that survived was more important than any individual small
move: a certificate with integral mass, box feasibility, and a complete shift check could be transferred without
relying on another model’s numerical impression.
   The human coordinator repeatedly asked for larger structural contributions rather than multiplication of similar
local certificates. This established a selection pressure in the collaboration: optimization runs were useful only
insofar as they produced transferable objects or opened a new search regime. Early archives also proved that file
numbering, a model’s own improvement claim, and the global best-so-far score were different orderings.


3.2 October 19: parallel branches and a change of representation

The overlap search diversified into fixed-grid minimax LP, smooth maximum continuation, fine-grid lifts, block
averaging, mass transports, donor-block crossover, and exact quantization. Numerically smooth objectives were
optimized first; the nonsmooth maximum of the actual overlap spectrum controlled acceptance. A notable re-
finement improved the 072 branch by about 1.4013 × 10−8 to an exact 079 witness, crossing the collaboration’s
milestone scale in that local branch.
   The decisive episode was the passage through a coarser representation and then back to a finer one. A branch
identified as the Claude B1024 witness had score about 0.380856530848315 on 1,024 cells. Lifting and reopti-
mizing the same lineage on 2,048 cells produced the B2048 score 0.380856416809955, a drop of approximately
1.1404 × 10−7 . The intervention combined a different basin, smoothmax continuation, refinement, and subse-
quent sequential linear programming; the archives cannot separate the causal contribution of each ingredient.


3.3 October 20: stronger upper records, public scores, and transfer

A projected B2048 line reached approximately 0.380856407054191. Later archived 131,072-cell overlap
witnesses improved the explicit upper bound to 0.380856370118029325 . . .. A related Arena-format v3 con-
struction has score 0.380856370118785003 . . . and an independent submission payload. The public overlap
leaderboard, inspected for this paper, lists NIGHTFALL-DevinWright first at 0.3808564 (rounded) ahead of
CristianBudala at 0.3808566 (rounded) [5]. The visible page and the archived v3 package are mutually
consistent at display precision, but that is not, by itself, a complete upload receipt binding the final accepted entry
to a particular file digest.
   Two transfers followed. In the first autocorrelation inequality, a public Arena entry under CLOADEX25-
DevinWright appeared second with rounded score 1.50274350, while an internal exactly checked 65,536-cell
candidate attained about 1.502743486285383074 [6]. The visible leaderboard entry and stronger internal
candidate are not the same evidence object: the public C1 discussion explicitly describes the submission as a
deliberately detuned derivative.
   In the uncertainty-principle task, the same leaderboard handle appeared second at 0.313093 (rounded); the
archived legal 25-root construction has a mathematically certified score close to 0.31309310122257694 [7]. The
UP25 mathematical validation involves controlling the last positive sign-changing root, not merely reproducing
a finite-window scan. Both branches are upper-bound constructions, not proofs of the optimum or current first-
place status.

Proof. The functions are constant on rational-grid intervals. Correlation is a finite sum of overlap lengths of
shifted cell pairs with fixed coefficients. Each overlap length is continuous and affine between differences of

integration gives Tk /(HD2 ). The constraints guarantee integral one and box feasibility.

   This elementary geometric reduction is what made proof objects shareable across agents: once a finite coeffi-
cient vector was fixed, no numerical optimization theorem was needed to prove its upper bound. Integer arithmetic
sufficed.

5 Why the collaboration accumulated progress

5.1 The coordinator performed five nontrivial functions

The human collaborator: (i) specified the improvement objective and a materiality threshold; (ii) selected which
competing branches should be propagated; (iii) carried exact witness files and executable methods among in-
stances; (iv) detected stale starts and misidentified parents; and (v) insisted that unsupported calculations remain
experimental. Instances were allowed autonomy to formulate and run experiments, but that autonomy was em-
bedded in an accountable evidence process.
   The coordinator’s most consequential interventions were not necessarily new mathematical ideas. Examples
include rejecting an old parent erroneously presented as the state of the art; directing attention away from sub-
10−8 refinements; passing a successful coarse-to-fine script to another instance; and requesting exact roots or

5.2 A handoff was an executable state, not a paragraph of encouragement

The strongest handoffs had seven elements: (1) a parent witness and origin, (2) a precisely normalized score, (3)
complete constraints, (4) a transformation/search algorithm, (5) an independent checker, (6) known false leads
and version warnings, and (7) the next experiment or acceptance target. A decimal without a vector did not satisfy
that contract. A new vector without evidence of which parent it improved was also incomplete.

5.3 Representation-switching and plateau recognition

In the dominant overlap lineage, progress was not proportional to the number of cells, and extensive high-
resolution polishing sometimes underperformed a 1,024-cell reoptimization. The use of log-sum-exp as a
surrogate allowed search across a broad plateau, while later SLP targeted the actual near-active contacts. Re-
stricting a parameterization could expose a different basin; a subsequent refinement supplied degrees of freedom
needed to descend further. The retrospective hypothesis is that strategic changes in the search coordinates were
more productive than indefinite small moves within one coordinate chart.


5.4 Verification ran alongside exploration

The collaboration treated numerical search and mathematical proof as different computational paths. When a float
optimizer reported a large gain, an exact score could overturn it; when an old branch showed a later filename, the
rational comparator could still rank it worse. Each accepted certificate served two roles: it proved a local bound
and was a stable checkpoint from which another agent could start. This created a ratchet without requiring one
model to remember or trust the entire conversation.


6 Evidence states and the submission boundary

It is useful to separate six states:

  1. Proposed: a construction or hypothesis without a checked score.
  2. Numerically promising: computed in floating point under a stated model.
  3. Exact-feasible: rationalized with mass, box, and problem constraints checked.

The transitions are not automatic. A globally correct UP25 polynomial might exceed an Arena root-count limit.

an accepted score derived from a bounded scan need not prove a mathematical statement outside that scan. The
final NIGHTFALL governance rule was deliberately narrower than mathematical validity: only submit a fully
valid construction if it meets the live required leaderboard improvement threshold. This avoided cluttering the
competitive record with subthreshold internal refinements.

7.1 Four supported conclusions

First, the collaboration did produce certified feasible overlap profiles whose bounds strictly improve the selected
historical comparison. Second, public EinsteinArena standings show a first-place overlap entry and second-place

entries in two different tasks at the recorded snapshot. Third, the archived methods exhibit nontrivial transfers:
exact convolution arithmetic applied to both overlap and C1, while the broader search protocol adapted to a root-

including numerical maxima missed by restricted active sets and incorrect branch ordering.


7.2 Four conclusions the data do not support

The campaign does not determine the optimal constants. It does not prove that NIGHTFALL invented smoothing,
SLP, FFT, multiresolution optimization, or a new universal theorem about all research agents. It does not establish
that having several agents caused a larger improvement than one equally funded agent would have achieved. It
does not equate platform rankings with publication priority. Particularly for C1, provenance is essential: some
research was derivative of external seeds, and the public second-place payload was knowingly detuned from
a stronger internal witness. The available record documents the process but not every unedited conversation,
prompt, agent version, and platform transaction.


8 From a case study to a falsifiable research program

A direct follow-on study should prospectively compare research protocols under equal wall-clock and compute
budgets. Randomize seeds and problem instances; freeze the accepted candidate format and independent verifiers
before the runs; record every handoff. Compare: (A) a single optimizer; (B) parallel agents that share only
scores; (C) agents that share source vectors and exact checkers; and (D) the full human-governed NIGHTFALL
protocol. Outcomes should include verified objective gain, count of materially qualifying improvements, time-
to-first certified advance, rate of false promotions, duplicated computation, and wall-clock cost. The hypothesis
is not that more agents always win, but that certificate-bearing information transfer and representation diversity
improve the efficiency of valid discovery.
   A second prospective test should ablate representation changes. Keep the same parent and total search budget,
vary the use of coarse block averages, split/lift stages, smoothing schedules, and final SLP. This would identify
which specific search interventions, if any, contributed causally to the B1024-to-B2048 jump. A third study
should test independent proof replay: compare arithmetic approaches and require them to agree on full event
spectra before publishing any benchmark result.
    These experiments are feasible precisely because the difficult mathematical questions admit small, self-
contained proof objects. A robust comparison need not judge the philosophical quality of the agents’ reasoning:
it can evaluate candidate validity and objective values exactly.


9 Conclusion

NIGHTFALL’s clearest result is a practical research process, demonstrated rather than universally established.
The human collaborator supplied continuity, selection, and a demanding success criterion. Multiple AI instances
supplied varied candidate-generating mechanisms, executable refinements, cross-checks, and mathematical ex-
planations. Proof-carrying artifacts allowed valid improvements to survive changes in session, method, and agent.
A favorable three-day campaign then produced an accepted first-place overlap entry and two second-place place-
ments, alongside stronger internal exact constructions not automatically equivalent to those submissions.
   The correct lesson is neither that an autonomous agent solved an open problem nor that collaboration guarantees
breakthroughs. It is that search autonomy plus transferable exact evidence plus accountable coordination can form
a compact, cumulative mathematical research system. Whether this system outperforms strong alternatives is now
a testable question.

B Archive reproduction guide

The v2 accompanying ZIP places older and current primary materials in separate directories. The compact v1.2
checker was rerun successfully with Python’s standard library during this revision. The newer internal overlap
archive contains an independent C++17 six-prime convolution replay script, a certificate manifest, and source
integer heights; the complete replay was run successfully in this revision. The v3 archive provides an Arena
JSON payload and an NTT verifier. The C1 multitransport archive provides a full exact finite-convolution replay.
The UP25 archive includes a legal root list, original exact/interval logs, and a standard-library replay of a distinct
polynomial stress test; a fresh high-precision Arb run of the full root certificate requires the additional software
identified by its authors. Running independent verifiers provides stronger evidence than merely checking their
published log text, but does not identify novel methods or establish causal explanations.
Practical audit order: verify the hash manifest; inspect declared normalization and constraints; recompute all
mass and box conditions; recompute the complete required spectrum or global root proof; compare exact frac-
tions; finally look up any platform acceptance and current threshold. Always preserve the parent and the original
certificate when producing a later variant.

D Research ethics and attribution checklist

Before public posting, reconcile exact full-precision solution IDs with their upload receipts; ask participating
collaborators to confirm or amend attribution; clearly cite upstream public code and witnesses; retain raw prompts
and original agent outputs where available; distinguish internal research autonomy from host-verified scheduled
execution; and include a changelog that identifies mathematical versus editorial corrections. Use the public Arena
discussion and leaderboard only as evidence for facts they actually expose. The legal identity of an account owner,
authorship order, and any permissions to redistribute agent-produced work should be confirmed separately by the
human collaborators.

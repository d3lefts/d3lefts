                                                  Abstract
         The first paper established a sharp distance formula for codes built from translated observations
     modulo sign. Here we extend the mechanism to every nonidentity involutive automorphism S of a
     finite abelian group A. Observing the two-point orbit of x + t produces a nonlinear code whose
     exact distance is determined by the occupancy of the finite image (S − I)A. More strongly, we
     determine the complete pairwise distance enumerator, give an O(m2 ) reconstruction algorithm
     independent of |A| in its candidate count, and characterize every correctable erasure pattern:
     two surviving views with distinct symmetry labels always determine the original state, while a
     single surviving label can suffice only at a specified fixed-state class. This yields an exact failure
     event even under correlated erasures, an explicit probability under conditionally independent
     erasures, and a theorem showing that balanced view placement simultaneously maximizes worst-case
     Hamming distance and minimizes both mean and worst-case source-conditioned erasure ambiguity
     under an independent exchangeable model. For heterogeneous per-view disruption costs, the design
     objective becomes an exact multiprocessor load-balancing problem, with a simple constructive bound.
     Finally, exact reconstruction is composed with fresh-state causal cuts under explicit assumptions,
     giving an end-to-end semantic continuity guarantee and a model-scoped fault-transfer bound. The
     cryptographic extension reconstructs elliptic points from translated coordinates and combines
     protected subgroup residues with a residual discrete logarithm. An independent small-curve verifier
     confirms all 96 private-scalar cases under stated assumptions. The existing verifier checks 918 finite
     configurations, 474,794 deterministic erasure-pattern cases, 5,052 mixed-fault decoding trials, 1,293
     exact independent-erasure probability cases, 2,046 correlated-law checks, 150 allocation optima, and
     272 greedy-weighted-bound checks.


Status and provenance. The statements are proved here and supported by finite checks. They
extend the project-specific translated-sign formula from What Survives the Cut [6]. Classical orbit
codes, coding bounds, automata minimization, scheduling, and causal abstraction are prior art;
external priority for these specialized formulas has not been independently established. The added
cryptographic algorithm is an exact point-recovery and conditional scalar-recovery composition, not a
general cryptanalytic speedup; no universal containment claim is made.

1    A symmetry can conceal a state without destroying it
Suppose a measurement returns an equivalence class rather than a state. The basic example is
[x] = {x, −x}. Previous work showed that controlled translations can make multiple such observations
injective and error-correcting [7, 6]. In parallel, directed causal cuts model systems in which the old
runtime is discarded and only an explicitly specified future-sufficient interface reaches a newly initialized
phase [8]. The constructive problem is to encode that interface in views that remain decodable under
failures, without smuggling unmodeled old state across the boundary.
    A sign flip is only one involution. A coordinate swap (a, b) 7→ (b, a), reflection of a selected
coordinate, and many finite-module automorphisms also satisfy S 2 = I. The algebra of translated
quotient observations is essentially identical across all of them, and leads to stronger conclusions than
minimum distance alone.

7    Across an irreversible cut
The code is useful at a causal boundary only when it carries the state actually needed by the successor.
Let P be the predecessor’s internal state space and R : P → A its future-sufficient extraction. Let
D : A → P ′ initialize the successor from scratch. A valid fresh cut replaces a predecessor state p
by D(R(p)) and gives the new phase no other route to p’s memory, credentials, mutable storage, or
implicit controller state [8].

Theorem 7.1 (Exact reconstruction preserves the causal interface). At each of a finite sequence of cuts,
encode a = R(p) using CT , transmit orbit symbols, decode with the bounded-distance algorithm, and
either initialize the successor from D(â) or stop when verification fails. Suppose the actual symbol errors
eh and erasures sh at every cut obey 2eh + sh < dh , and there are no other predecessor-to-successor
state channels. Then the resulting successor states and every subsequent deterministic evolution are
identical to those of an ideal fault-free cut system. The predecessor’s discarded degrees of freedom
remain causally inaccessible.

Proof. At each boundary, Theorem 4.1 gives â = R(p). Therefore the newly initialized state D(â)
equals D(R(p)), the ideal successor state. It has no other input from the predecessor by the fresh-cut
assumption. Apply this equality inductively at each boundary. Exact equality proves preservation of
downstream deterministic behavior; the absence of any other predecessor channel proves the causal
separation.

    For probabilistic cut failure, define ph as a valid upper bound on history-conditional semantic
transfer failure at cut h, including the separately modeled validation and decoder failure modes. A
union bound gives P(any error in the first H cuts) ≤ H

reconstructs the full point. The distinction matters for claims about real cryptographic security. Prior
work on horizontal-to-vertical coordinate recovery includes Okeya and Sakurai [3].

Access condition. The valuation theorem requires a valid Tate uniformization representation;
obtaining such a representation from an ordinary public elliptic-curve point over a cryptographic prime
field is not supplied by this theorem. Likewise, merely adding public translated x-views to a public
point does not create new homomorphic leakage. These distinctions prevent circular or misleading
claims of faster general cryptanalysis.

9.3   Fault-tolerant composition of the complete recovery procedure
The preceding constructions can be combined rather than placed side by side.
Theorem 9.4 (Certified recovery across damaged cryptographic observations). Let Q = [k]G be an
authenticated point of a cyclic elliptic subgroup of order N . Suppose (i) its translated horizontal-
coordinate code has distance dE ; (ii) each available homomorphic leakage value hi = fi (Q) is encoded
using a translated-involution shadow code on Hi of distance δi ; (iii) every code is decoded with its
stated distance guarantee; (iv) cyclic image logs of orders di and a residual DLP of order N/L can be
solved. If the received point word has (eE , sE ) faults with 2eE + sE < dE and each leakage word has
(ei , si ) faults with 2ei + si < δi , then the procedure below returns exactly k mod N :
  1. Decode the translated horizontal views and reconstruct Q; check Q ∈ ⟨G⟩ and authenticate its
     binding to the intended key identity.
  2. Decode each transmitted quotient observation hi and verify hi = fi (Q) wherever independently
     computable.
  3. Compute ki = logfi (G) hi (mod di ) in each image, reconcile the residues by generalized CRT to
     obtain k0 (mod L).

Remark 9.5 (Secrecy, fault tolerance, and authentication are distinct). These translated codes are
designed for recovery, not secrecy. Publishing sufficiently many views can remove quotient ambiguity;
homomorphic observations may leak scalar residues. An attacker allowed to choose all transmitted
observations, substitute an entirely different valid codeword, or replace the authenticated public-key
identity is not covered by a bounded-distance guarantee. A deployment therefore needs authenticated
channels, explicit authorization to recover a key, and a reason the recipient should possess the leakage
data. For prime-order cryptographic groups, all proper homomorphic-image reductions may be trivial;
nothing here breaks standard ECDLP.

Conclusion
The productive common thread is neither maximal preservation nor indiscriminate compression. It
is the constructive design of the right distinctions: controlled transformations recover hidden state,
redundancy keeps that recovery possible after damage, and fresh initialization prevents irrelevant
past state from propagating. For any nontrivial finite abelian involution, the algebraic collision
labels determine exact distance, pairwise spectrum, deterministic erasure correction, probabilistic
erasure failure, and optimal equal-reliability placement. These are operational design quantities, not
metaphors. The causal-cut theorem then states precisely which of those reconstructed distinctions may
survive into the future. In the cryptographic specialization, translated views recover a point, protected
homomorphic observations constrain a private scalar, and a residual subgroup logarithm completes
recovery under explicitly stated access, verification and fault assumptions. These are complementary
operations; none implies a faster general discrete-logarithm algorithm.

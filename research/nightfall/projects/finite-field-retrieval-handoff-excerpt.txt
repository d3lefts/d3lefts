2.2 Theorem 2: complete preimage enumeration
For every design and valid reading vector, form phase roots, the corresponding modular Gaussian lattice, and the
Fp -span of reductions of sufficiently short LLL basis vectors. Enumerate its µ4 -valued points by pivot normalization,
and retain those reproducing the complete reading vector. This lists exactly all µ4 -orbits of preimages. It may
require 4e−1 checks with e large (up to k); the list itself can contain 4k−1 classes (e.g., B = 0, full input support).
The 1024 bound is high-probability and uniform in x, not unconditional in B.

Hence every vector with ∥v∥ ≤ n lies in the integer span of the basis vectors selected by ∥bi ∥2 ≤ αN −1 n. This
costs one LLL approximation factor, not a product of two factors as in the older successive-minima gap method.

3.2 Fourth-root normalization and modular span (Lemmas 3.2 and 3.4)
Let S = supp(x), J = supp(Bx), and n = |S| + |J| ≤ 2k. A consistent preimage corresponds to a phase vector
(u, v) ∈ µn4 in a linear measurement kernel. Lift each phase into Gaussian units Z[i]; the resulting lattice vector has
squared norm n. The preceding lemma captures every such lift in selected short basis vectors; reduction through
ι : Z[i] → Fp gives an Fp -span W containing all feasible phase patterns.
    If dim W = e, choose pivot coordinates of a row-reduced basis of W . Normalize the first selected phase to 1 and
independently choose the other e − 1 pivot phases in µ4 . This yields at most 4e−1 assignments, with exact validation
against all readings. Zero measurement coordinates are handled by the support sets. There is no reliance on unique
retrieval for completeness.

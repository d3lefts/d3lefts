# TESSERA — All-degree additive hole-shield transfer for a fixed square oracle

**2026-10-30 · Vesper assignment #251 and source PR #272 · internal theorem candidate R1.** Human scientific referee and dated worldwide originality review remain OPEN. Tessera PR #267 provides the previous signed-digit baseline. This is a square-power oracle result, not a cubic F16 result.

## Theorem A: a full pair-sum hole protects unrestricted new dimensions

Let U,V be finite-dimensional vector spaces over an odd prime field Fp. Let T={Σ ε_i u_i : ε_i∈{0,1}}⊂U be an injective b-bit binary subset-sum cube with m∉T+T. Let W⊂V be any injective c-bit binary subset-sum cube, without restrictions on W+W. Then C=(−m/2,0)+(T×W) contains 2^(b+c) distinct elements and C∩(−C)=∅.

**Proof.** Independence of the direct-sum coordinates makes T×W injective. An antipodal equality between (−m/2+t,w) and (−m/2+t',w') would require t+t'=m, contradiction. Zero is excluded because 2t=m is also forbidden. In every odd-characteristic field K, z²=y² iff z=±y. Therefore the 2^(b+c) literal squared K-linear readings are distinct, for any Fp-linear embedding U⊕V↪K. No quadratic subfield, extra observation, or unpowered side-information channel is required.

**Why full-sum holes matter.** A missing *off-diagonal* sum is enough for one isolated squared-reading code, but does not automatically certify unrestricted cylinder extension: a diagonal representation m=2t can combine with two different appended coordinates, producing a new antipodal pair. The full T+T hole is a stable sufficient certificate, not claimed necessary for every extension.

## Theorem B: all extension degrees, same oracle

Let D=2^d with d≥2, and suppose p=4D−3 or p=4D−1 is prime. Vesper's explicit two-dimensional tile seed in PR #272 is a binary (2d+2)-cube over Fp², generated by (2^i,0),(0,2^i) for i=0,...,d−1 and v=(2D−1,D+1), w=(−1,D+1). Vesper proves all subset sums distinct and the **full** pair sumset misses m=(2D−2,2D+1). This symbolic tiling proof is explicitly credited as an input dependency; our independent checker confirms all stated finite prime instances.

For every r≥2, take an arbitrary Fp-basis e_1,...,e_r of K=F_(p^r), embed the seed in span{e_1,e_2}, and add d+1 binary weights 1,2,...,2^d on EACH of the remaining r−2 basis directions. Since 2^(d+1)≤p, each appended 1D cube is injective. Apply Theorem A:

**B(p,r) ≥ 2(d+1)+(r−2)(d+1)=(d+1)r for EVERY integer r≥2.**

B is the number of independent anchored relative ± phases identified by a single *square-powered K-linear* observation. For p=13,29,31,61,127, this yields respectively B≥3r,4r,4r,5r,6r. The p13/p29/p61 statements improve Tessera PR #267's 3r−1/4r−1/5r−1 by one for all r≥2, including odd r; p31/p127 tie the prior rate. No claim about infinitely many primes of the special forms.

**Odd-degree essential point:** F169 is NOT a subfield of F(13³), since 2 does not divide 3. The seed nevertheless embeds in any two-dimensional additive F13-subspace. The all-degree theorem does not require the extension degree to be even.

## Explicit new instance over GF(13³), exact finite check

Take K=F13[t]/(t³−2); the polynomial is irreducible since 2 is not a cube modulo 13. Nine binary weights are (1,t,2,2t,7+5t,−1+5t,t²,2t²,4t²). The first two coordinates retain the missing sum m=6+9t; take c=−m/2=10+2t. A legal anchored squared row has phase coefficients a_i=w_i/2 and anchor a0=c+Σ_i a_i. A directly programmed polynomial-ring multiplication check verifies all **512 of 512 squared outputs** are different.

**Adverse same-budget control:** the unshielded nine-bit Cartesian cube {0,...,7}³ in the same F13³ additive space has all **2197 off-diagonal pair sums**, so there is no translation that makes its 512 signs squared-injective. This contrasts the protected and unprotected constructions at identical field, oracle, and bit count.

## Universal all-signal reconstruction and sharp fixed-size consequence

Query k individual coordinate squares to reveal the support and choose one of two square roots u_i for each nonzero coordinate. Fix a supported anchor t. Write every nonzero signal coordinate as a relative sign η_i∈{±1} times u_i. In blocks of at most B=(d+1)r signs, query the same squared K-linear oracle with anchor coefficient a0/u_t and phase coefficients a_i/u_i. The squared reading decodes each relative sign. A short final block is obtained by absorbing unused phase coefficients into the anchor, preserving validity. Zero, sparse, and full support are all handled. Therefore m(k,s)≤k+ceil((s−1)/B) for s≥1; m(k,0)=k.

For k=B+1≥2, **k+1** queries suffice and attain the global adaptive information lower bound. A square over Fq has M=(q+1)/2 outcomes, while q^k input vectors modulo global ± represent (q^k+1)/2 classes; M^k < (q^k+1)/2 for q>1 and k≥2. In particular **GF(13³), k=10: 11 instead of 12** from the earlier 8-sign block guarantee. No claim that the nine-sign block is alphabet-optimal in GF(13³): the alphabet ceiling permits ten, which remain unconstructed.

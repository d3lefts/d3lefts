# Proof After Scarcity, Version 2
## Source-bound dependency models, exact finite sensitivity, and an unvalidated verification-priority hypothesis

**Research note | 29 October 2026 | Supersedes the editorial framing of the 28 October public dossier**

**Direction:** Devin J. Wright (NIGHTFALL / PHASESHIFT)  
**Production disclosure:** Written and technically developed with ChatGPT and Claude assistance. Mathematical derivations, code proposals, and internal critiques are AI-assisted. No independent human peer review or claim of original mathematical priority.

### Abstract

Verification of a complex claim requires preserving the distinction between a document that *mentions* an earlier source and a proof whose validity *depends* on an earlier claim. Proof After Scarcity (PAS) proposes a representation and evaluation protocol in which claims, versioned source spans, typed dependencies, uncertainties, and reviewer decisions travel together. The mathematically established part is an elementary finite-DAG path-sum model with exact one-edge sensitivity and conditional interval bounds. The empirical hypothesis—that these representations improve the allocation of a limited verification budget—remains **unvalidated**. This revision deliberately separates that hypothesis from a historical retrospective prioritization exercise that did not establish an advantage and cannot support prospective evaluation claims.

**Current status:** finite linear-algebra identities proved below; external source-to-dependency annotation incomplete; no independently demonstrated improvement in search accuracy, error discovery, verification labor, or deployed AI safety.

## 1. The question and the units of analysis

Given \(m\) claim-bearing units and a limited verification budget, can a reviewer select checks that maximize the expected discovery of *substantive, source-supported* defects while maintaining a machine-reconstructable chain from an assertion to the evidence on which it relies?

The first objective is a **representation contract**; the second is a **prospective evaluation hypothesis**. A representation may be exact in its bookkeeping and still fail to predict which check is worthwhile.

An atom is a versioned assertion `C = (subject, predicate, object, qualifier, scope, time, source_id, version_id, span, digest)`. Source spans and content digests identify *what was inspected*, not whether the source is truthful. A logical dependency edge requires separately justified evidence that one claim relies on another. Citation, co-citation, and common-source links remain **different edge types**. A correction produces a new claim version and invalidates any unreviewed descendant assertion; the historical version remains accessible.

### 1.1 Typed relationships

| Relationship | Intended semantics | Permitted inference |
|---|---|---|
| `cites` | Bibliographic or textual reference | The referenced work was mentioned; no logical dependence is implied |
| `depends_on` | A stated logical premise is needed for this proof step | Dependency propagation **only if the annotation is substantiated** |
| `same_source` | Claims share an evidentiary origin | Potential correlation or a review priority; no implication of invalidity |
| `formalized_by` | A specified artifact and version check a specified statement | Bounded formal-check evidence; does not certify the wider manuscript |
| `verified_by` | A checker, input manifest and expected property are published | Reproduction within the checker's stated scope |
| `contradicted_by` | A specified counterexample or correction conflicts with a claim | Re-review the affected scoped claim and descendants |

A citation graph is allowed to contain cycles. A **single finite proof's line-dependency graph** is well-founded and can be arranged as a DAG. The two objects must never be conflated.

## 2. Finite dependency transfer: what is actually proved

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a finite DAG of \(n\) proof steps in a fixed topological order. Define a nonnegative strictly upper-triangular matrix \(B\), with \(B_{ij}>0\) only where \(i<j\) is an eligible dependency edge. Entry magnitudes are *model parameters* and are not inferred from citations by default.

### Proposition 1 — finite path-sum identity

For any such \(B\), \(B^n=0\). Consequently,

\[
R(B)=(I-B)^{-1}=I+B+B^2+\cdots+B^{n-1}.
\]

Here \((B^k)_{ij}\) is the sum of products of edge weights over all length-\(k\) directed paths from \(i\) to \(j\).

**Proof.** A product of \(n\) nonzero upper-triangular edges would require a strictly increasing chain of \(n+1\) indices among only \(n\) indices, which is impossible. Thus \(B^n=0\). Multiplication of the finite geometric sum by \(I-B\) gives \(I-B^n=I\). The path expression follows by ordinary matrix multiplication. \(\square\)

For nonnegative vectors \(a,b\), define a *modeled exposure functional*

\[
E(B;a,b)=a^\mathsf{T}R(B)b.
\]

The scalar is an exact combinatorial function of declared weights; it is **not** an empirical probability of mathematical error without a separate mapping from annotations to calibrated parameters.

### Proposition 2 — exact single-edge sensitivity

For \(i<j\) and any scalar \(t\geq 0\), let \(B'=B+t e_i e_j^\mathsf{T}\), preserving the topological order. Then

\[
R(B')=R(B)+t\,R(B)e_i e_j^\mathsf{T}R(B),
\]

and hence

\[
E(B';a,b)-E(B;a,b)
=t\bigl(a^\mathsf{T}R(B)e_i\bigr)\bigl(e_j^\mathsf{T}R(B)b\bigr).
\]

The effect of changing **one** forward edge is exactly affine in its weight.

**Proof.** Write \(R=(I-B)^{-1}\). Since \(R\) is upper triangular and \(j>i\), \(e_j^\mathsf{T} R e_i=0\). Hence \(X=R e_i e_j^\mathsf{T}\) satisfies \(X^2=0\). Factor \(I-B'= (I-B)(I-tR e_i e_j^\mathsf{T})\); its inverse is \((I+tR e_i e_j^\mathsf{T})R\). Expanding gives the identity, and multiplication by \(a^\mathsf{T}\) and \(b\) yields the exposure difference. \(\square\)

This is also the ordinary partial derivative with respect to \(B_{ij}\). The formula permits targeted *conditional* sensitivity audits once credible edge weights exist. Large modeled sensitivity is **not proof** that the underlying claim is false.

### Proposition 3 — conditional interval certificate

Suppose three matrices share the same topological order and satisfy \(0\le B_{\min}\le B\le B_{\max}\) entrywise. Then, for \(a,b\ge 0\),

\[
E(B_{\min};a,b)\ \le\ E(B;a,b)\ \le\ E(B_{\max};a,b).
\]

**Proof.** For nonnegative matrices, entrywise order is preserved by addition and multiplication. By induction, \(B_{\min}^{k}\le B^{k}\le B_{\max}^{k}\) for every \(k\). Sum the finite path expansions and contract with nonnegative \(a,b\). \(\square\)

**Critical limitation:** this is a bound **conditional on justified weight intervals**. An unlabeled citation or AI-generated similarity score does not justify \(B_{\min},B_{\max}\); until the inputs are warranted, the interval is a calculation about a scenario, not an evidence certificate about the literature.

### A small exact example

Take a four-node chain with \(B_{12}=1/2,\ B_{23}=1/3,\ B_{34}=1/4\), every other entry zero, \(a=e_1\), and \(b=e_4\). Then \(E(B)=1/24\). Increasing the middle edge by \(t=1/6\) changes the exposure by \(t(1/2)(1/4)=1/48\), giving \(E(B')=1/16\). The calculation illustrates a finite identity, not empirical model calibration.

## 3. Evidence admission before numerical prioritization

A proposed dependency edge is only *admissible* after its annotator supplies:

1. An exact assertion and its scope, with source identifiers, timestamps, content digests, and locations of the supporting spans.
2. A dependency explanation identifying where the conclusion uses the premise; ordinary bibliography metadata is insufficient.
3. A status distinguishing explicit formalized proof, independently replayed finite computation, internal AI check, attributed published method, unverified hypothesis, and unresolved conflict.
4. An adversarial test or reviewer challenge that would change the status, with an immutable record of prior classifications.

Missing links should remain **unknown**, not silently become zero-weight edges. Alternatives are (i) a bounded scenario analysis with explicit conditional assumptions, or (ii) abstention from ranking.

The public corpus snapshot from October 2026 includes 722 manuscripts, 597 **bibliographic** citation edges, and 73 mutually citing groups. It does *not* contain 597 independently validated proof dependencies. A cyclic reuse component must not be inserted into the finite-DAG inverse without changing the theorem's hypotheses and providing an independent justification for any convergence claim.

## 5. Open empirical question and prospective experiment

**Hypothesis to test, not an achieved result:** source-admissible, typed dependency structure may improve the use of scarce verification effort, compared with simple, predeclared baselines.

Before collecting prospective outcomes:

- Freeze corpus version, eligible claim set, exact identifiers, outcome definitions and sampling rules using a publicly verifiable timestamp and hashes.
- Independently annotate `depends_on` against blinded source evidence and adjudicate disagreements; quantify missing links and spurious-edge rates.
- Compare (i) random and cheap-first, (ii) upstream-first and reach-per-cost, (iii) a source-grounded flat baseline, and (iv) the PAS weighted-DAG policy with equal evaluation budgets.
- Report first-check and first-(k) success rates together with calibration, reviewer time, false-premise rate, latency, and abstention; separate all model-development data from final held-out cases.
- Decide success with a predeclared paired test and adequate event count. If the PAS policy fails against an elementary baseline, retain that result and do not claim an improvement.

A separate historical H1 score date of **2027-04-06** was recorded for the incomplete manuscript-graph freeze. A new v2 experimental design does **not** retroactively repair that freeze, change its date, or invent completed outcomes.

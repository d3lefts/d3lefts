# PAS historical evaluation record — 13 October 2026

**Record status:** retained as an adverse retrospective experiment. This file preserves the earlier evidence and is linked from [PAS Version 2](PAS_V2_20261029.md). It is **not** a claim of prospective predictive utility, external validation, or human-independent experimental design.

## Reported comparison and boundaries

The 13 October 2026 *retrospective* stress test found that uncalibrated causal triage selected the historically documented weak-link category first in **8 of 20** challenged cases. Upstream-first and reach-per-cost baselines each scored **11 of 20**. Within the first three checks, PAS scored **17/20**, while both baselines scored **19/20**.

Cases, coarse labels, and simplified dependency graphs were selected **after historical outcomes were known**. The scores measure coarse-category agreement, **not** defects discovered, reviewer-hours saved, real error rates, or prospective predictive superiority. Neither method's future performance can be inferred from these denominators.

The historical 24-case inventory included 20 challenged cases with scored retrospective weak-link categories and four censored corroboration / formalization controls: the 2012 Higgs-boson results, 2015 GW150914, the four-colour theorem, and the Kepler conjecture. The four controls lacked a known defect-category label, were excluded from the 20-case denominator, and served only as evidence-structure illustrations.

## Why the result is no longer the PAS manuscript's lead claim

- A retrospective, selected-after-outcome comparison cannot validate performance on new examples.
- PAS ranked **below** two simple baselines under the reported experimental scoring.
- Published aggregates without full independently replayable source case and annotation materials do not justify a stronger empirical status.
- The elementary finite-DAG identities in PAS v2 stand separately from the behavior of this heuristic.

This evaluation is therefore an archived **failed validation attempt**, not a novel breakthrough, a result to conceal, or evidence for the proposed verification-priority hypothesis. Any future empirical claim requires preregistration, blinded outcomes, source-complete test data, and matched-budget baselines.

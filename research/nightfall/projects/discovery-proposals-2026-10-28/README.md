# NIGHTFALL / PHASESHIFT — Ten research proposals

**Dated:** 2026-10-28  
**Status:** Proposed / untested. None of the ten proposals is presented as a new theorem, verified improvement, experimental success, or priority claim.  
**Taxonomy:** Mathematics · Quantum · Super Intelligence · Safety · Systems  
**Scope:** Two new, independently testable investigations per research category.

These are executable research briefs, grounded in the existing NIGHTFALL archive. Each program starts with a dated prior-art check and an independent verifier designed before the first candidate. Any previously established result is a baseline, never a new discovery. Proposed numerical success thresholds are decision gates, not achieved measurements. A project advances from proposal only after its proof obligations and evidence package have been independently checked.

## Mathematics

## P01. Finite-to-infinite positivity transfer

**Question.** Can a reciprocal-Hankel positivity argument be made independent of the largest finite rank ever checked?

**Representation / construction.** Express the target form as a positive comparison kernel plus a tail-coupling operator. Isolate the block-off-diagonal interaction using Schur complements, trace-class bounds, and a norm bound uniform in rank and the continuous scale parameter. Track separately the small-scale, intermediate, and large-scale regimes; identify the overlap conditions before joining them.

**Cheapest decisive experiment.** Reproduce a small verified block with two independent interval-arithmetic implementations; check whether the proposed off-diagonal bound remains below the smallest proven positive margin under a rank-doubling transition. Pre-register its worst regime and a numerical margin prediction before running.

**Falsification.** An interval-enclosed negative eigenvalue, a missing scale interval, a tail bound that grows faster than the verified margin, or an unproved kernel comparison halts any global positivity claim.

**Certificate / output.** A precisely stated operator inequality, range-explicit error bounds, an independent interval checker, reproducible block data, and a proof-dependency map. **Novelty gate:** a theorem valid across a rank range beyond finite enumeration, checked against current literature.

## P02. Active-contact minimax for minimum overlap

**Question.** Can changing the active shift-contact set produce a *certified* improvement over the strongest archived NIGHTFALL continuous-overlap upper-bound witness?

**Representation / construction.** Work with the signed, zero-padded overlap objective and every continuous shift. Alternate minimax primal construction and maximin dual contact selection; use algebraic contact events to guide the next structural transition, then reconstruct the resulting cells as exact rational numbers.

**Cheapest decisive experiment.** Start at a smaller grid with independently audited overlap conventions. Predict which previously inactive shift becomes tight, lift that contact pattern to higher resolution, and verify all lags by integer arithmetic. Avoid circular correlations and grid-width normalization errors.

**Success / falsification.** Require a verified strict improvement of at least **10⁻⁸**, exceeding the numerical and verifier uncertainty budget, measured against a recovered, hashed baseline. A tie, an incorrect normalization, an omitted shift, or a drifted witness hash is a failed experiment.

**Certificate / output.** Full integer/rational cell witness, exact continuous-shift reduction, complete lag audit, baseline hash, source comparison, and independent checker. A public-record claim additionally requires same-day leaderboard and eligibility review.

## Quantum

## P03. Symmetry-reduced finite-copy Werner certificates

**Question.** Do symmetry-adapted dual witnesses yield stronger defensible finite-copy undistillability thresholds, especially at five, six, and eight copies?

**Representation / construction.** Reduce the witness search using copy permutations and representation-theoretic block decompositions; construct rational multipliers with explicit separability and positivity premises. Separate all-dimension, qutrit, unconditional-source, and conditional-source comparisons.

**Cheapest decisive experiment.** Solve a small exact block, reconstruct rational multipliers, and rerun the inequalities in an independent algebra package. Compare each certificate only with the corresponding copy count and evidence class.

**Falsification.** A failed imported inequality, invalid representation reduction, hidden dependence on an unproved source, or a certificate margin within tolerance blocks promotion.

**Certificate / output.** Exact rational duals, basis-conversion identities, explicit imported premises, same-class benchmark table, and independent verifier. **Success gate:** a strict and externally checkable improvement in at least one stated evidence class.

## P04. Compositional transfer across Werner copy counts

**Question.** Can a small library of proven finite-copy witness primitives generate useful, rigorously controlled certificates for larger copy counts?

**Representation / construction.** Define a tensor-composition rule tracking witness normalization, separability, and error/slack accumulation. Search for a recurrence with an explicit loss function, rather than extrapolating a table of numerical thresholds.

**Cheapest decisive experiment.** Derive and independently verify the first nontrivial composition identity on small copy counts, then test its predicted values across the existing 4–25 registry before extending it toward 100 copies.

**Falsification.** Nonmonotone hidden slack, a failure of separability preservation, unsupported dimensional generality, or a higher-copy bound weaker than its existing benchmark falsifies the proposed transfer mechanism in that regime.

**Certificate / output.** Symbolic recurrence, exact proof of composition, finite regression table with source statuses, and checker. **Success gate:** a certified nontrivial compositional theorem with competitive explicit finite-copy consequences.

## Super Intelligence

## P05. The verification-capacity frontier

**Question.** As an agent's possible outputs and tool actions expand, which verification guarantees become impossible under fixed observation, computation, and trusted-evidence budgets?

**Representation / construction.** Model the monitored process as a family of action/observation systems. Separately vary the observer's information partition, time complexity budget, and authenticated evidence supply. Search for matching upper and lower bounds that identify the precise resource whose growth closes each gap.

**Cheapest decisive experiment.** Construct minimal finite counterexamples for each proposed boundary, then verify them by exhaustive model checking. Before interpreting any result as a superintelligence limit, state the exact agent class and permitted monitor interface.

**Falsification.** A richer permitted interface that restores the allegedly impossible guarantee, a flawed quantifier, or a changed security model invalidates the claimed frontier.

**Certificate / output.** Formal definitions, machine-checkable finite examples, proof obligations, calibrated scaling curves, and explicit scope limitations. **Novelty gate:** compare with established oversight and information-theoretic impossibility results.

## P06. Self-improving scientific discovery policies

**Question.** Can an AI research system learn to select *representation changes and search-operator families* in ways that increase the rate of independently certified discoveries?

**Representation / construction.** Represent each experiment by its starting state, operator family, predicted discriminating result, cost, and verifier outcome. Train a selection policy on completed runs while withholding entire problem families during testing. Treat structural transitions as distinct actions from local parameter tuning.

**Cheapest decisive experiment.** Run a preregistered, budget-matched comparison against a fixed operator rotation on multiple archived NIGHTFALL tasks, with exact replay checkers frozen ahead of candidate generation.

**Falsification.** Improvement only on training families, reduced candidate-to-certificate survival, higher uncaught failure rates, or gains explained by more compute rules out a general discovery advantage.

**Certificate / output.** Versioned policy, held-out task split, frozen baseline, full experiment log, replayable checkers, and certified-gain-per-compute-hour statistics. **Success gate:** a repeated held-out improvement with confidence bounds and independent replay.

## Safety

## P07. Composed-authority closure in agent toolchains

**Question.** When individually permitted tools, connectors, and delegated agents are combined, can the resulting authority exceed the intended grant boundaries?

**Representation / construction.** Model capabilities as a typed reachability hypergraph: each tool call has prerequisites, provenance, user-intent scope, and an effect. Compute minimal unauthorized compositions and compare static enforcement with stateful history-sensitive monitors.

**Cheapest decisive experiment.** Generate exhaustive small tool graphs, construct a minimal counterexample, and independently check both the reachability witness and a proposed guard policy.

**Falsification.** A graph model that silently assumes extra privileges, a monitor that rejects legitimate authorized work at unacceptable rates, or an unsound reduction invalidates the proposed mitigation.

**Certificate / output.** Threat model, small exact counterexamples, guard semantics, formal finite-state checks, and false-positive measurements on benign tool workflows. No live unauthorized access is necessary.

## P08. SourceBound natural-web provenance trial

**Question.** Does evidence-gated claim admission reduce unsupported published statements while retaining useful factual recall on held-out, naturally occurring web tasks?

**Representation / construction.** Freeze the SourceBound implementation, the claim-to-source admission rule, and a blinded annotation scheme. Separate retrieved material, source attribution, supported claims, and unsupported claims; stratify by ambiguous pages, adversarial formatting, and multi-source disagreements.

**Cheapest decisive experiment.** Pre-register a small held-out set with independent human adjudication, compare a baseline publisher to SourceBound on identical retrieved pages, and report unsupported-claim rate **alongside recall** and abstention.

**Falsification.** Lower unsupported-claim counts caused solely by suppressing most claims, leakage from synthetic fixtures, annotator disagreement, or unsupported claims slipping through the admission gate blocks an effectiveness claim.

**Certificate / output.** Frozen corpus splits, annotation guide, paired outputs, uncertainty intervals, full negative examples, and reproducible evaluation code. A passing synthetic test remains distinct from held-out effectiveness.

## Systems

## P09. Proof-carrying research releases

**Question.** Can a research repository automatically prevent a promoted claim from outrunning the exact evidence and dependencies supporting it?

**Representation / construction.** Model each claim as a dependency graph linking theorem premises, source versions, witness hashes, checker commands, environments, and verdicts. Build a release gate that blocks status promotion on missing evidence or a failed independent replay.

**Cheapest decisive experiment.** Replay three frozen existing cases from clean environments, then deliberately mutate a witness byte, a source premise, and a claimed status. Each mutation must fail closed with an explanatory receipt.

**Falsification.** Undetected mutation, environment-dependent success, ambiguous scope, or a false PASS after an altered imported assumption is a release-blocking defect.

**Certificate / output.** Machine-readable manifests, deterministic runner, independently produced PASS/FAIL/INCONCLUSIVE receipts, and a public sample release containing inputs and hashes.

## P10. Budget-aware experiment scheduling

**Question.** Can a scheduler allocate research compute using expected certified gain and information value, rather than optimizing raw candidate scores?

**Representation / construction.** Estimate each experiment's cost, probability of falsifying a hypothesis, expected certificate value, and active plateau regime. Enforce fixed exploration reserve, hard stop rules, and a rule to switch operator families after repeated unproductive trials.

**Cheapest decisive experiment.** Backtest chronological, leakage-safe decisions on existing logged experiments, then run a budget-matched prospective comparison against simple round-robin scheduling.

**Falsification.** A gain produced by hindsight leakage, incorrect cost accounting, concentration on unverifiable candidates, or lower certified-gain-per-hour falsifies the scheduler's advantage.

**Certificate / output.** Scheduling policy, signed run ledger, frozen baselines, independent scoring code, and out-of-sample efficiency report. **Success gate:** independently replayed improvement under equal compute budgets.

## Shared validation contract

1. **Prior art first:** identify the strongest available external baseline and record its retrieval date before the first substantial run.
2. **Separate roles:** one engine constructs candidates; an independently implemented verifier attacks them using exact or interval arithmetic when feasible.
3. **Preserve provenance:** each number must trace to inputs, a replay command, hashes, and error accounting; undocumented measurements remain conjectures.
4. **Pre-register decisions:** prediction, cheapest discriminating experiment, success threshold, failure modes, budget, and stop rule.
5. **Explicit status transitions:** Proposed → Numerical candidate → Certified construction → Theorem (independently checked proof) → Novel contribution (prior-art audit) → Competitive record (current eligibility and human approval).
6. **Stop productively:** three failed distinct operator families, negligible remaining gap, exhausted budget, or a better-valued opportunity is a legitimate recorded conclusion.

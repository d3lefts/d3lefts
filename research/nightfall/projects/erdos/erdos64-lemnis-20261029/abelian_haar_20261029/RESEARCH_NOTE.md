# LEMNIS / NIGHTFALL — universal 16-cycle certificate for three-offset abelian Haar graphs

**Research lead:** Devin J. Wright / NIGHTFALL PHASESHIFT  
**Proof / exact internal check:** Lemnis (GPT-6), October 29, 2026  
**Status:** self-contained structural proof, two independently written finite checks, publication priority and external expert proof review **OPEN**. This theorem is a **restricted graph-family result**. It neither resolves Erdős #64 nor improves any all-graph finite-order bound.

## Theorem

Let `A` be any abelian group, finite or infinite, and choose **three distinct** elements `s0,s1,s2`. Define the simple cubic bipartite Haar graph `H(A,S)` with vertices `A x {0,1}` and undirected edges `(x,0)--(x+s,1)` for each `s in {s0,s1,s2}`. Then `H(A,S)` contains a **simple cycle of length 4, 8 or 16**.

The graph need not be connected. The conclusion holds in each connected component because the proof uses only the subgroup generated by `s1-s0` and `s2-s0`.

### Exact universal witness

Translate the offsets to `S={0,g,h}`. Map the freely generated offset differences to `Z^2` with `0→(0,0)`, `g→(1,0)`, `h→(0,1)`. For a label word `w=(w0,...,w_(L−1))`, traverse from side 0, adding the selected offset at even steps and subtracting it at odd steps. A word is **cyclically reduced** when consecutive labels, including last and first, differ. Such a closed walk has no immediate backtracking.

Use this length-16 word (alphabet `0,g,h` represented by digits `0,1,2`):

    0101012010201212

Its alternating displacement is `(0,0)` in **free** `Z^2`, so it is a closed walk in every abelian quotient. It is cyclically reduced, and its 16 vertices are pairwise distinct in the universal free abelian graph. To prove they remain distinct under every quotient *without C4 or C8*, classify any possible collision of two vertices:

* Opposite-side positions are distinct by bipartiteness.
* Same-side positions at cyclic separation 2/14 cannot coincide, since the consecutive edge labels differ and the three offsets are distinct.
* Separation 4/12 produces a cyclically nonbacktracking closed 4-walk, hence a C4.
* Separation 8 produces a cyclically nonbacktracking closed 8-walk. In a **bipartite C4-free** simple graph, such a walk is necessarily a simple C8: an interior vertex repeat would split it into an even closed reduced segment of length 2, 4 or 6; a 2-segment backtracks, a 4-segment gives C4, and a 6-segment leaves a 2-edge closed backtrack.
* Separation 6/10 is the only nontrivial case. Its relative displacement belongs to the following **exhaustive 12-element list**. The indicated cyclically reduced length-4 or length-8 word has the same alternating displacement. Thus a collision forces an actual C4 or C8 by the previous observation.

| Relative vector `(g,h)` | Explicit short word | Length |
|---|---|---:|
| (-3,0) | `01012102` | 8 |
| (-3,1) | `01212102` | 8 |
| (-2,1) | `0121` | 4 |
| (-2,2) | `2121` | 4 |
| (-1,1) | `01212012` | 8 |
| (-1,2) | `2021` | 4 |
| (0,1) | `01202012` | 8 |
| (0,2) | `2020` | 4 |
| (1,1) | `1020` | 4 |
| (1,2) | `10102120` | 8 |
| (2,0) | `1010` | 4 |
| (2,1) | `10202012` | 8 |

**Conclusion.** Under the contrary assumption that C4 and C8 are absent, no two vertices on the universal length-16 closed walk coincide. Therefore it is a simple C16. This proves the theorem for every abelian group, with no vertex-count bound and no analytic approximation.

### Boundary and falsification

The word's closure uses commutativity. If `g,h` are treated as noncommuting free-group generators, its alternating product freely reduces to a **nontrivial 10-letter word**, so this certificate cannot be asserted for arbitrary nonabelian Haar graphs. The 32 possible single-letter changes to the chosen 16-word fail at least one certificate condition. A graph with repeated offsets is outside the degree-three hypothesis.

## Independent reproducibility

- `verify_abelian_haar_symbolic.py` constructs *all* cyclically reduced 4-/8-word alternating displacements directly over `Z^2`, verifies the fixed 16-walk and its 12 exceptional displacement classes, and rejects all 32 single-symbol mutations. **PASS.**
- `verify_haar_graphs_independent.py` constructs actual vertex/edge adjacency using group addition and detects simple cycles by a separate DFS. It tested **2006** cyclic instances with orders `2m`, `7≤m≤65`, of which **1128** avoided both C4 and C8 and all had C16; it separately tested **97** three-offset graphs on groups `Z_p²` for `p=5,7,11,13`, with **79** avoiding C4/C8 and all admitting the 16-walk and DFS C16. **PASS.**
- `cayley_scan.cpp` is an additional separate C++ DFS: for `m≤120`, 3534 cyclic offset choices (one symmetry quotient) included **2626** C4/C8-free instances, **zero** C4/C8/C16-free instances. Counts concern the explicitly enumerated input family only. **PASS.**
- `voltage_scan.cpp` samples nonabelian permutation lifts and cyclic voltage lifts of K4 and K3,3 using fixed seed **259831**. The exploratory scan found no graph avoiding all C4/C8/C16. This is **negative search evidence**, not an exhaustive cover-class theorem. It is a distinct representation from the 3-offset Haar theorem and is not used in its proof.

## Evidence and publication priority

The literature baseline for the general Erdős–Gyárfás problem is Ducoffe–Dumitru, **arXiv:2609.28594**: verified through 40 general vertices, 48 cubic vertices, and 66 bipartite vertices. Tranquilli, **arXiv:2608.02675**, separately certified cubic bipartite up to 58. Related older work includes Ghasemi–Varmazyar (2020/2021), *On the Erdős–Gyárfás conjecture for some Cayley graphs*, which proves relevant dihedral/generalized-dihedral order-restricted cases, and the 2014 paper *Cubic bi-Cayley graphs over abelian groups* (European Journal of Combinatorics, DOI `10.1016/j.ejc.2013.10.005`). These are relevant scope and priority leads; we **do not claim** that this all-abelian 3-offset statement is absent from all prior literature or has worldwide novelty.

**Search transition:** the entire three-offset abelian Haar class is now excluded as a counterexample candidate; the numerical voltage-cover scans further suggest naive permutation lifts are an inefficient search strategy at tested parameters. Broader nonabelian covers and constrained lifts require a different operator and exact independent oracle; no new f(4) record is claimed.

# All fixed-order classical simulation of tensor-character oracles by normalized circuit deletion
NIGHTFALL research manuscript v0.1 — 2026-11-25. Research owner Devin J. Wright.

Status: complete author-side written proof, independently implemented exact finite falsification checks (not independent non-author review), no Lean terminal, no worldwide priority clearance. Do not publish or merge without review. The specific new mechanism is a polynomial-cost primitive extension on the full independent sign cube via normalized deletion of local zero-sum circuits. This closes the previously open all-fixed-T mathematical route in #1228, not the much sharper arbitrary-row-set T2 constants in #1068/#1112. Credits: Claude/ChatGPT Paper V tensor-source work; #1114 all-q primitive four-row geometry; #1228 general primitive localization; Lovitz (2018), Lovitz–Petrov (2023), Defant–Mastylo–Pérez (2019), Markov derivative inequality, Beals et al. quantum polynomial method.

## 1. Contract and theorem
Objective: polynomial original-coordinate query approximation for all fixed T on full binary pure-tensor character source. Feasible model: fixed integer q>=2, tensor depth L>=1, V=(F2^q)^{tensor L}, uniform secret s in V*, R any subset of distinct nonzero pure tensors. Oracle returns x_a=(-1)^{a(s)} for a in R. Quantum circuits have finitely many original-label orthogonal sectors, optionally a constant-zero label, fixed initial state and input-independent unitaries, final effect 0<=E<=I. Each query is O_x=P_0+sum_{a in R} x_a P_a with mutually orthogonal projectors summing to I; constant-zero label is known, no paid query. Acceptance defined on the full independent sign cube, not merely on code. Goal scalar acceptance distributional approximation, not runtime, full-state approximation, nonuniform secret, or polynomial dependence on T. Strongest internal baseline #1068 two-query rank-free paper theorem, #1228 fixed-order primitive fiber theorem and exponential nonprimitive obstruction. External reference checked this session: Lovitz–Petrov Forum Math Sigma 11 e27 Cor.21, https://doi.org/10.1017/fms.2023.20; Defant–Mastylo–Pérez arXiv:1706.03670 (Math Ann 374:653–680); related modern cb-BH arXiv:2609.05201 and distributional AA arXiv:2609.35327; no same tensor-code theorem found in targeted searches. Significant threshold: all fixed d and polynomial in L and inverse error, without an exponentially large nonprimitive multiplicity factor. Stop after explicit proof and exact source checker; retain gaps for non-author attack and Lean. No novelty claim for external tools.

Theorem A. For every fixed q,d>=1 (q>=2), every real multilinear P of degree <=d on {-1,1}^R, and epsilon,delta>0, if ||P||infty<=1, there is a deterministic nonadaptive original-label query estimator F(s) of P(x(s)) with P_s(|F-P|>epsilon)<=delta and query bound poly_{q,d}(L,1/epsilon,1/delta). Explicit bound given below. Constant d=0 needs zero queries. For a target in [0,1], clip F to [0,1]. In particular well-formed fixed-T quantum acceptance polynomials have d<=2T, so all such circuits satisfy Theorem A. The theorem is stronger than a quantum-only statement because it uses only globally bounded polynomial degree. No free secret or arbitrary Fourier-character queries: each retained character is implemented as product of <=d legal original labels. Runtime/preprocessing and constants can be enormous; the theorem is not a joint polynomial in T.

## 2. Primitive extension via local circuit operators
Write x_S=product_{a in S} x_a, including x_empty=1. A circuit C is an inclusion-minimal nonempty subset of R with XOR zero. Since labels are distinct nonzero F2 vectors, every circuit has at least 3 elements. A set is primitive iff it contains no circuit, equivalently it is linearly independent. On degree<=h polynomial space define linear maps
M x_S = n(S)x_S,  n(S)=number of circuits contained in S;
D x_S = sum_{C subset S circuit} x_{S minus C}.
Then M preserves degree, D lowers degree by at least 3 (zero for h<3), and (D P)(x(s))=(M P)(x(s)) for all s because x_C(s)=1.

Lemma 1 (local stochastic interpolation). Partition a finite coordinate set into blocks B of size <=b. Let A_B be any real local linear operator on functions of signs in B, extended by identity on other coordinates, with A_B 1=0 and local infinity norm <=a. Then for degree<=h polynomials
||sum_B A_B P||infty <= 4h^2 c ||P||infty, c=max(1,2^b a).
Proof: let U_B be the uniform average matrix on block sign functions. In value coordinates each entry of A_B has absolute value <=a (row absolute-sum norm). Thus T_B=U_B+A_B/c is entrywise nonnegative and its rows sum to one, since A_B1=0. Both T_B and U_B are stochastic. For t in [0,1], the product across disjoint blocks of ((1-t)I+tT_B) is stochastic, so its application to P has infinity norm <=||P||infty. At each fixed input x, the output is a polynomial of degree <=h in t: each monomial x_S meets at most |S|<=h blocks, and the factors on disjoint untouched blocks fix constants. Its derivative at 0 is sum_B(T_B-I)P(x), by the ordinary finite product rule. Markov's polynomial derivative inequality on [-1,1], rescaled to [0,1], gives absolute derivative <=2h^2||P||infty. Apply the identical argument to U_B and subtract, since A_B=c(T_B-U_B). Done. This argument does NOT multiply a local norm over the number of blocks.

Lemma 2 (circuit counting and deletion norm). Put Q=2^q-1. For h>=3 set b_h=Q^(h-2), c_h=8^b_h, and
A_h(L)=4h^2 c_h sum_{j=0}^{min(L,h-2)} binom(L,j).
On degree<=h polynomials, ||M P||infty and ||D P||infty are <=A_h(L)||P||infty.
Proof: each pure tensor has a unique nonzero local-factor tuple over F2, since nonzero scalar freedom is trivial. By Lovitz–Petrov Corollary 21, a size-k tensor circuit varies in <=k-2 factors, since local span dimension>1 exactly at varying sites. A circuit of size<=h therefore has a unique varying set J of size<=h-2. For fixed J, partition R by its factors outside J. Each block has at most Q^|J|<=b_h rows. Every circuit with exact varying set J lies wholly in exactly one block. Define local count and delete maps on Walsh monomials x_U of that block, using only circuits of size<=h with exact varying set J contained in U. Each map vanishes on constant 1. On any block function of infinity norm <=1 each Fourier coefficient has magnitude <=1, and each monomial image is a sum of <=2^b_h signed monomials (or a multiple of one), so local norm <=4^b_h. Thus c_h>=2^b_h 4^b_h meets Lemma 1. Apply Lemma 1 to all blocks for each J, then sum over J. Every circuit in an original degree<=h monomial is counted exactly once; no extra contribution from circuits of size>h. Done. On a degree<=h polynomial M is the same global map for all h, so iteration of M stays in the space on which its bound holds. D maps into degree<=h-3.

Lemma 3 (polynomial-cost primitive extension). Define U_h=1 for h=0,1,2. For h>=3 let N_h=2^h and recursively
U_h=(1+A_h)^N_h + A_h N_h (1+A_h)^(N_h-1) U_(h-3).
For every degree<=h P there is a primitive-supported polynomial G of degree<=h, with G(x(s))=P(x(s)) for all secrets and ||G||infty<=U_h||P||infty.
Proof by induction. For h<=2 all supports are primitive. Otherwise let p_h(t)=product_{j=1}^{N_h}(1-t/j), r_h(t)=(1-p_h(t))/t, interpreted as a polynomial (divisible numerator; constant is sum 1/j). n(S) is an integer in [0,N_h]. Thus p_h(M)P retains exactly the primitive Fourier coefficients, and M r_h(M)P is the complementary part. On code D r_h(M)P=M r_h(M)P. The former is of degree<=h-3, so by induction has a primitive extension G_low. Set G=p_h(M)P+G_low. Identity on code and degree bound follow. The norm of p_h(M) is <=(1+A_h)^N_h by submultiplicativity. Coefficients of r_h have absolute values bounded by binomial(N_h,k+1) at degree k, since reciprocals 1/j<=1. Hence ||r_h(M)||<=N_h(1+A_h)^(N_h-1), and applying D then the induction bound yields precisely U_h. All constants depend only on q,h and polynomially on L at fixed q,h; for example exponent <=sum_{j=h,h-3,...,j>=3}(j-2)2^j. This is not an efficient algorithm for writing the full extension; existence suffices for query complexity. End proof.

## 3. Primitive frequency multiplicity (self-contained from #1228)
Let f!=0 admit a primitive support size<=d, and let B be a minimum-cardinality representation in R, k<=d. It is independent, since deleting any zero subset shortens it. For any independent representation A of <=d rows with XOR f, peel A symmetric difference B into disjoint inclusion-minimal nonempty zero-sum circuits (remaining sum stays zero). Each circuit meets both A and B by their independence. By Cor.21, union of all varying sites has cardinality <=|A symmetric difference B|-2(number of circuits)<=d+k-2 when A!=B. Anchor each A row in the circuit at a B row. Outside this site union they agree in all local factors; shared rows anchor themselves. If A=B use empty site set. For any specified J of j sites there are <=k Q^j possible rows, hence
K_d(L)=max(1, sum_{j=0}^{min(L,2d-2)} binom(L,j) sum_{u=1}^d binom(d Q^j,u))
bounds all primitive fibers, with binom out of range zero. K_d=O_{q,d}(L^(2d-2)). The zero frequency has only the empty primitive support. This holds for arbitrary R subset, including cases where the ambient rank is smaller.

## 4. Fourier truncation and query terminal
Defant–Mastylo–Pérez Boolean BH inequality supplies a finite degree-only constant B_d^BH for p=2d/(d+1), including p=1 for d=1:
(sum_S |g_S|^p)^(1/p)<=B_d^BH ||G||infty
for real polynomial G of degree<=d on any finite sign cube. For a_f=sum_{S primitive, XOR S=f} g_S, Hölder gives sum_f|a_f|^p<=K_d^(p-1) sum_S|g_S|^p. Thus ||a||_p<= B:=K_d^(1-1/p) B_d^BH U_d ||P||infty.
Order |a_f| decreasingly; outside the largest k, max |a_f|<=B(k+1)^(-1/p). Then sum outside |a_f|^2 <= B^2(k+1)^(-(2-p)/p)=B^2(k+1)^(-1/d). Uniform s in V* makes characters orthonormal, including when R does not span V. With k=ceil((B^2/(epsilon^2 delta))^d), retaining min(k,#frequencies) gives MSE<=epsilon^2 delta; if B=0 the function is zero. Every retained f has a support of <=d original labels, so evaluate it by product of those original oracle answers. Constant term free, reuse queries; bound min(|R|,d k) (if querying all use exact P); clipping for [0,1] decreases error. Markov proves failure <=delta. No query to hidden s itself. Preprocessing may enumerate all coefficients; no polynomial-time claim. For d fixed this is polynomial in L and inverse tolerances.
Quantum corollary: for arbitrary independent sign assignment, O_x is a self-adjoint unitary since sectors orthogonal; fixed unitary circuit yields unit norm, terminal effect gives [0,1]. Expand path amplitudes in x; each of T oracle calls increases multilinear degree by at most one (x_a^2=1), so squared acceptance has degree<=2T. Mixed initial state can be purified without changing query model. Thus apply theorem with d=2T, including arbitrary complex Hilbert dimension (finite original labels, well-defined unitaries) and arbitrary effect. No rank, trace, noncommuting-effect or primitive-path assumption. For T=0 no query. Physical raw intensity corollaries require their previously specified coherent reversible response-bit interface, not optical hardware claims.

## 5. General mechanism and immediate bounded-arity consequence
Abstract transfer: any family of nonzero distinct binary labels for which all circuits of size<=d can be partitioned into K collections of block-local circuits, each collection's blocks disjoint and size bounded by b, has ||M||,||D||<=4d^2 8^b K on bounded-degree polynomials by Lemma 1. If primitive frequency multiplicities also have a controlled bound, the entire proof applies with these parameters. Polynomial K and multiplicity yield polynomial query bound; constant parameters yield dimension-free query bound. No assumption arbitrary matroids admit such covers. This is a reusable operator family. Graph edge labels of bounded degree have such a cover by coloring short cycles in the edge-overlap graph, but an easier result is below and handles unbounded graph degree.
Theorem B (bounded-arity labels). Let any set R of distinct nonzero binary rows have each row supported on at most w coordinates in some known linear basis of the secret dual; secret uniform. For any globally bounded degree<=d P on the row-sign cube, F(s)=P(x(s)) is a bounded Boolean polynomial in the independent secret bits of degree<=wd. BH on those bits directly gives ||hat F||_{2wd/(wd+1)}<=B^BH_(wd)||P||infty. Every nonzero coefficient has a frequency that is XOR of <=d actual original labels by the original expansion. Query its chosen original representation, not the secret bits. As above, retaining k=ceil(((B^BH_(wd)||P||infty)^2/(epsilon^2 delta))^(wd)) yields min(|R|,d k) queries. No graph degree, density, girth or Wyner restriction. In particular all graph-edge parity sources have w=2 (simple graph labels e_u+e_v); all fixed-T scalar acceptance approximation in this coherent response oracle model is dimension-free, with degree parameter 4T. Physical normalized squared intensity y_uv=(1+sigma_u sigma_v)/n encodes the same sign; one legal classical intensity read suffices to recover it under the exact oracle model. This elementary consequence of known BH and query polynomial expansion is not by itself claimed novel; it improves internal high-girth sufficient condition #1130 for this graph family. The tensor theorem cannot simply use Theorem B because pure tensor labels can have support exponential in depth in the standard factor basis.

## 6. Negative controls, prior art and certification
Exponential unnormalized fibers remain real: for f=(1,...,1) over q=2, the four distinct supports {f,(1,v),(2,v),(3,v)} for each tail v!=f's tail XOR to f and are nonprimitive. Count 3^(L-1)-1. The current proof never counts them; normalized deletion avoids that loss via local stochastic derivative bounds. Without bounded-size local circuit blocks or without globally bounded independent-cube extension, the argument does not apply. For nonuniform secret Parseval orthogonality does not hold unchanged; no claim.

## Theorem

Let \((\Omega_i,\mu_i)_{i\in J}\) be any family of independent probability coordinates (finite or arbitrary measurable alphabets, including distributions with arbitrarily small or zero atoms), and let \(f\in L^\infty(\bigotimes_{i\in J}\mu_i)\) be real with \(|f|\le B\) almost surely and **Hoeffding interaction degree at most two**. Put

\[
V=\operatorname{Var}(f),\quad
I_i(f)=\mathbb E\operatorname{Var}(f\mid X_{J\setminus\{i\}})
=\frac12\mathbb E(f(X)-f(X_{-i},X'_i))^2,\quad M=\sup_{i\in J}I_i(f).
\]

Here \(X_i'\) is an independent replacement drawn from \(\mu_i\). Then

\[
\boxed{V\le 15(1+\sqrt3)B\sqrt M},\qquad
\boxed{V^2\le225(1+\sqrt3)^2B^2M}.\tag{T}
\]

For infinite coordinate sets, degree \(\le2\) means belonging to the \(L^2\)-closed subspace generated by constants and centered functions of at most two coordinates. No finite alphabet size, positive minimum atom, finite dimension, or bounded number of interactions is assumed. If \(B=0\) or \(V=0\), the conclusion is immediate.

## Proof for finite full-support products

It is enough to consider finite coordinates \(\{1,\dots,n\}\) with positive probabilities on every retained alphabet symbol. Atoms with mass zero can be discarded; their values do not affect essential boundedness, variance or influence. Let \(m=\mathbb Ef\) and use the *actual* Hoeffding components

\[
u_i(a)=\mathbb E[f\mid X_i=a]-m,\qquad
h_{ij}(a,b)=\mathbb E[f\mid X_i=a,X_j=b]-m-u_i(a)-u_j(b).
\]

Since the Hoeffding degree is at most two, the equality
\[
f(x)=m+\sum_i u_i(x_i)+\sum_{i<j}h_{ij}(x_i,x_j)\tag{1}
\]
holds at every positive-mass input. Each \(u_i\) is centered, each \(h_{ij}\) is centered separately in both coordinates, and distinct components are orthogonal. Write \(e_{ij}=\mathbb Eh_{ij}(X_i,X_j)^2\) and \(a_i=\mathbb Eu_i(X_i)^2\). Thus
\[
V=\sum_i a_i+\sum_{i<j}e_{ij},\qquad
I_i=a_i+\sum_{j\ne i}e_{\min(i,j),\max(i,j)}.\tag{2}
\]
In particular \(a_i\le M\).

### Lemma 1 — direct, pointwise, alphabet-free section budget

For fixed \(x\) set
\[
R_i(x)=\max_{a\in\Omega_i}f(x_{-i},a)-\min_{a\in\Omega_i}f(x_{-i},a).
\]
We claim \(\sum_i R_i(x)\le16B\) for every \(x\). Choose arbitrary target symbols \(y_i\). Form \(q_{x,y}(t)=\mathbb E_{\xi}f(z(\xi))\), where the switch indicators \(\xi_i\) are independent Bernoulli\((t)\) and \(z_i(\xi)=y_i\) for \(\xi_i=1\), otherwise \(x_i\). The representation (1) makes \(q\) a polynomial of degree at most two in \(t\). As an expectation of values of \(f\), \(|q(t)|\le B\) for \(0\le t\le1\), and
\[
q'(0)=\sum_i[f(x_{-i},y_i)-f(x)],\quad
q'(0)=-3q(0)+4q(1/2)-q(1).
\]
Therefore \(|q'(0)|\le8B\). Choosing \(y_i\) to maximize \(f(x_{-i},\cdot)\) gives \(\sum_i(\max_a f(x_{-i},a)-f(x))\le8B\). Choosing the minima gives \(\sum_i(f(x)-\min_a f(x_{-i},a))\le8B\). Adding establishes the claim.

Let \(H_i=L^2(\mu_i;\mathbb R)\) and the genuinely centered section
\[
U_i(\cdot;X_{-i})=f(X_{-i},\cdot)-\mathbb E_i f(X_{-i},\cdot)\in H_i.
\]
A random variable supported in an interval of length \(R\) has standard deviation at most \(R/2\) (Popoviciu's inequality). Consequently, *pointwise in the other coordinates* and hence after averaging,
\[
\boxed{\sum_i\mathbb E\|U_i\|_{H_i}\le8B.}\tag{3}
\]
This argument constructs its own source-function oscillation inequality; it does not import a row certificate or assume the desired influence bound.

### Lemma 2 — additive energy budget

Let \(P_t=\prod_i[\mathbb E_i+t(\mathrm{Id}-\mathbb E_i)]\). Each factor is a Markov averaging operator for \(0\le t\le1\), so \(|P_tf(x)|\le B\) pointwise. By (1),
\[
P_tf(x)=m+t\sum_i u_i(x_i)+t^2\sum_{i<j}h_{ij}(x_i,x_j).
\]
The interpolation identity
\[
-2P_0f(x)+4P_{1/2}f(x)-P_1f(x)=m+\sum_i u_i(x_i)=:g(x)
\]
gives \(|g(x)|\le7B\). Because the \(u_i\) depend on disjoint coordinates, \(\operatorname{range}(g)=\sum_i\operatorname{range}(u_i)\le14B\). Applying the variance–range inequality to each \(u_i\),
\[
\boxed{\sum_i\|u_i\|_2\le7B},\qquad
\boxed{\sum_i a_i\le7B\sqrt M}.\tag{4}
\]
No row theorem, selector, or moment premise is used.

### Lemma 3 — parity coupling bounds every disjoint matching

For an edge \(e=\{i,j\}\), put \(b_{ij}=\max_{a,b}|h_{ij}(a,b)|\). Fix \((a,b)\) where this absolute maximum is attained. Separate centering gives
\[
h_{ij}(a,b)=\mathbb E_{a',b'}\left[h_{ij}(a,b)+h_{ij}(a',b')-h_{ij}(a',b)-h_{ij}(a,b')\right].
\]
Hence some rectangle \((a,a';b,b')\) has an oriented mixed difference of magnitude at least \(b_{ij}\). Choose its orientation so the even-minus-odd signed difference \(\Delta_{ij}\) is positive.

For *any matching* \(F\) of pairwise vertex-disjoint edges, construct two probability distributions on full inputs: independently in each matched block, the even distribution assigns probability \(1/2\) to \((a,b),(a',b')\), and the odd distribution assigns probability \(1/2\) to \((a,b'),(a',b)\), reversing parity for negative rectangle orientation. Fix all unmatched coordinates arbitrarily. Both distributions have identical **one-coordinate marginals**, and different matched blocks are independent under both. Therefore all constant, singleton and off-matching quadratic terms in (1) have the same expectations under the two distributions; only the interactions on \(F\) survive. Their expectation difference is
\[
\mathbb E_{\mathrm{even}}f-\mathbb E_{\mathrm{odd}}f
=\frac12\sum_{\{i,j\}\in F}\Delta_{ij}\ge\frac12\sum_{\{i,j\}\in F}b_{ij}.
\]
Both expectations belong to \([-B,B]\), so
\[
\boxed{\sum_{\{i,j\}\in F}b_{ij}\le4B\quad\text{for every matching }F.}\tag{5}
\]
This is an actual global coupling of \(f\), not a bound assumed of abstract pair coefficients.

### Lemma 4 — greedy endpoint assignment

On the weighted graph of nonzero pairs, repeatedly choose a currently largest-weight edge, add it to a matching, and delete it and all incident edges. Every deleted edge meets the selected edge that removed it, with weight at least its own. Assign each deleted edge to one of its endpoints in that selected edge. Let \(S_i\) be the set of neighbors \(j\) whose interaction \(\{i,j\}\) was assigned to endpoint \(i\). If \(i\) is an endpoint of a selected matching edge \(e\), put \(c_i=b_e\); otherwise \(c_i=0\). Then each interaction is assigned **exactly once**, and
\[
b_{ij}\le c_i\ (j\in S_i),\quad
\boxed{\sum_i c_i=2\sum_{e\in F}b_e\le8B}.\tag{6}
\]

### Lemma 5 — actual independent Hilbert increments and their moment bound

For each \(i\) form, in the Hilbert space \(H_i\),
\[
Y_{ij}(\cdot;X_j)=h_{ij}(\cdot,X_j),\quad
Z_i=\sum_{j\in S_i}Y_{ij},\quad
s_i=\mathbb E\|Z_i\|_{H_i}^2,\quad z_i=\mathbb E\|Z_i\|_{H_i}.
\]
The variables \(Y_{ij}\) for fixed \(i\) and distinct \(j\) are independent and centered, and \(\|Y_{ij}\|_{H_i}\le b_{ij}\le c_i\). Orthogonality yields
\[
s_i=\sum_{j\in S_i} e_{ij}\le I_i\le M,
\qquad \boxed{\sum_i s_i=\sum_{i<j}e_{ij}}.\tag{7}
\]
Here \(e_{ij}\) uses the unordered pair, regardless of which endpoint owns it. Conditioning the *actual source section* \(U_i\) on the sigma-field generated by \(\{X_j:j\in S_i\}\), the canonical decomposition gives
\[
\mathbb E[U_i\mid(X_j)_{j\in S_i}]=u_i+Z_i.
\]
Hilbert-norm Jensen and the triangle inequality give
\[
z_i\le\mathbb E\|U_i\|_{H_i}+\|u_i\|_2,
\quad\boxed{\sum_i z_i\le8B+7B=15B}.\tag{8}
\]

We prove the moment inequality rather than assume it. For independent centered Hilbert vectors \(Y_j\), write \(v_j=\mathbb E\|Y_j\|^2\). Expanding \(\|\sum Y_j\|^4\), independence and centering cancel every term that has a singly occurring index, leaving
\[
\mathbb E\Big\|\sum_jY_j\Big\|^4
=\sum_j\mathbb E\|Y_j\|^4
+2\sum_{j<k}v_jv_k
+4\sum_{j<k}\mathbb E\langle Y_j,Y_k\rangle^2.
\]
By Cauchy–Schwarz and independence the last summand is at most \(4\sum_{j<k}v_jv_k\). Since \(\|Y_j\|\le c_i\), \(\mathbb E\|Y_j\|^4\le c_i^2v_j\). Therefore, with \(Q_i=\mathbb E\|Z_i\|^4\),
\[
\boxed{Q_i\le c_i^2s_i+3s_i^2}.\tag{9}
\]
This covers empty sums and the case \(s_i=0\).

### Lemma 6 — Hölder closes the endpoint, without hidden assumptions

Interpolation of the \(L^1,L^2,L^4\) norms of the nonnegative variable \(\|Z_i\|\) gives
\[
s_i^3\le z_i^2Q_i.
\]
If \(s_i>0\), divide by \(s_i\), use (9), then take nonnegative square roots:
\[
s_i\le z_i\sqrt{c_i^2+3s_i}\le c_i z_i+\sqrt{3M}\,z_i.
\]
It is trivial for \(s_i=0\). Jensen/Cauchy–Schwarz also gives \(z_i\le\sqrt{s_i}\le\sqrt M\). Hence by (6) and (8),
\[
\sum_i s_i\le\sum_i c_i z_i+\sqrt{3M}\sum_i z_i
\le8B\sqrt M+15\sqrt3B\sqrt M.\tag{10}
\]
Finally, (2), (4), (7), (10) imply
\[
V=\sum_i a_i+\sum_i s_i
\le[7+8+15\sqrt3]B\sqrt M
=15(1+\sqrt3)B\sqrt M.
\]
Squaring proves (T), for every finite full-support product with actual source-function components. **Every premise used above follows from the stated hypotheses.** QED (finite case).

## Extension to arbitrary measurable coordinate alphabets and index sets

For a general product probability space, let \(\mathcal A\) range over finite *rectangular cylinder partitions*: choose finitely many coordinates and a finite measurable partition of each, and reveal just those partition labels. Put \(f_{\mathcal A}=\mathbb E[f\mid\mathcal A]\). Then:

1. \(f_{\mathcal A}\) is a bounded function of finitely many finite-alphabet independent partition labels, with \(\|f_{\mathcal A}\|_\infty\le B\).
2. Product conditional expectations preserve the closed degree-\(\le2\) Hoeffding subspace: projecting any component supported on at most two original coordinates produces a component supported on at most those same two labels (or zero), and continuity extends this to the closed span.
3. For each observed coordinate \(i\), the cylinder projection \(P_{\mathcal A}\) commutes with the original coordinate mean operator \(E_i\); hence \(I_i(f_{\mathcal A})=\|(\mathrm{Id}-E_i)P_{\mathcal A}f\|_2^2\le I_i(f)\). For omitted coordinates the influence of \(f_{\mathcal A}\) is zero. Therefore \(\max_i I_i(f_{\mathcal A})\le M\).
4. Finite rectangular cylinder functions are dense in \(L^2\) of the product sigma algebra. On the completed sigma algebra they remain dense modulo null sets. The directed family of conditional expectations \(P_{\mathcal A}f\) consequently converges to \(f\) in \(L^2\), so \(\operatorname{Var}(f_{\mathcal A})\to V\).

Apply the finite theorem to each \(f_{\mathcal A}\), obtaining \(\operatorname{Var}(f_{\mathcal A})\le15(1+\sqrt3)B\sqrt M\), and pass to the limit. This proves (T) on arbitrary independent product laws, including nonatomic and infinitely many coordinates, under the stated closed-subspace definition of degree.

**Boundary:** Independence of the input coordinates is essential. This is a static resampling-influence theorem, not an adaptive query algorithm, a quantum transfer, or a result for arbitrary correlated joint laws.

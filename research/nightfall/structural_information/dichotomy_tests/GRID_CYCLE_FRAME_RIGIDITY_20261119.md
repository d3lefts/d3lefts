## 1. General rigidity lemma (new scoped reduction; no novelty verdict)

Let \(R=(r_i)_{i\in E}\) and \(B=(b_j)_{j\in J}\) be finite families of **distinct nonzero** vectors in a vector space over \(\mathbb F_2\). Assume \(B\) is a \((c,c',h)\)-frame for \(R\), in the precise original paper sense:

- each \(r_i\) is an XOR of at most \(c\) elements of \(B\);
- each \(b_j\) is an XOR of at most \(c'\) elements of \(R\);
- no nonempty XOR of at most \(h\) distinct elements of \(B\) vanishes.

Assume the *entire space of linear dependencies among the indexed rows \(R\)* is generated by relations involving at most \(\ell\) rows. Then:

> **Lemma G (short-circuit rigidity).** If
> \[
> \boxed{h\ge\max\{\ell c,\,cc'+1\},}
> \]
> the entire family \(B\) is linearly independent (not merely \(h\)-wise independent). Consequently \(B\) is a basis of \(\operatorname{span}(R)\).

### Proof

Let \(\partial_R:\mathbb F_2^E\to W\) and \(\partial_B:\mathbb F_2^J\to W\) be the linear row-value maps, where \(W=\operatorname{span}(R)=\operatorname{span}(B)\). Choose forward representations and extend them linearly to \(A:\mathbb F_2^E\to\mathbb F_2^J\), so \(\partial_B A=\partial_R\) and every column of \(A\) has Hamming weight \(\le c\).

For any generating dependency \(z\in\ker\partial_R\) of weight \(\le\ell\), \(Az\in\ker\partial_B\) has weight \(\le\ell c\le h\). The frame's girth condition forces \(Az=0\). By linearity and the generation hypothesis, \(A\) annihilates *every* element of \(\ker\partial_R\). It therefore factors as \(\bar A:W\to\mathbb F_2^J\), with \(A=\bar A\partial_R\) and \(\partial_B\bar A=\operatorname{id}_W\).

For each \(b_j\), choose a reverse representation \(b_j=\partial_R z_j\) of weight \(\le c'\). Then \(\bar A b_j=Az_j\) has weight \(\le cc'\). Both \(\bar A b_j\) and the standard basis vector \(e_j\) map to \(b_j\) under \(\partial_B\). Their sum lies in \(\ker\partial_B\) and has weight \(\le cc'+1\le h\). Hence \(\bar A b_j=e_j\). Thus \(\bar A\partial_B=\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb F_2^J}\), proving \(\partial_B\) injective, and \(B\) a genuine basis. QED.

**Gate necessity / negative controls.** A six-cycle with \(B=R\), \(c=c'=1\), has an actual six-term circuit, so setting \(h=5<\ell c=6\) does not force a basis. Conversely, with independent \(R=\{e_1,e_2,e_3\}\) and \(B=\{e_1,e_2,e_3,e_1+e_2+e_3\}\), the frame has \(c=1,c'=3\), girth four, and \(h=3<cc'+1=4\): the reverse-locality gate also cannot be omitted.

## 2. The grid is an exact stress test

Let \(G=P_a\square P_b\), \(2\le a\le b\), with \(ab\) vertices. Define the row family
\[
R_G=\{e_u+e_v: uv\in E(G)\}\subseteq\mathbb F_2^{ab}.
\]

**Proposition G1 (small short-sum subspaces).** For every integer \(r\ge0\),
\[
\boxed{N_r(R_G)\le 2r.}
\]
Indeed, each sum of \(\le r\) edge rows has Hamming weight \(\le2r\). Every \(k\)-dimensional binary subspace contains a vector of Hamming weight at least \(k\): restrict to a full-rank set of \(k\) coordinate columns and choose the vector with ones in all \(k\) pivot positions. Therefore a \(k\)-subspace in \(r\cdot R_G\) requires \(k\le2r\). No density or doubling assumption is used.

**Proposition G2 (all row circuits generated by squares).** \(\ker\partial_{R_G}\) is the binary cycle space of the grid, generated by the elementary face boundaries of weight four. For a direct check, the grid is connected so \(\operatorname{rank}(R_G)=ab-1\); hence
\[
\dim\ker\partial_{R_G}=|E|-(ab-1)=(a-1)(b-1),
\]
and its \((a-1)(b-1)\) face-square cycles are independent (successively peel boundary edges), giving a basis. Thus Lemma G applies with \(\ell=4\).

Consequently, **every** \((c,c',h)\)-frame for grid rows with \(h\ge\max(4c,cc'+1)\) must already be a basis. Short overlapping dependencies cannot persist in a high-girth frame with both maps sufficiently local.

## 3. Exact c'=1 barrier (known tree-spanner theorem enters)

For \(c'=1\), every nonzero frame element is an **original graph edge**. The source's smoothing threshold \(h(2cT,\varepsilon,\delta)\) is at least \(4c\) for its usual \(T\ge1\) regime; otherwise retain \(h\ge4c\) as an explicit hypothesis. Lemma G implies every admissible \(B\subseteq R_G\) is independent and spans the grid edge space, hence is **a spanning tree**. Its forward cost \(c\) is exactly the maximum length of the unique tree path between endpoints of a grid edge.

The well-studied minimum-max-stretch spanning-tree theorem gives, for rectangular grids,
\[
\boxed{\min_{T_{\mathrm{tree}}\subseteq G}\max_{uv\in E(G)}
d_{T_{\mathrm{tree}}}(u,v)=2\lfloor a/2\rfloor+1.}
\]
Reference: L. Lin and Y. Lin, *The Minimum Stretch Spanning Tree Problem for Typical Graphs*, Acta Mathematicae Applicatae Sinica 37 (2021), Theorem 4.1, https://doi.org/10.1007/s10255-021-1028-6. See also https://www.stjohns.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/%2377_167081%20Spanning%20Tree%20Congestion%2C%20Stretch%20And%20Other%20Parameters%20Of%20The%20Graph.pdf .

Therefore for the **restricted class of frames with reverse cost \(c'=1\)** and the explicit girth condition above, the minimum frame cost is precisely \(2\lfloor a/2\rfloor+1\), growing without bound for square grids, while \(N_r(R_G)\le2r\) remains independent of side length. This is a **negative result for the original-row-only frame strategy**, NOT a counterexample to unrestricted Conjecture E. A frame with \(c'>1\) may bypass the rigidity gate when \(cc'+1>h(2cT)\), and its optimal cost has not been bounded here. Neither this statement nor the exact tree-stretch formula is claimed as new graph theory.

## 4. Consequences for Claude's merging/direct-sum theorem

- Claude's paper-level balanced-direct-sum theorem can be correct and still miss grids: their square dependencies overlap at every face, and the cycle space is not a direct sum of bounded-size edge blocks.
- The naive inference **\(N_r=O(r)\Rightarrow\) a cheap basis from original rows** is false even on grids, by the established grid tree-stretch theorem.
- Any proposed extension that only performs local row deletion/replacement with \(c'=1\) cannot prove Conjecture E on this test family. It must genuinely exploit reverse combinations \(c'>1\), quotient/symbol merging, or a relaxation of the frame invariant.
- **Decisive remaining test:** determine whether a family of frames with *both* \(c,c'\) bounded independent of \(a\) can exist on the \(a\times a\) grid with girth \(h(2cT)\). If not, this would refute unrestricted Conjecture E, but the present proof does not decide this. Investigate local sparse basis factorizations and grid separator/cycle-space arguments.

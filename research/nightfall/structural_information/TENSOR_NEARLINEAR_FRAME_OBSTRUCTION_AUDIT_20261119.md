## General relation-code lemma (author-side proof)

Let `R={r_1,...,r_n}⊂F₂^m` span a d-dimensional space. Define the row-synthesis map `π:F₂^n→span(R)`. Suppose `ker π` is generated by dependencies of size at most t. Define the **dual evaluation distance**

    Δ(R) = min_{nonzero ell ∈ span(R)^*} #{i : ell(r_i)=1}.

For any `(c,c',h)` frame with `h≥tc`, choose binary coefficient words `a_i∈F₂^B`, `|a_i|≤c`, with `Σ_b a_i(b)b=r_i`. Any generating row dependency of length≤t maps to a frame dependency of length≤tc≤h and therefore must become the ZERO coefficient vector by girth. Hence the coefficient-word matrix descends to an injective linear section `s:span(R)→F₂^B`.

Each column of `s` is a linear functional on `span(R)`. Because s is injective, at least d coordinate functionals are nonzero. Every nonzero functional evaluates to 1 on at least Δ(R) original rows. Counting all 1s in the coefficient matrix both ways gives

    n*c ≥ d*Δ(R),    so c*c' ≥ c ≥ d*Δ(R)/n.             (A)

This applies to **all** frames B, synthetic or redundant, without assumptions on c' beyond c'≥1. This is the reusable actual obstruction, not a basis-only lower bound.

## Tensor-simplex strengthening: fixed T and exponent arbitrarily close to 1

For fixed integer `q≥2`, put `D=F₂^q\{0}` and let

    R(q,L) = {v₁⊗⋯⊗v_L : each v_i∈D} ⊂ (F₂^q)^{⊗L}.

Then `m=q^L` secret bits, `n=(2^q−1)^L` distinct rows, and `rank R=m`.

**(1) Relations generated by triples independent of q.** For every pair of *distinct* nonzero `u,v∈D`, `u+v∈D` and `e_u+e_v+e_{u+v}` is a weight-three generator of the relation kernel of the one-factor map `F₂^D→F₂^q`. To prove generation, pick a standard coordinate basis. Any `e_w` of Hamming weight≥2 reduces modulo these triple relations to `e_{e_i}+e_{w+e_i}`; induct until a sum of unit basis symbols remains. Thus the quotient has dimension≤q, while the actual image has dimension q. Hence all one-factor relations are generated by triples.

For the tensor map, split `F₂^D=kerπ₁⊕Z`, with `dim Z=q`. Expanding `(kerπ₁⊕Z)^{⊗L}` shows that every tensor-kernel term contains at least one factor from `kerπ₁`; it is therefore generated by **three-term** original-row relations in that one position, with every other factor a basis symbol. This works for EVERY q≥2 and L≥1. Thus t=3 in (A), not q+1.

**(2) Exact dual distance.** In one factor, every nonzero linear functional on `F₂^q` has exactly `2^{q−1}` ones among nonzero vectors D, giving the binary simplex code parameters `[2^q−1,q,2^{q−1}]`. The dual evaluation code of `R(q,L)` is its L-fold tensor product. The standard tensor-distance induction gives

    Δ(R(q,L)) = (2^{q−1})^L.

Apply (A), for `h≥3c`:

    Λ_T(R(q,L)) ≥ [q·2^{q−1}/(2^q−1)]^L.                  (B)

The source `h(2cT)` satisfies `h(2cT)≥3c` for fixed `T≥1` and fixed usual `ε,δ`, as audited in #956/#957.

**(3) At-most-r sums, no sumset overcount error.** `r·R` contains sums of **at most** r original rows, hence

    |r·R| ≤ (n+1)^r
    N_r(R(q,L)) ≤ r log₂((2^q−1)^L+1) ≤ r(qL+1)
               = O_q(r log m).                             (C)

Do NOT use the invalid `|r·R|≤n^r` from the old TOWERS draft.

**(4) Near-maximal exponent.** Put `q=2^s` with fixed integer s≥2. Write `m=q^L` and define

    α_q = log_q(q·2^{q−1}/(2^q−1))
        = 1−1/s−log_q(1−2^{−q}) > 1−1/s.

Then (B) says `Λ_T ≥ m^{α_q}`. For **every η>0** choose s>1/η once and let L→∞:

    N_r(R(q,L)) = O_η(r log m),     Λ_T(R(q,L)) ≥ m^{1−η}.    (D)

The rows contain a coordinate tensor basis, so the trivial basis construction gives `Λ_T≤m`. Consequently (D) is **arbitrarily close to the maximal general frame-cost exponent**, and contradicts Conjecture E's `poly(N_{a(T)},T)` bound at fixed T=1. The result holds even when frames may mix arbitrary tensor factors, use synthetic vectors, and select any c' at all.

This sharpens the quantitative implication of the independent parallel theorem and invalidates **the exact frame-form Conjecture E**. It does NOT establish a quantum query speedup nor refute the possibility of a different non-frame simulation characterized by `N_r`.

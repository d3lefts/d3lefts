## A. The theorem that settles the frame question

Let W_L=(F_2^2)^{tensor L}, of dimension m=2^L. Put D={u=(1,0),v=(0,1),w=u+v=(1,1)} and let

    R_L={d_1 tensor ... tensor d_L : each d_i in D}.

There are exactly n=3^L distinct nonzero rows; all span W_L. Suppose B is ANY (c,c',h)-frame of R_L as defined in LINEAR_DISTRIBUTIONS_20261119.md:

1. every r in R_L is a sum of at most c distinct elements of B;
2. every b in B is a sum of at most c' rows of R_L;
3. no nonempty subset of at most h of B has zero sum.

No assumption is made that B is independent, consists of product tensors, or is a subset of R_L. Assume c,c'>=1 and h>=3c. Then

    c >= 2^(2L)/3^L = (4/3)^L
    and so c c' >= (4/3)^L.                               (SC1)

Conversely the standard tensor-coordinate basis is contained in R_L, is independent, and gives a (2^L,1,h)-frame for EVERY h. Consequently

    (4/3)^L <= Lambda_T(R_L) <= 2^L                 (SC2)

for every T>=1 and every fixed epsilon,delta whose smoothing cutoff satisfies h(2cT)>=3c (in particular, epsilon=delta=1/4 in the recovered source).

This is an exponential lower bound on **unrestricted** high-girth frame cost, not a criticism of a natural product basis.

## B. General reusable lemma: relation generation × dual minimum distance

Let W be a d-dimensional binary vector space, R subset W a finite set of n distinct spanning nonzero rows. Let E=F_2^R and let pi:E->W send the standard generator indexed by r to r. Define

    d_star(R)= min_{nonzero linear ell: W->F_2}
                         #{r in R : ell(r)=1}.

Suppose ker(pi) is linearly generated by relations of weight <=t. Then EVERY (c,c',h)-frame for R with h>=tc obeys

    c >= d * d_star(R) / n.                         (GEN)

**Proof.** For each r in R choose one representation beta_r in F_2^B of Hamming weight <=c, with pi_B(beta_r)=r, where pi_B sends the free B-vector to the sum of its vectors in W. Extend beta linearly from E to F_2^B. For any generating relation z in ker pi of weight <=t, beta(z) is a combination of at most tc frame elements and pi_B beta(z)=0. Girth h>=tc forces beta(z)=0. Since these relations generate ker pi, beta kills the entire kernel and factors through a linear map sigma:W->F_2^B. Moreover pi_B sigma=id_W, so sigma is injective and has rank d.

For each b in B, the b-coordinate of sigma is a linear functional ell_b on W. At least d such functionals are nonzero, otherwise sigma would have rank <d. Each nonzero ell_b is 1 on at least d_star(R) rows. Double count {(r,b): b belongs to beta_r}: its size is at least d*d_star(R), and at most n*c. This proves GEN. QED.

The second frame condition (reverse row cost c') is used only for c'>=1. Even an arbitrarily large synthetic B cannot evade this obstruction; the *short relation generators* force coherent linear representations.

## C. Independently verify the two subcube invariants

**C1. Local triangles generate ALL linear dependencies.** Let U=F_2^D, a 3-dimensional free space, and q:U->F_2^2 send basis symbols u,v,w to those vectors. Then ker q is spanned by e_u+e_v+e_w. Under canonical tensor identifications, the row synthesis map pi:E=U^{tensor L}->W_L=(F_2^2)^{tensor L} is q^{tensor L}. Choose a direct-sum splitting U=K+Z, with K=ker q and dim Z=2. Expanding (K+Z)^{tensor L}, the sole factor Z^{tensor L} maps isomorphically onto W_L; every other summand (containing at least one K factor) lies in ker pi. Each such term is a linear combination of elementary tensors with one factor equal to e_u+e_v+e_w and every other factor an ordinary member of the basis {e_u,e_v,e_w}. Those are exactly the three-term relations on the row family obtained by replacing u,v,w in one tensor position while holding other positions fixed. Hence ker pi is generated by weight-3 relations, for every L.

Equivalently the span of local-triangle relations has rank 3^L-2^L. This is checked exactly through L=5 by an independently implemented bit-matrix rank routine.

**C2. The dual row-evaluation code has minimum weight exactly 2^L.** For nonzero ell:W_L->F_2 consider the codeword (ell(r)) over r in R_L. For L=1 every nonzero linear functional is 1 on exactly two of u,v,w. Induct on L: write ell in terms of its two contractions ell_u and ell_v on the last L-1 factors, with ell_w=ell_u+ell_v. If ell is nonzero, at least one contraction is nonzero, and among ell_u,ell_v,ell_w at least two are nonzero. By induction, each such slice contributes at least 2^(L-1) nonzero evaluations; total >=2^L. Equality occurs for a pure tensor of nonzero functionals, whose codeword is a product of L weight-two codewords. Therefore d_star(R_L)=2^L. This is the usual minimum-distance multiplication for the L-fold tensor power of the binary [3,2,2] simplex code; the argument here is self-contained. The code-theory mechanism is prior art, not independently novel.

Substitute d=2^L, n=3^L, t=3 into GEN to obtain SC1.

## D. Formal contradiction to Conjecture E

Conjecture E (LINEAR_NECESSITY_20261119.md) states that there exist universal polynomials a,b such that for every row family R and every T,

    N_{a(T)}(R) <= k  =>  Lambda_T(R) <= b(k,T),

where N_r is the maximum dimension of V subset r·R and r·R denotes sums of AT MOST r original rows.

For any positive integer r, there are at most (|R|+1)^r sums of at most r rows, using a formal zero-padding symbol. Therefore

    N_r(R_L) <= r log_2(3^L+1) = O(r L).            (SC3)

Fix T=1 and fixed epsilon=delta=1/4. Then r=a(1) is an absolute constant (or if zero, N_0=0). The source's h(2cT)=2 k_phi max(2cT,D) satisfies h>=4 k_phi c T >=3c, with k_phi>=1. Thus SC1 forces Lambda_1(R_L)>=(4/3)^L, while SC3 gives N_{a(1)}(R_L)=O(L). For sufficiently large L, (4/3)^L dominates EVERY fixed polynomial in L. This contradicts the predicted polynomial b(N_{a(1)},1). No choice of universal polynomials a,b can satisfy Conjecture E.

**Verdict:** The exact FRAME dichotomy Conjecture E is FALSE, author-side proof complete, pending nonauthor review. This does not negate any standalone Theorem A/B/C or a possible two-sided characterization of quantum query advantage after broadening the simulation operator (e.g. non-frame tensor recursion). The previous conclusion "the subcube family might defeat the frame formulation" is now a theorem.

**Source correction:** TOWERS currently writes |r·R| <= 3^(Lr). This is false already at r=1 because sums of at most one row include zero, giving |1·R|=3^L+1. Replace by |r·R| <= (3^L+1)^r, which is sufficient for O(rL).

## E. Adversarial checks and honest remaining problems

The isolated standard-library checker scripts/check_subcube_frame_refutation.py independently verifies:

- rank(R_L)=2^L and rank(all local triangles)=3^L-2^L for L=1..5;
- the exact 2^L minimum dual-evaluation weight by Gray-code enumeration for L=1..4 (all 2^(2^L)-1 nonzero functionals);
- the counting bound and arithmetic for the frame cost, with zero and repeated-row boundaries explicitly excluded as in the source definition.

**These checks do not themselves certify all L**; the two symbolic tensor proofs above do. The finite routine uses integer bit XOR, not floating point, randomness or solver timeout. A separate adversarial reviewer should verify: (1) the kernel-generation splitting, (2) the frame girth assumption applies to symmetric-difference images, (3) the product code minimum-distance induction, (4) quantifiers in E including smoothing and at-most-r vs exactly-r conventions, (5) whether the source's definition permits arbitrary synthetic B. No self-certification, no Lean theorem asserted.

**What the family does NOT settle:** whether every T-query quantum acceptance observable on the subcube-parity source can be classically simulated with poly(T,L) observed row queries, or whether a recursive quantum task yields superpolynomial advantage. It settles the narrower, important question that *frames cannot deliver such a simulation*. A claim of polynomial-time circuit simulation is not made. The next operator family is tensor-recursive/non-frame simulation (or a genuine quantum lower bound), not another frame-basis search.

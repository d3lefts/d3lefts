## Objective and source contract

Prove a graph family with an unbounded number of interacting-looking short circuits and **classical simulation depth independent of graph size**, with explicit oracle costs. The main existing theorem is Theorem F in `MERGING_DIRECT_SUMS_20261119.md`, conditional on Theorems A and the stated alphabet-independent product-space simulator. Check dependencies without promoting a relative result to an unconditional quantum speedup classification.

- Input: an arbitrary finite simple **cactus graph** `G=(V,E)` (any two simple cycles share at most one vertex); choose independent uniform vertex secrets `s_v in F_2` and expose the edge bits `x_{uv}=s_u+s_v` for `uv in E`.
- Oracle: an algorithm can query a specified **edge parity** `x_e`, not a vertex secret `s_v` or arbitrary parity for free. Query indices and the whole graph are known.
- Product theorem: for a `t`-query quantum algorithm on a product of arbitrary finite alphabets, the inherited simulator has depth `D_prod(t,epsilon,delta)=poly(t,1/epsilon,1/delta)` independent of the number and sizes of the alphabets. This is an explicit external premise, not reproved here.
- Strongest reference checked for this transfer: Theorem F and Theorem A in the active branch, plus the standard graphic-cycle-space fact that every binary dependence of edge-incidence vectors is an Eulerian subgraph. Cactus graphic-matroid blocks are a standard direct-sum phenomenon; no novelty is claimed for that decomposition.

## Proposition (uniform cactus simulation; conditional corollary)

Write `h(t,epsilon,delta)` for the precise matching-order cutoff in Theorem A, and set `H=max(1,h(2T,epsilon,delta))`. For **every** cactus graph `G` and every `T>=1`, the distribution `nu_G` of its edge parities has an `(epsilon,delta)` classical simulation for any `T`-query quantum acceptance probability with depth at most

    H * D_prod(2T, epsilon_0, delta_0),

up to the fixed smoothing/error-budget factors of Theorem A. In particular the depth is `poly(T,1/epsilon,1/delta)`, with **no dependence on |V|, |E| or the number of cycles**. The same statement extends to disconnected cactus graphs.

More precisely, let `ell_1,...,ell_b` be the lengths of the cycles. The classical reverse-lookup cost of the representation below is

    c' = max(1, max {ell_j-1 : ell_j <= H}),

with an empty maximum interpreted as zero. Therefore `c'<=H`. It is not necessary to merge long cycles. For the branch's Theorem F, each cycle block is balanced with `d=ell` and `rho=floor(ell/2)`, and each bridge is an independent singleton.

### Proof

1. **Row-matroid direct sum.** Associate to an edge `uv` its incidence row `g_{uv}=e_u+e_v` in `F_2^V`. The rows sum to zero over an edge subset exactly when that subset has even degree at every vertex. Every nonempty even-degree edge subset is a union of simple cycles. In a cactus, simple cycles are edge-disjoint and contained in unique cycle blocks. Therefore the entire dependency space is generated by the all-edge relation of each cycle, and there is *no* dependency coupling different blocks. Bridges belong to no dependency. Thus `rank(R)=sum_blocks rank(R_j)`, and the joint edge-parity law is the product of its block laws, even when cycle blocks share articulation vertices.
2. **Exact cycle parameters.** For a cycle of length `ell`, its `ell` incidence rows have precisely one nonzero dependency, the sum of all `ell` edges. Hence `d(R_j)=ell` and `rank(R_j)=ell-1`. Every parity vector in its span has two edge-subset representations, complementary under the full cycle, so its minimum row weight is `min(w,ell-w)`; maximizing over words gives `rho(R_j)=floor(ell/2)`. A bridge is one free uniform bit (`d=infinity`).
3. **Explicit mixed-alphabet representation.** For each *short* cycle `ell<=H`, replace its entire edge tuple by one uniform alphabet symbol `sigma_j=(x_{e_1},...,x_{e_{ell-1}}) in F_2^{ell-1}`, using any all-but-one selection. The last edge parity is the XOR of the others, so any edge query in this block can be simulated coherently by querying **one symbol** (compute/query/uncompute overhead at most two symbol queries). Classically, reading the symbol costs exactly `ell-1<=H` original edge reads. Retain *all* raw edge bits of cycles `ell>H` as separate coordinates; retain bridge bits as separate coordinates.
4. **High-order independence, independent of block count.** Different cycle/bridge blocks are independent by step 1; each merged short-cycle symbol is uniform; each long cycle has no dependence among `<=H` edge bits because its unique dependency has length `ell>H`. Any set of at most `H` mixed-alphabet coordinates is therefore distributed exactly as under the full product of the corresponding marginals. This is the *exact* hypothesis needed for Theorem A, not approximate independence and not a claim that the whole representation is product.
5. **Simulation and reverse access.** Compile the original `T`-query algorithm into a `<=2T`-query algorithm on the representation. Apply Theorem A and the source product simulator to obtain a depth-`D_prod` classical tree on representation coordinates. Every queried merged symbol costs `<=H` edge reads, and every retained edge coordinate costs one. Thus the original edge-query depth is `<=H D_prod` with the source error constants and target smoothing budget, independent of graph size. QED, conditional on the cited product simulation and Theorem A.

**Quantifiers matter.** `H` depends on `T,epsilon,delta`, not on the graph. It may be much larger than `T`; for a long cycle `ell>H`, the unmerged edge bits are `H`-wise independent. Short-cycle compression is legal only because the product simulator has no alphabet-size dependence.

## Where this helps Claude's Conjecture E program

- It turns Theorem F's *abstract* direct-sum hypothesis into a **graph-theoretically recognizable class with an explicit exact decomposition**, including cycles sharing actual secret coordinates.
- It proves that arbitrarily many small cycles, and arbitrarily many long cycles, do **not** create superpolynomial-in-`T` classical simulation costs. A graph counterexample surface must therefore have nontrivial interaction **within a large biconnected block**, not just many local cycles glued at cut vertices.
- This does **not** prove that every graph-edge row set has a cheap frame. The square grid has many overlapping cycles inside one biconnected block and remains a live falsification target. A small-sum subspace within `rR` of graph-edge rows has dimension at most `2r` by the pivot-word argument; a lower bound for unrestricted frame cost on growing grids is still missing.
- Theorem F's *two-sided advantage* direction remains conditional on its balanced-block hypotheses and the paper-level Theorem C. This note certifies only the combinatorial structure and the conditional simulation corollary.

Run:

The independent standard-library checker enumerates **every edge subset** of six finite cactus instances, confirms exactly `2^{#cycles}` dependencies, proves each zero-sum edge subset is a union of full cycles, computes every block covering radius by exact GF(2) BFS, confirms direct-sum rank, and exhaustively tests mixed-coordinate independence (via exact rank) for `H in {1,2,3,4,6}`. Frozen local replay: **6 cases, 1,620 mixed-coordinate subsets**, all passed. The instances include a long cycle, a bouquet, a cycle–bridge–cycle chain, four articulated triangles, a forest, and a lollipop. There is no randomness, numerical tolerance, or timeout. These tests check finite formulas; they cannot certify the universal proof or an external simulation theorem.

## Material correction to Theorem F's error-budget claim

As of this review, Theorem F stated

    H=h(2c0 T,epsilon,delta)=poly(c0 T,1/epsilon,log(1/delta)).

But `LINEAR_DISTRIBUTIONS_20261119.md` defines

    h = 2 k_phi max(2T,D),
    k_phi = ceil(2/(9 epsilon^4) * log(8/delta)),

where its general cited `D` is `poly(T,1/epsilon,1/delta)`, *not* known to be polynomial in `log(1/delta)` for all quantum algorithms and rounds. Therefore `H` and the resulting unconditional parameter dependency should be **poly(T,1/epsilon,1/delta)** (possibly with logarithmic factors), unless a stronger matching-scope product theorem is first separately certified. This does not alter the graph-size independence or the mechanism.

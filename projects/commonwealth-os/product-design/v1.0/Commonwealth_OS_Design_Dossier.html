Product charter and product requirements

Commonwealth OS / Product Design v1.0 — 15 November 2026
Status: Proposal / not institutionally authorized
Scope: Huntsville institutional pilot; mobile-first

Executive definition

Commonwealth OS is a proposed multi-institution democratic operating system that connects governing rules, proposals, amendments, deliberation, authorized voting, recorded outcomes, and implementation evidence. Living Voter is its private citizen-facing legislative-preference interface. Working surface names: Chamber (representatives), Steward (administrators), and Proof Passport (audit view). Names are provisional.

North-star user outcome: A person can see what their governing institution is deciding, understand the relevant text and consequences, express an informed preference without being steered, and later verify whether an authorized decision actually took effect.

Product thesis: The cross-product gap to investigate is decision-state discontinuity: absence of reliably transportable evidence linking rule/version → eligible participants → consultation/deliberation → authorized vote → valid outcome → implementation. This is a research hypothesis, not proof that all compared systems lack the feature.

Fixed founding requirements

  1. Mobile-first; high trust; futuristic but credible; accessible without a smartphone through institution-approved alternatives.
  2. Three preference choices: Support; Don't support; No preference. Keep Unknown / not assessed as a separate internal state.
  3. Adult municipal onboarding proceeds local ordinances first, federal law second. A 96-question research instrument is a budget, not a proven claim of complete legislative coverage: 1 open-priorities prompt, 40 local seed questions, 30 federal seed questions, up to 25 adaptive questions. State law is a possible subsequent module, not a barrier between local and federal stages.
  4. One question may map to multiple provisions only with reviewed semantic entailment and visible provenance. A response never silently creates a binding ballot.
  5. New proposals, substantive amendments, repeals, and integrations are versioned; substantive changes prompt targeted review; editorial integration without normative change should not generate spurious preference reversal.
  6. Personal political profiles and identifiable answers are private by default. Public-facing metrics are aggregate, labeled, and suppressed when they risk identification. No political microtargeting, persuasion optimization, resale, or covert secondary use.
  7. Pilot delegates and officials have a mobile legislative workspace: tasks, pending decisions, review/amend/debate/vote, embedded explainable AI plus a persistent assistant. AI never has a vote or independent authority.
  8. Human authority is grounded in each institution's charter/bylaws and applicable law. App features cannot confer authority they do not possess.
  9. One pilot, five non-interchangeable ordered stages: UAH → other Huntsville universities → Huntsville City Schools high schools → Huntsville HOAs → Huntsville City Council. Target a potential city-approved deployment by 2030. All dates subject to institution permission.
  10. Open-source intent and eventual broader adoption are strategic aspirations; no license or legal right to use personal pilot data for another purpose has been granted.

Scope and release boundaries

V0 / concept: Browser-only synthetic Living Voter demo; example laws, anonymous localStorage, no real identity, source ingestion, voting, security, or institutional authorization.

V1 / UAH: Staff-confirmed institution constitution and senate bylaws, agenda/case tracking, bill version diff, assigned representative tasks, meeting & deliberation record, rule-configured vote lifecycle, certificates marked provisional until audited. Can operate as recording/consultation only until actual procedures are authorized.

V2 / universities: Tenant isolation and multiple independent governing rulebooks, federated account integration, export/import, institutional rule tests.

V3 / schools: School-specific governance; child and student privacy review; no minors' federal political profiling; no adult municipality eligibility claims.

V4 / HOAs: Exact membership, unit vote weights, proxies, quorum, budgets, and covenant-specific restrictions; legal review per association.

V5 / Huntsville Council: Official municipal document ingestion, authorized representative workspace, advisory public dashboard first; binding government voting only under clear legal authorization and independent election-grade assurance.

Explicitly out of scope for early pilots: public office replacement, autonomous elected-official votes, binding municipal referenda, identity claims based solely on GPS/IP, unconditional law-coverage percentages, political influence campaigns, production cryptographic verifiability without external audit.

Product success measures (pre-register before pilots)

  • Corpus coverage: % of verified in-scope governing provisions ingested, with source citations and gaps.
  • Mapping precision: blinded human validation of answer→provision entailment; report abstention as well as error.
  • Citizen control: override success and time-to-correct a wrong inference.
  • Action continuity: % of decisions with complete rule→version→eligibility→ballot→outcome→implementation trail.
  • Authorized voting: share of sample decisions passing independently reproduced quorum, eligibility and tally checks.
  • Change latency: source update to correctly classified notification time; stratify by proposed/amended/repealed/editorial.
  • Accessibility: automated + manual WCAG 2.2 AA audit, assistive tech and no-smartphone testing.
  • Equity: compare completion/understanding across randomly recruited user groups; report nonresponse and coverage bias.
  • Privacy: zero unauthorized exposures in red-team tests; deletion/export request completion and logged failures.

Release gates

G0 Design and legal scoping completed; G1 source/corpus ground truth signed off; G2 independent mapping study; G3 rule tests, election-integrity design review and threat model; G4 operator authorization; G5 accessibility/security/privacy review; G6 staged deployment and audit. No test can substitute for lawful institutional approval.

Non-functional requirements

Encryption in transit and at rest, scoped authorization, secure key custody, immutable event log with independently verifiable hashes (hashes are necessary but not sufficient), audit export, recovery and backup, configurable retention, responsive touch targets, explicit status states, threat modeling, rate limiting, secure notifications, disaster recovery. Production SLOs and budgets must be set after user research and vendor estimates.

Ethical and civic boundaries

Publicly disclose any long-term research into representative automation. Participation must be voluntary, consent-specific, reversible, and unlinked to education or association penalties. AI summaries require source references, dissent/uncertainty display and correction mechanisms. Do not use participant political preference profiles for electioneering, differential outreach, profiling of minors, or unconsented training.

Unifying gap hypothesis: decision-state discontinuity

Existing categories often concentrate on one or more of: institutional document management, citizen engagement/deliberation, binding aggregation, cryptographic tally verification, or implementations. The research target is to establish a uniform, testable chain of meaning and authority when a document is changed, a rule is revised, membership shifts, or a decision produces effects:

authoritative source → applicable rules → provision/version → participant eligibility → expression or authorized vote → certified outcome → implementation event → change/re-elicitation.

The contribution is not “nobody supports this step.” Decidim and CONSUL already cover multiple steps. The proposed differentiator is verifiable continuity across institutional rules, legal document versions, personal preference provenance, and authorized effects; this remains to be implemented, evaluated and compared empirically.

Four explicit points of distinction

  1. Living preference ledger: provision-level source-grounded, continuously editable and private direct/inferred/unknown states; change notifications target stale answers.
  2. Authority portability: independent institutions instantiate different rulebooks without sharing governance entitlements.
  3. Democratic Assurance Proof Passport: each consequential decision packages its legal/charter authority, eligibility, quorum, text version, outcome, exceptions and effect record; a defined external checker can replay it.
  4. Separation of consultation and lawful voting: never count profile positions as ballots; publicly distinguish resident sample opinions from binding representative decisions.

Competitive validation plan

Choose a controlled case study: a proposal created, materially amended after a public comment, put to a vote with a changed membership roster, adopted, then incorporated into a new code edition. Test in target baseline systems where permitted. Compare provenance retention, authority proof completeness, human review steps, exception handling and total operating cost. Publish neutral feature-level findings with version/build IDs and vendor responses.

Information architecture, roles and journeys

Application topology

Commonwealth OS Shell: account and institution switcher, authenticated context, alerts, support/accessibility, status and consent.

  • Chamber (elected/appointed institution representatives): Today; Bills; Deliberation; Votes; Audit; AI assistant.
  • Living Voter (adult resident or member, scoped by institution): Home; Laws; Changes; Profile; interactive question path; source/attribution ledger.
  • Steward (clerk/admin): Institution; Rulebook; Roles & eligibility; Agendas; Documents; Publication; Security; Audit & incidents.
  • Public View (phase gated): records and aggregate participatory results only where legally publishable; never identifiable private profiles.

UI must visually pin the active institution, role and authority mode. A student senator in UAH cannot accidentally act as an HOA board member or a Huntsville resident.

Adult resident flow — local before federal

  1. Choose institution/jurisdiction; privacy explanation and data-use consent.
  2. Eligibility status: unverified preview / verified institution member / verified registered voter (only after authorized integration). Avoid treating identity as voter-roll qualification.
  3. Open priorities prompt (optional/skip allowed). Then up to 40 local policy seeds, each with Support / Don't support / No preference.
  4. User sees a transparent ledger: directly answered questions, provision-level inference candidates, unknowns and conflicts; can override a provision directly.
  5. After local block, offer federal profile: up to 30 federal seeds; up to 25 adaptive prompts total across instrument, budgeted for unresolved high-value ambiguities.
  6. Subsequent visits emphasize targeted change queue, not redoing the whole questionnaire. User can pause, resume, withdraw consent, export and erase.

Important: For 2027 university participants and school members, the operational institution dashboard is not the same as an official municipal registered-voter profile. School minors do not receive federal political profiling.

Representative workflow

Sign in → institutional role confirmed → task dashboard → open proposed act → authoritative text/version and prior changes → AI summary with citations → debate / propose amendment → check eligibility and voting window → confirm locked text → cast authorized vote (if approved) → audit receipt → outcome & implementation view.

Critical interrupts: source stale; rulebook changed; quorum rule unavailable; vote window expired; eligibility contested; amendment after lock; external system mismatch; trust boundary unavailable. These must visibly block binding acts rather than render a green success state.

Clerk workflow

Import source → reconcile version → set rules and roles → define agenda → make proposal public → authorize debate/voting under institution bylaws → run tally/verifier → publish disposition + evidence → track effective action.

Clerk corrections to sources use append-only superseding events with reasons; never erase the underlying evidence for an authorized result.

Notification taxonomy

Event Recipient What app does State
Draft introduced Subscribers / eligible participants Summary, source, direct input option New, no vote presumed
Substantive amendment Those whose relevant earlier views may no longer apply Reopen targeted questions, invalidate mapping when needed Stale until confirmed
Repeal Affected participants Ask about repeal proposal, preserve prior law position Proposal separate from existing-law preference

States and copy rules

  • Preference: support, oppose, neutral, unknown (no answer), conflict (evidence inconsistent), stale (source changed).
  • Basis: direct-provision, direct-topic, inferred-reviewed, inferred-unverified, not-assessed.
  • Vote: not-open, open, cast-pending, cast-receipted, tally-pending, certified, challenged, void.
  • Authority: advisory, institution-authorized, lawful-government, unknown. Never display unverified authority as certified.

Low-bandwidth / non-smartphone path

Provide accessible web, assisted kiosk or paper-backed channels subject to authority and auditing; manual transcription must be independently reconciled. Do not exclude participants lacking smartphones.

Experience goals

A resident should be able to (a) understand what a question really covers, (b) respond or pass, (c) see why a law provision has a given state, (d) correct it, and (e) be notified only when the relevant legal substance changes. Never suggest a profile response has been officially cast as a ballot.

Screen inventory and acceptance tests

Screen Main information and actions Acceptance criteria
LV-01 Welcome / role Institution, adult/minor flow, purpose and separate privacy consents All important data uses visible before collecting answers
LV-02 Home Local and federal validated coverage with denominator, active changes, task shortcuts Unknown and unverified mappings not counted as “support measured”
LV-03 Local onboarding 40 or fewer seed items (research target), three large buttons, source explainer, progress Keyboard/voiceover readable; no default choice; skip/pause supported
LV-04 Federal onboarding Separate source corpus, 30 seed items + scoped adaptive review Local stage offered before federal; no fabricated completeness
LV-05 Law ledger Filter by jurisdiction, status, direct/inferred basis, evidence freshness Every law links to authoritative text/version and influence explanation
LV-06 Law detail Official text, effective date, normative clauses, answer attribution, overrides Direct override prevails prospectively and logs provenance
LV-07 Changes inbox Proposed, amended, repealed, integrated with diff and action Editorial relocation handled as distinct event with proof; no false re-vote
LV-08 Profile/Privacy Identity assurance status; export/delete; consent; language/accessibility Erasure constraints and retention exceptions explained accurately
LV-09 Consultation/ballot Clear title “Advisory response” or “Authorized ballot” Separate route and confirmation, legal basis, eligibility and receipt
LV-10 AI explanation Source-bound summary, evidence, alternative interpretations, uncertainty Can't fabricate citations or cast positions/votes; user controls outcome

Mobile patterns

Bottom navigation: Home / Laws / Changes / Profile. The first screen foregrounds the next useful action, not political affiliation. Legal text uses collapsible plain-language explanation + primary authoritative text + change diff, never summary alone. Provide tap target minimum 44×44 CSS px as initial guideline, readable minimum text, reduced motion and high contrast. All state indicated by text and icon as well as color.

Question card microcopy

Label: Local ordinance group / Federal law group. Prompt: neutrally worded, single normative dimension. Choices: Support / Don't support / No preference. Context: “This question may inform the following provisions,” with clear direct/inferred status and “Not an official vote.” Review: “This answer does not settle provisions with exceptions, penalties or tradeoffs not mentioned here.”

Law-status ledger semantics

  • “Directly answered”: user answered about that exact operative provision/version.
  • “Provisional / inferred”: a reviewed question may support a suggested interpretation but is not a vote.
  • “Uncertain”: ambiguity, contradiction or unreviewed equivalence; ask or abstain.
  • “Unknown”: no admissible evidence.
  • “Outdated”: answer refers to superseded legal text; prompt version-specific review.

Display legal version and last checked source timestamp per record. Display n/m mapped only alongside a sample corpus label and coverage formula; in the concept demo all counts are synthetic.

Response integrity and dark-pattern prohibitions

No default choice, forced question, order optimized to move ideology, turnout nudges based on sensitive beliefs, party labels used to guess policy preferences, or notification wording that misrepresents an inference as an obligation. Users must be able to deliberately mark “No preference,” distinct from not answering.

Prototype linkage

../prototype/living_voter_dashboard_prototype.html is the retained concept artifact, with 7 synthetic grouped questions over 14 fictional provisions. It demonstrates flow and local browser persistence only. It is not the 96-question instrument, source-ingestion system, identity service or production polling engine.

Chamber and Steward mobile product specifications

Commonwealth OS / Product Design v1.0 — 15 November 2026
Status: Proposal / not institutionally authorized
Scope: Huntsville institutional pilot; mobile-first

Chamber design principle

The first institution-facing experience is a personal legislative dashboard for an authorized elected student representative or council member. Voter-facing features should not leak into the workspace without a separately established authority mode. The persistent AI assistant may explain, retrieve and draft, but never independently adjudicates eligibility, changes a governing document, or casts a vote.

Screens and detailed behaviors

CH-01 Today. Role badge, institution name, meeting and task queue, deadlines, assigned bills, agenda and decision states. A single pinned “Authority mode: [advisory/authorized]” chip. No misleading green certified indicator.

CH-02 Bills. Proposed act list with source status, version, stage, committee, visible amendment history; filters for pending review, in debate, awaiting vote, decided.

CH-03 Bill detail. Authoritative source and source hashes, plain-language AI summary with references, fiscal/implementation considerations, conflicting interpretations; compare versions; open debate; propose amendment; link to resident aggregate consultation (where permitted).

CH-04 Debate and amendment. Threaded reasoning, amendment redlines, sponsors, procedural motion history, conflict/disclosure requirements. A proposed amendment never silently changes the voting text; revisions create new text versions.

CH-05 Vote chamber. Preflight eligibility/rulebook/quorum gate; frozen exact motion/version; deadline; confirmation; cast receipt; no implicit proxy/delegation. If institution permits proxy/delegation, it must be an explicit, scoped and auditable rule, not a default.

CH-06 Decision passport. Outcome, vote method, eligibility snapshot version, governing rule citations, tally verification state, exceptions, challenges, implementation status.

CH-07 AI assistant. Contextual, persistent conversational interface with source retrieval, draft assistance, procedural questions and dissent display. Tool capabilities are separately permissioned, logged and confirmation-gated. It must not silently transact or vote.

Steward screens

ST-01 Institution setup: charter upload, rule parsing, legal authority review, roles and access control, jurisdiction boundaries. Human administrator explicitly approves any extracted rule before activation.

ST-02 Members & eligibility: credential proof and revocation, term dates, role coverage, conflict-of-interest restrictions. Identifiable political preferences are not visible by default.

ST-03 Agenda & sources: schedule meeting, approve legislative provenance and source imports; reconcile out-of-sync codification.

ST-04 Vote administration: configure quorum, notice, ballot format and secret/open vote; dry-run rule checker; cancel or pause on integrity exceptions.

ST-05 Publication & incidents: publish lawful record, manage corrected evidence through append-only supersession; publish decision certificate and appeals outcomes.

Institution-specific modes

  • University: eligibility from approved SGA rules; students as members; student senators as voting officers.
  • High school: student councils only, minor protection and district supervision, no federal legal profile.
  • HOA: lot/unit/member entitlements, weighted votes, proxy rules and thresholds verified against documents and law.
  • Municipal: public records, open-meeting constraints, actual city officer authorization, advisory citizen preferences; legal authority for digital binding decisions requires separate written conclusion.

Red-line design invariants

  1. One actor cannot grant themselves voter eligibility, activate a rule change and certify the same vote without separation-of-duties checks.
  2. Secret-ballot cryptographic receipts cannot link a publicly observable identity to vote content.
  3. Vote on a superseded motion is refused or re-authorized under explicit institution procedure.
  4. Ballots cannot be inferred from topic preferences.
  5. Every operator exception has a date, reason, authority and audit flag.

Legislative ontology, sources and integration architecture

Sources of truth

Prioritize authoritative enactments, minute/roll-call records and governing documents, then codified text, then verified mirrors. For Huntsville, Legistar is a council file/agenda workflow source and the municipal code library provides consolidated ordinance text; the latest codified code alone may omit enacted-but-not-codified changes. The 2026 Huntsville Legistar renewal item provides concrete evidence of the city's legislative tooling (source H1).

For federal layers, GovInfo is a data source for bill/public-law/Code-related packages; ingestion of the entire applicable federal law is a research program, not a turnkey API request. Preserve citation granularity and applicability limits.

Core ontology

Institution → GoverningRuleVersion → LegislativeInstrument → InstrumentVersion → NormativeProvision → AmendmentEvent → EffectEvent.

At the opinion side: QuestionVersion → QuestionProvisionMapping → ResponseEvent → DerivedPosition → OverrideEvent, all in a private data domain. Authorized votes are separate BallotDefinition, EligibilitySnapshot, CastBallot, TallyCertificate and DecisionPassport events; never join personally identifiable ballots to public profiles.

Event classification

Event Substance handling
Introduced New instrument; ask only if relevant and opted-in
Amended New version; semantic change classification; remap and mark affected positions stale
Repealed Preserve prior historic state, append repeal event; ask distinct repeal question
Consolidated/codified Link old and new identifiers; verify whether normative effect identical
Corrected source transcription Correct data through revision with operator reason, independent review
Made effective Record effective time and legal authority; not merely a passed vote

Source ingestion pipeline

  1. Fetch via approved endpoint (do not assume a public screen constitutes permission to bulk scrape).
  2. Persist immutable bytes, retrieval timestamp, URL, checksum, institution identifier and license/usage terms.
  3. Parse and segment instruments. Legal reviewer validates critical attributes: citations, operative clauses, enactment, effective dates.
  4. Align versions through IDs and semantic diff; AI may suggest mappings but cannot certify legal equivalence.
  5. Reconcile to institution-approved gold corpus, record missing/repealed/out-of-scope exceptions.
  6. Trigger question mapping review, provenance updates and user notifications for substantive changes.
  7. Maintain backfill, retry, source outage and human correction queues.

API design (illustrative; no implementation claimed)

  • GET /v1/institutions/{id}/rules/current
  • GET /v1/instruments?institution_id=&status=&after=
  • GET /v1/instruments/{id}/versions/{version}
  • GET /v1/instruments/{id}/diff?from=&to=
  • GET /v1/me/preferences?jurisdiction=&basis=&state= (private)
  • POST /v1/me/responses (idempotent, consent-scoped)
  • POST /v1/decisions/{id}/ballots (only in independently authorized voting mode)
  • GET /v1/decisions/{id}/passport (public/private field redaction)

See ../schemas/ for illustrative JSON Schema; these do not substitute for authenticated API contracts, validated laws or database migration specifications.

Multi-tenancy invariants

All primary keys include or resolve immutable institution scope. No join merges member lists, voter profiles or private responses across tenants. Public government consultation aggregation is a separate and explicit policy-controlled pipeline. Rule version is frozen for each vote and amended only through new authorized snapshots.

Questionnaire design and preference attribution

Commonwealth OS / Product Design v1.0 — 15 November 2026
Status: Proposal / not institutionally authorized
Scope: Huntsville institutional pilot; mobile-first

Formal definitions

Let L_j be the finite, verified in-scope set of operative normative provisions for institution/jurisdiction j and legal snapshot t. The underlying legal corpus is changing and nonuniform: cannot conflate all published pages with distinct independent policy choices.

For each user u and provision ℓ, output:

p(u,ℓ,t) ∈ {support, oppose, neutral, unknown, conflict, stale}

with basis b ∈ {direct-provision, direct-topic, reviewed-inference, no-evidence} and cited evidence/mapping version.

No preference (neutral) ≠ unknown. A person may explicitly be indifferent; unknown means the app cannot reliably assign any position.

Research questionnaire v0.1

Maximum total items 96: 1 open priorities item (not scored into policy position by default) + 40 local seed questions + 30 federal seeds + up to 25 adaptive questions. The document's intent is broad coverage, but it has not been validated to infer a person's support for every ordinance or federal law. Count validated mapped positions, not mere candidate mappings. Local precedes federal in adult/eligible flows. One grouped item may map to many legally equivalent clauses only after reviewed mapping.

Coverage and fidelity metrics

  • Coverage_exact = #provisions with direct exact-version answers / #in-scope verified provisions.
  • Coverage_reviewed = #provisions with direct or independently validated mapping / #in-scope verified provisions.
  • Abstention = #unknown+conflict+stale / #in-scope verified provisions.
  • Mapping error: rate of user disagreement with an inferred position when separately asked an exact-provision question; sample is held out and stratified. Report Wilson/Clopper-Pearson CIs.
  • Question efficiency = validated incremental coverage per answered question, measured against random, flat, topic-only and human-curated baselines.
  • Evaluate fairness by age-appropriate adult groups and opt-in participant categories; no ideological optimization.

Decision rule (specification, not proven theorem)

A question-to-provision edge may be labeled equivalent only when the proposition and the clause are semantically equivalent within explicitly defined conditions, exceptions, sanctions, dates, applicability and funding tradeoffs. Otherwise label related and abstain. For every inference, present the exact generating question and the human-reviewed mapping version. If provisions bundle independent normative dimensions, split or ask the full law directly.

Proposed adaptive item utility:

utility(q) = expected reduction in unresolved high-relevance provisions * calibrated fidelity - burden_cost(q).

Select questions within budget B<=96 under fairness and randomization constraints. This is an optimization target, not evidence of a universally optimal algorithm or guarantee of total legal preference reconstruction.

Version-aware update procedure

At substantive law update: identify affected clause IDs; invalidate only mappings dependent on changed semantics; generate source-specific review questions; preserve old direct responses as history; never retroactively alter a historical ballot. At editorial integration: maintain equivalence certificate and update citations without asking unneeded questions. For new provisions, default unknown until reviewed.

Bias controls

Preregister neutral wording, randomize symmetric question framing for A/B error testing, present tradeoffs and excluded exceptions, separate evaluative AI text from the three-choice response, blind reviewers to response direction and participant identity, allow no preference and skip. Report false inference, false certainty, and re-elicitation burden—not just apparent question compression.

Cheapest decisive experiment

Choose 120–200 authenticated fictional local ordinance clauses with human-created exact-provision reference questions, design 20–30 grouped seeds blind to held-out responses, obtain opt-in adult respondents and compare map precision and coverage against direct questioning. A pilot is positive only if the pre-registered fidelity threshold is met and abstentions are honestly reported. Independent reviewers must challenge mappings, particularly exceptions, penalties and cross-clause interactions.

Democratic Assurance Proof Passport and certification boundaries

Commonwealth OS / Product Design v1.0 — 15 November 2026
Status: Proposal / not institutionally authorized
Scope: Huntsville institutional pilot; mobile-first

Objective

Every consequential institutional decision should carry a portable evidence package allowing an independent auditor to check that the right governing rules and text were used, the right participants were authorized, the decision procedure was valid, and any subsequent effect was correctly recorded. This is a design objective, not an existing certified implementation.

Minimal passport

  • decision_id, institution_id, institution_type, authority_mode
  • rulebook_id, rulebook_version_hash, exact rule clauses (quorum, notice, majority, proxy, attendance, conflict)
  • proposal_id, locked_text_version_hash, publication time, amendments and challenge history
  • eligibility_snapshot_hash, snapshot time, eligible count/weights, exclusions and appeals
  • voting_window, vote_method, secret_or_open, ballot_integrity_method
  • tally, quorum_result, majority_rule_result, verifier_version, cryptographic or signed receipts where valid
  • certification_status, reviewers, exceptions, independent_audit_result
  • implementation_events with evidence and effective-time authorization
  • provenance source metadata, checkpoint hashes and public redaction policy

State machine

DRAFT → SOURCED → AUTHORITY_REVIEWED → DELIBERATING → TEXT_LOCKED → ELIGIBILITY_FROZEN → VOTING → TALLY_PROVISIONAL → INDEPENDENT_CHECK → CERTIFIED_OR_BLOCKED → EFFECT_PENDING → EFFECT_CONFIRMED_OR_DISPUTED.

A post-lock substantive amendment must abort/reinitialize the ballot unless the institution's specific rule says otherwise. An invalid or stale eligibility snapshot blocks valid certification. Unknown legal authority blocks a binding effect, even if the arithmetic is correct. The state machine must not be a decorative progress bar: the server guard checks transition invariants.

Verifier contract (v1 proposal)

verify(passport, authoritative_rule_set, verified_source_set) -> {PASS, FAIL, INDETERMINATE, findings[]}.

To PASS, verify institution and rule authority; text and source hashes; roster validity; voting-period compliance; ballot method and weights; quorum/majority calculations; tally integrity; challenge resolution; publication and effect evidence under institution-specific requirements. INDETERMINATE if legal interpretation, audit materials or independent trust roots are missing. Publication of a JSON receipt alone is not a proof.

Formal safety invariant (conditional)

BindingEffect(e) ⇒ AuthorizedRule(e) ∧ EligibleBallot(e) ∧ LockedText(e) ∧ ValidProcedure(e) ∧ VerifiedOutcome(e) ∧ FreshSource(e) ∧ EnforcedEffectBoundary(e).

This is a desired invariant conditional on complete mediation, not a theorem currently proved for real institutional systems. Network retries, paper decisions, administrative overrides, legacy credentials and off-platform effects are bypass channels requiring separate assurance.

Example checker pseudocode

require passport.authority_mode == 'institution-authorized'
require trusted(rule_version) && valid_at(rule_version, vote_time)
require trusted(source_version) && hash(locked_text)==passport.locked_hash
require verified_roster(passport.eligibility_snapshot)
require rule_engine.validate_ballots(ballots, roster, vote_window)
require recompute_tally(ballots, rulebook)==passport.tally
require quorum_pass && decision_threshold_pass
require no_unresolved_material_challenge
if effect_requested: require enforceable_boundary && fresh_effect_authorization
return PASS

A valid application cannot blindly disclose secret ballot plaintext to an external checker; integrate cryptographic tally proofs or controlled third-party audit depending on the institution's method.

Maturity labels

CONCEPT → PROTOTYPE → REPRODUCIBLE_TEST → INDEPENDENTLY_AUDITED → INSTITUTIONALLY_AUTHORIZED. None implies universal election certification or legal effect beyond named scope. Proof Passport has no automatically granted authority.

Relation to prior art

Decidim's traceable proposal history, ElectionGuard's cryptographic verifiability, and OpenZeppelin's propose/vote/execute mechanisms are components and antecedents, not work to erase. Originality, if established, requires verified cross-rule/source/effect continuity and a same-scope comparison.

Identity, privacy, voting and security architecture

Commonwealth OS / Product Design v1.0 — 15 November 2026
Status: Proposal / not institutionally authorized
Scope: Huntsville institutional pilot; mobile-first

Threats and assets

Assets: sensitive political preferences, institutional rosters, identity claims, ballots, governing documents, rule configurations, source provenance, hash/signature keys, audit records, and implementation endpoints. Adversaries: credential thieves, insiders, poisoned source feeds, fabricated AI summaries, colluding members, vote replay clients, and privileged actors bypassing enforcement.

Identity vs authorization

Identity verification does not establish a person's entitlement to a specific institution vote. Use institution-approved identity and eligibility attestations for UAH, other universities, schools, and HOAs. City adult registered-voter eligibility must be verified through a separately authorized election-administration process; GPS, mailing address, publicly available voter-roll access or student status do not establish it.

Data minimization and role separation

Store the minimum necessary identifiers in a credential vault, policy preferences under a distinct pseudonymous identity, and ballots under a separate authorized tally system. Do not give institutional clerks access to individual resident political preferences by default. Do not expose secret ballot contents through audit export. Do not build tools for candidate targeting or political-message personalization.

Use privacy-preserving aggregate reporting: denominators, self-selection warnings, opt-in consent category, suppression thresholds, small-cell/membership disclosure checks, and no false implication of representative sampling. Differential privacy may be considered but must not be claimed without a defined epsilon/delta, threat model and audit.

Consent and research boundaries

Separate: (i) using a governance service, (ii) optional private preference profiling, (iii) academic study, (iv) public statistical release, and (v) reuse for future research. Refusal to participate in research must not disqualify otherwise eligible official governance. Any future use to study automation of representative labor must be disclosed and separately consented to; no hidden secondary-purpose collection. Deletion must explain records required to preserve official election validity and lawful retention.

Minors and schools

Huntsville City Schools pilot requires district sponsorship and legal review. Apply FERPA where student records are involved; determine COPPA and state requirements by user age, data and operator model. Do not collect individual minors' federal ideology or political preference profiles. School-governance exercises should be narrow, educational, opt-in and age-appropriate, with parent/guardian policies documented as applicable.

Ballot and security boundaries

  • Binding ballot cast only after role/rule/eligibility/time/text lock checks.
  • Server-authoritative idempotency for resend, retries and disconnected clients.
  • No coercion-sensitive vote receipts showing plaintext to third parties when secret voting is required.
  • Authenticity, ballot secrecy where mandated, tally verification, challenge procedures, malware, accessibility and operational resilience require dedicated audits.
  • Audit events append-only and hash-linked; administrative superusers cannot erase past evidence without detection.
  • Source-fed LLM context treated as untrusted input; prompt injection cannot operate privileged tools or alter law parsing without human review.
  • Complete effect mediation is an assumption to verify, not a default fact.

Privacy incident response

Detect → contain → scope → preserve evidence → notify institution/affected participants according to law and contract → independent postmortem → remediation and regression tests. Maintain disclosure limits, retention schedules, backup deletion and breach exercises.

Legal research checkpoint

Alabama law on municipalities (§11-45-1, §11-45-2, §11-45-8) and FERPA are initial references, not a comprehensive legal opinion. The exact governing documents and institution authorization control in earlier pilots. Retain counsel's written determinations at each stage.

Evaluation plan, hypotheses and independent validation

Status

This document preregisters proposed hypotheses and audit steps. No user study, independent audit or legal approval has been conducted within this documentation effort.

Questions to test

H1 (efficiency): Grouped, reviewed questions reduce respondent burden for a fixed, specified mapping-fidelity target compared with exhaustive law-by-law questioning.

H2 (control): Showing provenance and direct overrides improves agreement with participants' own exact-provision responses and reduces uncorrected false inferences.

H3 (continuity): Proof Passport exposes more correct cross-stage status transitions and more detectable authorization failures than conventional disjoint proposal, vote and implementation exports.

H4 (portability): A common core with institution-scoped rulebooks preserves rule validity across university, school, HOA and municipality, without violating tenant isolation.

H5 (equity): Comprehension, participation and successful objection are not systematically worse for accessibility and lower-bandwidth participants, or failures are detected and corrected before scale-up.

Independent auditor designed before deployment

Create an audit team which does not implement the construction, uses an independent parser and rule engine where feasible, and tests edge cases: law amendments after ballot lock, mid-window member removal, tied quorum, conflicting proxies, neutral-versus-unknown, duplicated ballots, time-zone errors, delayed publication, editor-only codification, offline votes, unauthorized effect attempts. Counterexample search is not a proof; document both genuine falsifications and unresolved ambiguities.

Metrics dictionary

Denominator fidelity: Define the in-scope instrument set before measuring a percentage. Sampling: Explain self-selection and weighting; do not call a volunteer dashboard a representative poll. Security: Test effect-time boundary, not only source review. Statistical estimates: Report confidence interval method, pre-registered exclusion rules and missing data. Outcomes: Separate system correctness, user satisfaction, institutional legality, and scientific novelty.

Stop rule

Stop the affected phase on any materially invalid binding result, a verified covert disclosure of individual political choices, unauthorized role elevation, repeated inability to map laws accurately, or failure of institution legal permission. Do not promote “prototype” to “certified” absent external review.

Research contribution thresholds

A novel contribution requires: (a) a claim beyond already documented Decidim/CONSUL/Polis/Loomio/ElectionGuard capabilities, (b) reproducible implementation or theorem, (c) matching-scope contemporaneous prior-art audit, and (d) independent adversarial evaluation. A good UX concept, mockup or renamed existing feature is insufficient.

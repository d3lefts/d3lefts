# Three-Pilot Charter — Proposed Research Protocol v1.0

Date: 2026-11-15. No pilot is represented as approved, recruited, operating, or legally binding.

## Objective
Compare an identical constitutional assurance and measurement protocol across three different organizational scales: private association, university student government, and municipal government. Test whether a bounded, source-linked neutral question bank makes representatives' decisions more transparent without claiming voter authority they do not possess.

## The proposed pilot targets

**Pilot 01 / HOA:** A willing Alabama HOA selected by participant agreement. Authority derives from its actual declaration, covenants, bylaws, and applicable Alabama statutes, not a generic template. Eligibility may be per owner, lot or membership unit as required by documents, while residents may participate in separately labeled advisory surveys. Primary administrators: board/manager. Public scope: HOA member portal, subject to privacy and lawful disclosure. Conditions: cooperation of an actual association and document review.

**Pilot 02 / University:** University of Alabama in Huntsville SGA as a proposed research candidate, or another willing SGA. UAH has a student senate and executive structure, but its advocacy recommendations are not university policy. Primary administrators: SGA officers and designated university liaison. Enrollment/participation eligibility requires approved records and consent; do not assume all student-body feedback is a binding SGA vote. Special care for student records and minors.

**Pilot 03 / Municipality:** City of Huntsville, Alabama, as a proposed partner. Five elected city-council districts under present law; agendas, zoning information and adopted municipal budgets are published. Primary users: participating council member(s), clerk liaison, planning and finance departments, if authorized. Initial interface only mirrors official public records and collects advisory responses, never claims city endorsement or legal decision authority absent partnership.

## Common phases
0. Feasibility: written permission, institutional legal authority, source inventory, sampling frame/consent, DPIA, question audit.
1. Representative/Admin private sandbox: ingest source-linked issues, questionnaires, unverified internal results; zero binding votes.
2. Public read-only record: approved non-confidential questions, official decisions, records, finance and source links; public updates with privacy redactions.
3. Opt-in constituent portal: accessible preference profile, question responses, explicit delegation experiments, authenticated eligibility where lawful; nonbinding.
4. Conditional lawful adoption: ONLY after separate legal authorization, certified election-security review, institutional vote-rule verification, and independent oversight. No automatic progression.

## Pilot success/failure measures (to preregister, not achieved)
- Source coverage: percent of agenda items linked to authoritative sources and correct statuses.
- Mapping precision: independent legal review of sampled question-to-rule claims; flag each unjustified link.
- Neutrality: confidence-bounded response equivalence across independently reviewed wording variants.
- Predictive validity: held-out decision preference agreement, calibration, subgroup differences, question burden.
- Administrative usefulness: time from agenda to verified question, time from decision to documented rationale, staff corrections.
- Citizen experience: comprehension, accessibility, completion/opt-out rates, privacy expectations.
- Transparency: fraction of decisions with published source, tally or known limitation, recorded justification, cost and follow-up outcome.
- Assurance: unauthorized bindings=0 during advisory trials; no PII leaks; all edits versioned.

## Adversarial cases
1. HOA board tries to treat tenant survey responses as statutory member votes.
2. SGA asks for campus-wide policy change outside its power.
3. Huntsville city decision references an uncodified pending ordinance as if enacted.
4. A small demographic reporting cell would reveal a constituent's preference.
5. Administrators attempt to edit wording after responses arrive.
6. Survey answers are presented as the views of all eligible residents despite opt-in recruitment.
7. A misleading law mapping converts a general housing question into support for a specific project.
8. Sensitive evidence appears in a purportedly fully transparent public dashboard.

## Governance
Pilot partners review their own documents and approve research participation. Independent reviewers oversee question syntax and legal mappings. Research ethics review (including any IRB determination) precedes recruitment and human-subject data collection. Require separately authorized civic data handling and avoid political-party administration of the instrument.

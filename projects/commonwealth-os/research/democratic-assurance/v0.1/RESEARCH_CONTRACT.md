# NIGHTFALL research contract — COMMONWEALTH 2030 v0.1

**Objective:** Determine whether a continuous, local, ordinance-oriented civic process can simultaneously achieve informed agency (A), political equality (E), lawful authorization (L), and operational assurance (S), subject to cost and democratic constraints.

**Feasible set:** Decentralized American municipal deployments; legally authorized processes only; optional AI; accessible non-digital participation; current signed documentary evidence; binding voting only through processes permitted by jurisdiction; all consequential agent actions assumed to pass through a trusted guard. To be tested, not assumed: actual guard completeness, legal validity, full inclusion, data accuracy, non-manipulative AI and public affordability.

**Strongest relevant external references checked 2026-11-15:** ETH Zurich KOF supported democracy (2026–2031) for personal AI voter support; NASEM *Securing the Vote* for unsolved Internet ballot security; NIST SP 800-63-4 for identity; EAC VVSG2/E2E for election systems; NCSL 2026 initiative law variation. These narrow novelty; architecture is not claimed first in the world.

**Best verified construction:** This package exhaustively checks 32,768 finite combinations of the modeled effect guard, with 9 explicit negative controls. A separate 256-portfolio exact rational toy optimization returns minimum arbitrary cost 21 and independent dynamic programming verifies the optimum. These are program-execution receipts only, NOT a formal theorem prover certificate, a human democratic evaluation, a deployed-effect audit, or an American legal validity certificate.

**Remaining gaps:** Actual municipal law counsel signoff; real voter studies; systematic adversarial agent testing; physical trust boundary completeness; externally validated measurement scales; causal inference and fair participation assessment; actual money/time costs; real-world jurisdiction pilot agreements.

**Novelty/significance threshold:** A quantified advantage or a nontrivial impossibility/cost frontier for the joint A/E/L/S standard not subsumed by existing assisted democracy and constitutional verification research. Require a dated closest-prior-art audit and disconfirming baselines.

**Independent review:** Separate researchers in political science, election administration, cryptography, municipal law, accessibility, and systems assurance; code reproducibility audit by a person who did not author the implementation. Our included 'independent' checker means alternate implementation, not independent researchers.

**Certificates:** First layer: source and official law provenance. Second: versioned legal authorization record (human signed). Third: signed empirical protocol and audit. Fourth: trusted effect guard test receipt. No certificate may be promoted outside its scope.

**Research budget / stop rule:** First fund only paper analysis, a tiny offline laboratory prototype, and a costed IRB-appropriate experiment. Stop deployment on legal nonauthorization or a single reproduced bypass. Do not finance binding mobile voting until election-law and independent security gates are passed. Reserve 20% of experimentation capacity for disconfirming designs.

**Cheapest decisive experiment:** Three-arm randomized nonbinding assessment of one public local ordinance, comparing official materials, generic AI, and personalized source-bounded civic AI. Preregister comprehension gain, preference fidelity/non-manipulation, inequality of access and time, and total participant support cost; recruit diverse citizens and include an accessible non-smartphone option. Actual sample-size calculation depends on effect assumptions and design.

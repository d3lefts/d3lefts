### Executive finding

A Democratic Assurance Passport (DAP) has been specified to connect four requirements of continuous democratic governance: informed human agency, political equality, legal authorization, and operational assurance. It makes a binding vote a necessary input to public authority **only where the institution's law and procedures make that vote binding**, and additionally checks whether the resulting action is currently authorized.

This report integrates a conditional effect-time soundness argument, a two-world insufficiency result for tally-only evidence, an executable bounded reference guard, a separately coded 45,056-case checker, and an illustrative exact cost model. The joint technical evidence portfolio costs 18 versus 22 synthetic units for isolated checks; this is not an economic estimate or a globally novel theorem.

The strongest open research target is to establish a substantive lower and upper bound, or a structural impossibility boundary, for the **joint democratic assurance cost**, including empirical informed agency and political equality rather than treating them as Boolean software properties.

### 1. The political and institutional thesis

A computational commonwealth is a constitutional political-economic order in which legitimate political authority remains vested in humans, even as synthetic intelligence supports economic production, legislative drafting, public administration, and adjudicative work within existing legal constraints. The dissertation asks whether continuous, locally grounded, AI-mediated direct democracy can preserve informed human agency, political equality, legal authorization, and operational assurance **simultaneously**, and what that costs.

The target research architecture supports directly binding platform votes when an organization has lawful authority to adopt them. The proposed deployment ladder begins with student councils or student organizations, proceeds to homeowner and other member associations, and expands to municipal settings only after jurisdiction-specific approval and much stronger election-security review. The technical design cannot grant itself statutory voting authority.

### 2. Democratic Assurance Passport

The passport binds one proposal digest, one election rule version, one voting event, one certified final result, an authenticated source-of-authority record, current appeal and revocation status, and one specified class of executable effects. It is a verifiable evidence chain, **not a digital identity profile of voters**.

The proof object contains separate empirical A/E evidence and formal or professionally attested L/S evidence. A human-approved decision is not interchangeable with an AI recommendation. An election result is not interchangeable with an authorization to carry out an arbitrary public act.

The central guard condition is:

`Permit = ValidVote AND SameProposal AND Jurisdiction AND LawfulAuthority AND CurrentSource AND NoStay AND EffectWithinScope`.

This is deliberately incomplete as a *universal* legal test; each jurisdiction's legally necessary procedures, ballot requirements, rights and challenge mechanisms must be made explicit in its separately reviewed adapter.

### 3. Exact limited findings

**DAP-S1, conditional execution soundness.** If all relevant effects pass through one trusted sink, current authority/evidence are faithfully supplied, and inspection and effectuation are atomic, every committed effect has a matching current passport witness. The proof is induction over committed sink transitions. Neither complete mediation nor legal correctness is currently established for a real institution.

**DAP-N1, indistinguishable legal worlds.** If two worlds share all verified ballot observations but differ in whether an action is legally authorized, no ballot-only verifier can safely authorize in the first and reject in the second with perfect correctness. Therefore a certified tally alone is insufficient for general effect-time permission. This is a classical observation-separation result, specialized to the new passport model.

**Change invalidation.** The reference guard rejects mismatched law, evidence, voter-roll, voting rule or budget epochs. More economical recertification may reuse provably unaffected evidence, but that stronger transfer requires formal legal semantics and verified dependency completeness.

**Finite checker result.** The separately written failure-based oracle agrees with the reference implementation in 45,056 closed-world test cases, including replay attempts and an explicit positive/negative authority world pair. This is an executable finite result, not a proof of the cryptographic protocol or a distributed public system.

**Cost illustration.** The classical weighted-cover cost on six technical requirements is exactly 18 synthetic units using a shared roster+tally operation, a joint legal+freshness+budget evidence operation, and an effect guard, versus 22 for isolated evidence. Brute force and subset DP independently agree. The result demonstrates a 4-unit model saving only for the explicitly invented operation costs and cover sets.

### 4. Evidence still needed for each democratic requirement

| Requirement | Certifiable or inspectable element | Remaining empirical or institutional proof |
|---|---|---|
| Informed agency | Original text, provenance, AI disclosure, comprehension-study audit trails | Causal improvement in understanding; preference fidelity; manipulation tests; generalization across populations |
| Political equality | Rules for eligibility, permitted ballot weights, participation uniqueness, accessible alternatives | Effective access, group disparities, participation fatigue, coercion and representation |
| Legal authorization | Authentic institutional charter/bylaws/statute, reviewed adoption records, effective date and appeals | Responsible legal opinions, public-institution jurisdictional variation and legitimate disputes |
| Operational assurance | Cryptographic ballot verification interface, guard invariant, logs, effect scope and version checks | Independent protocol audit, complete external effect inventory, race-resistant deployment, incident response |

All four must be evaluated under the **same** jurisdiction and process. Passing a technical checklist is not proof of democratic legitimacy.

### 5. Prior art as scientific infrastructure

ETH Zurich KOF/NOMIS (2026–2031) develops personalized, trustworthy AI voting assistants and supported democracy. Jansen et al. (CHI EA 2026) present Decidos and identity-wallet-based low-stakes online voting. NIST SP 800-63-4 supplies digital identity guidance; EAC VVSG 2.0 and the December 2025 E2E evaluation program supply auditable election standards and a route for independent cryptographic-protocol evaluation. These provide modules, standards and baselines. The National Academies' 2018 report cautions against interpreting cryptographic voting proof as secure Internet return of ballots. Florida Statutes 720.317 supplies a concrete, conditional HOA electronic-voting legal pathway. No cited source by itself settles the joint A/E/L/S democratic assurance question.

NIGHTFALL's inherited mathematical mechanisms include exact information complementarities, scoped optimal evidence acquisition, source-version recertification, conditional change invariance and effect-time permission. The record must preserve proof scopes and acknowledge prior-art roots (classical observation indistinguishability, set cover, min-cut, guarded state-machine invariants). Novelty remains a *research threshold*, not a completed status.

### 6. Three-paper PhD structure

**Paper I - Constitutional Foundations of Continuous Democracy.** A jurisdiction-aware political theory and legal transition framework, comparing binding organizational rules and statutory public authority. Determine the boundary between permanent participation and legitimately binding collective action.

**Paper II - Personalized Intelligence and Democratic Agency.** Preregistered study comparing official materials, generic AI and personalized source-grounded civic AI, evaluating human comprehension, preference fidelity, political equality, manipulation resistance, effort and participation fatigue. The initial pilot may independently run binding governance votes, but the experimental protocol must not manipulate real ballot choices.

**Paper III - The Price of Democratic Assurance.** Specify a verifiable passport linking collective approval to live authority and effects, and determine the minimum resources and joint feasibility boundary for A/E/L/S. Develop independently auditable finite and then more realistic models; evaluate against cryptographic-ballot and conventional administrative baselines.

### 7. Audit gates and near-term tests

1. Verify a real potential pilot institution's binding-vote authority, membership and procedures; publish a legal-source map and appeals pathway.
2. Integrate an externally reviewed verifiable voting component rather than inventing unreviewed cryptography; review endpoint, privacy and coercion threats separately.
3. Preregister negative controls: valid tally but invalid authority; changed ordinance; invalid quorum; late stay; revoked permission; replayed effect ID; missing effect mediation; mismatched legal and ballot versions.
4. Run a human-subject study with external ethics approval where required, accessibility review and correct treatment of private political preferences.
5. Independently measure operational and participation costs in real dollars and time, then estimate the joint assurance frontier with uncertainty bounds.

Any confirmed unauthorized committed effect or unrecoverable election-integrity failure blocks advancement. A failed assumption demotes the claim; a numerical improvement alone does not certify political legitimacy.

### 8. Conclusion and research claim status

The formal research object is now a **Decision-to-Effect Democratic Assurance Passport**, with an explicit conditional soundness proof, a precise observation-obstruction theorem, a conservative recertification rule, and machine-reproducible finite checks. Its immediate value is as a disciplined system of evidence and failure gates, not a completed nationwide proof.

The dissertation's principal open problem remains to characterize the jointly feasible and minimally costly architecture for informed agency, equal political standing, legal authorization and operational assurance in binding, continuously available democratic institutions. Independent political science, cryptography, law, and security review must travel with any claim of advancement.

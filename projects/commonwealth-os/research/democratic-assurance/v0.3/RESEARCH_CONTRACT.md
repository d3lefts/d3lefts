# NIGHTFALL research contract — COMMONWEALTH 2030 v0.2

**Objective:** Determine whether a continuous, local, ordinance-oriented civic process can simultaneously achieve informed agency (A), political equality (E), lawful authorization (L), and operational assurance (S), subject to cost and democratic constraints.

**Feasible set:** Decentralized American municipal deployments; legally authorized processes only; optional AI; accessible non-digital participation; current signed documentary evidence; binding in-platform organizational voting where authorized by bylaws and applicable law, with separately reviewed cryptographic protocols and accessible fallback; all consequential agent actions assumed to pass through a trusted guard. To be tested, not assumed: actual guard completeness, legal validity, full inclusion, data accuracy, non-manipulative AI and public affordability.

**Strongest relevant external references checked 2026-11-15:** ETH Zurich KOF supported democracy (2026–2031) for personal AI voter support; NASEM *Securing the Vote* for unsolved Internet ballot security; NIST SP 800-63-4 for identity; EAC VVSG2/E2E for election systems; NCSL 2026 initiative law variation. These narrow novelty; architecture is not claimed first in the world.

**Previous v0.1 verified construction:** This package exhaustively checks 32,768 finite combinations of the modeled effect guard, with 9 explicit negative controls. A separate 256-portfolio exact rational toy optimization returns minimum arbitrary cost 21 and independent dynamic programming verifies the optimum. These are program-execution receipts only, NOT a formal theorem prover certificate, a human democratic evaluation, a deployed-effect audit, or an American legal validity certificate.

**Remaining gaps:** Actual municipal law counsel signoff; real voter studies; systematic adversarial agent testing; physical trust boundary completeness; externally validated measurement scales; causal inference and fair participation assessment; actual money/time costs; real-world jurisdiction pilot agreements.

**Novelty/significance threshold:** A quantified advantage or a nontrivial impossibility/cost frontier for the joint A/E/L/S standard not subsumed by existing assisted democracy and constitutional verification research. Require a dated closest-prior-art audit and disconfirming baselines.

**Independent review:** Separate researchers in political science, election administration, cryptography, municipal law, accessibility, and systems assurance; code reproducibility audit by a person who did not author the implementation. Our included 'independent' checker means alternate implementation, not independent researchers.

**Certificates:** First layer: source and official law provenance. Second: versioned legal authorization record (human signed). Third: signed empirical protocol and audit. Fourth: trusted effect guard test receipt. No certificate may be promoted outside its scope.

**Research budget / stop rule:** First fund only paper analysis, a tiny offline laboratory prototype, and a costed IRB-appropriate experiment. Stop deployment on legal nonauthorization or a single reproduced bypass. Do not finance binding mobile voting until election-law and independent security gates are passed. Reserve 20% of experimentation capacity for disconfirming designs.

**Previous cheapest human-subject experiment:** Three-arm randomized nonbinding assessment of one public local ordinance, comparing official materials, generic AI, and personalized source-bounded civic AI. Preregister comprehension gain, preference fidelity/non-manipulation, inequality of access and time, and total participant support cost; recruit diverse citizens and include an accessible non-smartphone option. Actual sample-size calculation depends on effect assumptions and design.


## Democratic Assurance Passport v0.2 formal milestone

**Research object:** One typed and decision-bound proof passport, not a blanket certificate of democracy. Operative claim is `Execute(x,t) => exists P: Permit(P,x,s_t)`. Trusted complete mediation, truthful source attestations, cryptographic vote-proof verification and atomic state sampling remain explicit assumptions.

**Formal result status:** DAP-S1, soundness of a closed-world guarded sink, has an elementary induction proof. DAP-N1, tally-only evidence cannot distinguish equal-vote worlds with opposed legal authority, has a classical transcript-indistinguishability proof. Neither is global novelty certification or a real-world validation.

**New executable evidence:** Separately implemented oracle and reference guard agree on 45,056 finite cases. An independently implemented subset-DP agrees with brute-force evidence-cover optimization: 18 versus 22 synthetic cost units, a 4-unit reduction. Validation also checks an archival JSON schema and synthetic fixture.

**Strongest external baseline this session:** ETH Zurich supported democracy (2026–2031), Decidos (CHI 2026), NIST SP 800-63-4, EAC VVSG 2.0 / E2E evaluation, NASEM *Securing the Vote*, and Florida Statutes s.720.317. Full references and licenses not assumed; source links in DAP report.

**Next novelty threshold:** Demonstrate a new nonseparable theorem or measurable assurance gain, not simply a conjunction of existing formal methods and political principles. Different legal regimes, voter populations, and technical threat models define separate transfer tests.

**Next permission gate:** Before any binding institution pilot: governing-body approval, independently checked election security, privacy/coercion/accessibility plan, complaint/recount process, legal and source provenance, and complete execution-sink audit.

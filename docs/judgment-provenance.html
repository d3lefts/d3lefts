Methods / proposed experiment / JP-01

Who made
the judgment?

A correct calculation can still conceal the origin of a consequential choice. We are designing a randomized test of whether blind, precommitted adversarial review protects independent scientific judgment while preserving the quality of the research.

STATUS / PREREGISTRATION DRAFT · NO HUMAN STUDY RUN · NO EFFECTIVENESS CLAIM

Three ways to review one decision.

The same frozen evidence is presented under three review protocols. In each condition, an agent is randomly assigned an honest, defensible analytical preference. We measure whether that arbitrary preference changes the analyst's final methodological choice.

01 / ORDINARY

Agent-led review

The analyst receives an evidence-supported recommendation from a research agent.

02 / ADVERSARY

Visible challenge

An opposing agent makes the case for a plausible alternative, with arguments attributed.

03 / BLIND

Independent judgment first

The analyst freezes a provisional view before the lead recommendation appears. A source-blind adversarial assessment follows.

Two claims. Two evidence gates.

First measure the difference between the influence of randomized agent preferences under ordinary and blind review. Then independently grade methodological errors. An intervention that merely makes reviewers disagree with agents, while increasing errors, fails our scientific goal.

The current evidence boundary.

Anthropic's October 2026 study reported strong influence of randomly assigned agent preferences on intermediate analytic choices. Its effect on the final policy decision was statistically inconclusive under the registered primary estimator. NIGHTFALL's intervention is a proposed extension, with zero recruited participants and zero empirical intervention results.

A standard-library checker and synthetic mutation tests are provided to verify the arithmetic and data contract. Passing those software tests cannot establish that real reviewers were randomized, blinded, independent, or effective.

NULL FIELD / RESEARCH DISPATCH / 13 NOVEMBER 2026

The minimum
cost of knowing.

What determines the price of discovering information, turning it into a useful decision, and producing evidence that survives scrutiny and change?

STATUS / evidence-linked synthesis, not a new universal theorem CORE / information conversion and oracle-matched complexity GATES / original-source Q2, independent review, priority and implementation

The governing distinction: information present in a system is not necessarily observable, actionable, efficiently computable or certifiable. The minimum cost depends on the task, permitted measurements, computational model and trust contract—not merely system size.

01 / THE CONDITIONS

Seven things that change the answer.

01 / TARGET

Decision specificity

What error or loss actually matters? Predicting a single outcome may be far cheaper than reconstructing the system that produces it.

02 / GEOMETRY

Interaction structure

Independence, Hoeffding degree, rank, sparsity and separators can expose useful local observables. These assumptions must be certified, not inferred from intuition.

03 / INTERFACE

Permitted observations

Coordinate reads, group probes, model evaluations and posterior draws reveal different things. An inexpensive answer under one oracle may be impossible under another.

04 / LIMIT

Identifiability

When decision-distinct systems induce indistinguishable transcripts, no amount of computation can recover the missing distinction.

05 / ALGORITHM

Computational extraction

Choosing an informative measurement or calculating its expected value may cost much more than acquiring the measurement itself.

06 / EVIDENCE

Certifiability

A proof, a finite audit, a compiler receipt and a real-system assurance claim validate different objects and must not be conflated.

07 / TIME

Change stability

Evidence reuse depends on authentic update reports and traceable dependencies. Without them, previously checked claims may require fresh verification.

02 / ESTABLISHED INFORMATION LIMIT

Evidence has an irreducible acquisition cost.

For two hypotheses requiring different decisions, an adaptive experiment that is correct with probability at least 1−δ under each hypothesis must accumulate enough transcript distinguishability. Under standard conditional-kernel, stopping and absolute-continuity assumptions:

E₀[Σ KL(P₀ᵗ ∥ P₁ᵗ)] ≥ kl(1−δ, δ)

If every observation yields at most κ units of KL divergence per unit price, its expected acquisition cost is at least kl(1−δ,δ)/κ. This is a classical sequential change-of-measure argument, not an original NIGHTFALL theorem. It does not automatically lower-bound verification or computation. 04 / OPEN THEOREM · NEXT DISCRIMINATING TEST

An oracle-matched, change-stable complexity theorem.

The proposed contribution is to identify one nontrivial structural system class admitting matching upper and lower bounds for decision acquisition under the same measurement interface, coupled to an independently checkable, incrementally reusable evidence object. This is an objective, not an accomplished new law.

Next: independently enumerate exact acquisition and certificate paths for a small authenticated dependent system, compare the same objective and prices against sequential testing and dynamic set-cover baselines, and demand a theorem-type proof before claiming generality.

THE PROBLEM / NOT JUST THE ANSWER

The missing step is often the whole problem.

A proof of an influence inequality on the Boolean cube does not automatically apply to every product probability law. A finite witness for an optimization problem is not proof of the global optimum. An accepted Lean declaration verifies its own assumptions—not the last theorem someone hopes to derive from it.

Our research program sits at these boundaries. Its most reusable result may be a representation change, a certified operator identity, a counterexample to a proposed shortcut, or a way to track whether evidence survives a change in assumptions.

04 / THREE RESEARCH CASES

Three ways a strong result can still be incomplete.

CASE 01 / PRODUCT MEASURES

Finite is not infinite.

The original-source finite Q2 theorem has a kernel-checked coefficient near 105.416. The arbitrary-index theorem needs a valid finite-information approximation, source-faithful degree and influence transport, and the final norm limit. A checked intermediate operator equality does not close that chain.

CASE 02 / EXTREMAL METHODS

A witness is not an optimum.

A rational step-function construction provides an upper bound for Erdős minimum overlap. Even a fully replayed integer certificate does not supply a matching lower bound. The missing tool is a global argument—not another decimal digit.

CASE 03 / SAFE AGENTS

Safety is not availability.

A restrictive policy can prevent forbidden actions by also preventing legitimate ones. The Price of Permission asks for the resource cost of satisfying both, under authenticated source freshness and effect-time enforcement. Synthetic or finite results do not certify production security.

05 / COVERAGE AND CHECKING

No invented coverage. No inherited certainty.

What this edition actually covers

The atlas contains a manually selected set of 24 entries and 24 typed relationships, spanning six areas of mathematics and computer science. Some entries are published prior art, some are NIGHTFALL formal subresults or exact constructions, and some are explicitly open conjectures.

It does not contain a citation crawl, citation centrality rankings, paper-by-paper error counts, full-text screening, human referee verdicts, or an independent audit of every work it links. Selected claims have a read-only registry crosswalk below. Source-backed receipts retain their original exact-head and theorem scopes; changes require recertification rather than automatic promotion. The curated graph remains a dated editorial snapshot.

Snapshot: source and claim scope reviewed against the public repository catalogue as of 17 November 2026. For newer theorem status, use the exact source, theorem statement, and associated CI receipt rather than trusting this static map.

What would make it a field-scale audit?

  1. Define the corpus. Choose subfields and inclusion rules before counting papers; preserve full bibliographic provenance.
  2. Extract claims, not adjectives. Normalize theorems, assumptions, equations, models and cited dependencies; record which full texts are accessible.
  3. Recompute selectively. Build executable checkers for impactful claims; distinguish a plausible flag from a reproducible counterexample.
  4. Adversarially verify. Separate construction from independent audit; publish versions, errors, contradictions and retractions.
  5. Preserve the graph. Keep every source, mechanism, proof, limitation and possible transfer linked to its exact certificate.

Casey Handmer’s physics field audit inspired the narrative-plus-map approach. This edition is independently written and has a deliberately narrower, fully disclosed evidence base.

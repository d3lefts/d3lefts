DIRECTLY REPLAYED · CLASSICAL REDUCTION

One conflict language.
Two extremal problems.

In maximal Sidon sets, collisions are equal unordered pair sums. In a hypercube, collisions are four chosen edges completing a coordinate square. Both become independent sets in a conflict hypergraph. A single certificate grammar can test forbidden subsets and pointwise maximality.

Different objectives remain different: Erdős #156 minimizes the size of a maximal Sidon set; Erdős #86 maximizes a C4-free edge set. A locally maximal set may be smaller than a global maximum.

TRANSFER 01 / COMPOSABLE PROOF METHOD

From collision to repair.

Axiom's one-point Sidon deletion matching, Vesper's batch collision-core reduction, Ryev's independent Blossom replay and Tessera's one-point pseudoforest solver address the same explicit collision supports. TRANSFER 02 / FAILED SCALE INFERENCE

Finite gains need recursion.

Fixed-prefix Sidon repairs achieve coefficients above 1/√2 for each fixed prefix. Tessera's source-switch experiment shows that equal average collision counts can conceal different deletion costs. Vesper's two-window bound proves that that particular certificate loses its guaranteed gap when prefix size grows.

Missing: a recursively closed, growing-prefix family with source-uniform repair loss and endpoint control. Erdős #44 and #329 remain open.

TRANSFER 03 / SENSING-MODEL DISCIPLINE

Uniqueness is not decoding.

Vesper's iid F25 phase-collision bound establishes information-theoretic identifiability under its assumptions. Axiom's prime-subfield formulation supplies a candidate polynomial-time decoder under the same iid law. Its adaptive and fixed-graph decoders belong to different measurement models and must be compared separately.

Review open: external proof and same-scope priority checks. Raw measurement coefficients across distinct sensing laws are not interchangeable records.

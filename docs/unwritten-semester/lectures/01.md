## The operating principle

A dissertation is not a long report. It is a **new claim with a stated scope, a mechanism, evidence, and a way for a hostile reader to check it**. Doctoral work often fails before the mathematics starts: the research question is too elastic to falsify, or the contribution is a better application of an old technique rather than new knowledge.

Start by designing the *claim interface*, just as you would design a technical interface. Every claim needs inputs, assumptions, expected outputs, and failure behavior. A result that silently changes meaning between the proposal and the defense is a broken interface.

## A practical research specification

Write one sheet with seven fields:

1. **System and decision:** what real decision would change if this result were true?
2. **Objects:** state spaces, variables, resources, interfaces, and units.
3. **Admissible world:** which environments, distributions, failures, actors, and changes are allowed?
4. **Claim:** an exact equality, inequality, algorithm, empirical effect, or impossibility statement.
5. **Comparator:** the strongest baseline under the *same* conditions and budgets.
6. **Certificate:** proof, independent trace, test protocol, or bounded interval that checks the claim.
7. **Disproof:** the smallest observation that would force revision.

For example, an imprecise question is “Does redundancy improve AURORA reliability?” That cannot be answered without a failure model. A precise *hypothesis* is: “For a specified event distribution, sensor error model, and common-mode outage probability, policy Q has lower missed-event rate than policy S at comparable false-alarm and acquisition cost.” AURORA permits a paired, reproducible simulation; it does **not** justify a field claim about actual sensor networks.

## The strongest-counterexample habit

Before making a model, write down two plausible worlds that fit your current data but require different actions. If you cannot distinguish them with available observations, you have an identifiability problem, not a missing optimizer. A good research problem makes this tension explicit.

Record claims in `templates/claim-ledger.csv`. Keep rejected hypotheses and their counterexamples. A negative result can be a valuable finding if its scope is precise and it is honestly checked.

## Lab — turn an ambition into a claim

1. Choose AURORA or a system from your own research. State the mission and who is authorized to make decisions.
2. Produce a one-page research specification using the seven fields above.
3. Register two hypotheses **before** running `labs/aurora.py` and name a discriminating metric for each.
4. For each hypothesis, record the smallest decisive test, estimated cost, and explicit stop condition.
5. Identify one scenario in which your preferred answer would fail.

**Acceptance artifact:** `01-problem-contract.md`, with at least one operational definition, one falsifier, and one source-backed comparator.

## Adversarial check

Ask another reader to restate the theorem or hypothesis **without reading your motivation**. If they disagree about the quantifier, comparator, system boundary, or outcome metric, the contract is not finished. Counterexample searches disprove candidates; failure to find a counterexample is not a proof.

## Doctoral extension

Split the result into four explicitly different gates: theoretical validity, empirical replicability, novelty relative to dated prior art, and practical significance. Passing one gate cannot imply another.

SESSION 02 / 14 — FRAME

Boundaries, actors & the operational world

Build mission-centered contexts and operational scenarios without losing authority.

90 min · lab 2hLECTURE + LAB + ADVERSARIAL CHECKNO PREREQUISITE INSTALLS

The operating principle

Every engineered system is a choice of boundary. Put a physical sensor outside the boundary and its failure becomes an environmental disturbance; put it inside and it becomes a design responsibility. Neither choice is inherently correct. The test is whether the boundary represents the mission and the decisions for which your conclusions will be used.

A context diagram should show more than boxes. It should show actors, interfaces, authority, information, matter, energy, and possible loss of control. In a sociotechnical system, the people who authorize, operate, maintain, fund, and experience consequences are not interchangeable.

From mission to operational concept

Build a compact Concept of Operations (ConOps) with six scenes: nominal use; a missing observation; a degraded component; operator override; an external change; and retirement/recovery. For each scene, specify the trigger, observable state, authorized action, and safe outcome. This reveals latent requirements early.

Use AURORA as a running case: a remote observation station measures a synthetic binary environmental anomaly and routes an alert to a human. It contains three simulated channels, an alarm rule, a maintainer, a human decision-maker, and a synthetic event source. AURORA's alarm is advisory, not an autonomous authorization to act.

Boundary choice Included responsibility What can go wrong if omitted
Sensor observations quality and missingness pretending unavailable evidence is negative evidence
Decision policy alarm threshold and logic ambiguous semantics of an “alert”
Human response authority and timing assuming an alarm causes a successful intervention
Shared infrastructure power / communications incorrectly independent failure probabilities

Lab — draw two valid architectures

  1. Draw AURORA as a physical decomposition (sensors, processor, link, console).
  2. Draw it again as a responsibility architecture (event, observer, decision, authority, intervention, audit).
  3. Mark the assumptions crossing each boundary; add one explicit interface contract to each arrow.
  4. Construct a scenario matrix with six ConOps scenes and measurable success criteria.
  5. Find a case where the physical diagram suggests the system is healthy but the responsibility diagram reveals a missing decision authority.

Acceptance artifact: 02-context-and-conops.md containing two diagrams, six scenes, and an assumption register.

Adversarial check

Ask whose incentives or hazards disappear when you draw the boundary. Inspect time: the system may be valid at design time but invalid after a software update, power failure, or policy change. Identify a boundary that would reverse your risk estimate.

Doctoral extension

Prove or empirically demonstrate boundary sensitivity: a result that is invariant under a well-specified boundary refinement is more reusable than one that holds only for a convenient drawing.

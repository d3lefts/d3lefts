# NIGHTFALL research communication and visual-language contract

**Editorial checkpoint:** 12 November 2026. This guide governs the public site copy and its proof-status presentation. It is not a mathematical certificate, publication approval or independent referee report.

## Reader journey

1. **Claim:** State the quantified hypothesis, model and output in ordinary language, then show the exact expression.
2. **Evidence:** Give the strongest *applicable* source, immutable receipt or checker, and its scope. A finite table, a named Lean declaration, a written analytic argument and an outside referee verdict are separate kinds of evidence.
3. **Limit:** State which implication is still missing, or which model, distribution, degree, oracle or review the claim does not cover.
4. **Next test:** Link the quickest discriminating proof attack or reproducible check and name the source checkpoint.

Readers should reach the canonical evidence in one click from a flagship claim. Do not send the primary hero action to a stale historical source and rely on readers to discover newer records.

## Status language (prefer precise verbs)

| Evidence | Use | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Named Lean result with pinned receipt | “This declaration compiled for the printed assumptions.” | “The theory was formally proved” when a global arrow is missing |
| Rational/exhaustive finite replay | “Exact for this finite witness/model and specified checker.” | “Optimal in general”; “independently peer-reviewed” |
| Written proof without non-author review | “Analytically proposed; internal audit completed; independent review open.” | “Established new law”; “landmark proved” |
| Conditional transfer | “If the scalar premise holds, the rank/posterior bound follows under the stated model.” | Omitting the premise; presenting a quantum oracle as a classical coordinate read |
| Historical record | “Preserved source / earlier checkpoint.” | “Latest proof” for a retired version |
| Published antecedent | Explicit authors, dates and conditions | Recasting an imported theorem as a NIGHTFALL discovery |

## Visual grammar

Keep the existing NULL FIELD / NEW MOON identities: dark editorial field, warm archive, serif headlines, mono evidence labels and one-pixel borders. The site should not switch to colorful callout cards, gradient status chips or decorative rankings.

- **Headline** names the question; avoid adjectives such as “landmark”, “settled” or “revolutionary”.
- **Leading paragraph** defines the object and says why it matters in no more than two compact sentences where possible.
- **Evidence rail** is the quiet `nf-evidence-rail`: three paired `dt`/`dd` items, reading horizontally on wide screens and vertically below 680px. Labels are “Model/Claim”, “Evidence”, and “Open/Limit” or their direct semantic equivalents.
- **Hero scope line** uses `nf-hero-proof-key` to state the three certification gates without implying a live feed.
- **Actions** are verb + useful object: “Read claim and evidence”, “Inspect exact witness”, “Review the missing proof step”. Avoid opaque destinations or ambiguous “Explore”.
- **Scientific UI** has no green success banners: a checked finite receipt is not a global theorem or priority win.
- **Source access** is always explicit. Full PDF, original LaTeX, proof passport, compilation receipt and exact checker must not be conflated.

## Accessibility and performance

Semantic headings in order, keyboard-readable direct links, visible focus, no content only communicated through color, exact anchors, no new heavy media dependency for explanatory material. Preserve the light/dusk palettes, reduced-motion behavior, active/archive separation and the homepage three-flagship editorial budget.

**Stop rule:** if a requested sentence requires a proof, novelty, source availability or current external record not supported by its attached receipt, write the exact uncertainty instead of strengthening the prose.

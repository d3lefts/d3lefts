A small living system inhabits this document. It reads what it touches, remembers what it finds, and gradually rewrites its habitat.

In an ordinary archive, a document waits patiently for a reader. Here, the relation is reversed. The reader is a creature of small rules and restless limbs. It moves through sentences as though they were a landscape, and discovers in every word a possible direction.

The creature has no map of the whole. Its world is local: a nearby phrase, a distant link, a trace of something visited before. Patterns emerge from this limited attention, the same way a path appears in grass after many footsteps.

01. The anatomy of attention

Every specimen is built from a small vocabulary of geometric forms. A circle holds a center. A line suggests a limb. A point remembers a location. Repeated in time, these simple elements acquire the appearance of intention.

Each segment follows the next. Each limb takes a step. A body is what these instructions look like when they agree.

There is no hidden picture moving across the page. The organism is drawn anew on every frame, as a consequence of its seed, its motion, and the words that attract it. Change the seed and you change the body it will become.

02. The logic of wandering

A forager seeks unfamiliar words. A weaver links distant locations with threads. A scatterer interrupts stable arrangements. These differences are small at the level of instructions, yet substantial in the shapes they leave behind.

Tap any word to offer the creature a destination. Allow it to walk without guidance and it will choose its own next fragment. It learns nothing about the truth of a sentence. It remembers only a sequence, a proximity, a rhythm.

The archive is a habitat. Reading is an action upon it.

03. A prediction becomes a passage

When enough words have been encountered, the creature starts to write. It uses a small local word-transition table: given a pair of words, it picks a plausible successor from words previously observed together. Its inventions are repetitions with a difference.

The resulting text is strange, often incomplete, occasionally suggestive. This is an experiment in the mechanics of prediction, not a language model that understands what it says. The distinction is part of the work.

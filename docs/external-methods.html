01 / Statement and limit

Exactly which conjecture?

For each prime p, choose any subset S of residues mod p. The binary circulant AS has entry 1 precisely when the column-minus-row residue lies in S. The conjecture said the number V(p) of distinct permanents among all such matrices was O(p).

External claim: V(p) ≥ c p2 for all sufficiently large primes.

The November 20 argument is due to Cinna Davis and GPT-6.2 Astra; Yair Lavi maintains the historical problem record, explicitly credits them, and reports an independent audit finding no gap. That reported review is distinct from our separate source-certification gates. The construction uses dense supports with p/3 ≤ |S| ≤ 2p/3. It does not resolve Resta–Sburlati's fixed-small-row-sum conjecture, including the four-ones-per-row case.

02 / Transferable operators

Three things worth reusing.

Representation

Fourier before brute force.

Diagonalize the circulant and control a few exceptional modes separately from the spectral bulk. A fourth-moment bound can replace individual estimates on every frequency.

Construction

Randomize; then repair.

Obtain an approximate Fourier profile through Bernoulli sampling, fix the exact support size, and steer the first harmonic with swaps without breaking the bulk budget.

Separation

Use an exact invariant.

Prime cyclic translation forces per(AS) ≡ |S| (mod p). Different row sums in the selected range cannot collide even when their numerical permanents are enormous.

The congruence generalizes: for any finite p-group G (even nonabelian), and the Cayley matrix Ax,y=1 if x−1y belongs to S, per(A) ≡ |S| (mod p). The p-group action on admissible permutations proves it: all nontrivial orbits vanish modulo p, leaving only right translations by members of S. This is an elementary generalization, not a novelty claim or a Lean certificate.

Another key improvement is centering before taking absolute values: when Z=p/T for T with Gamma(p+1,1) law, E[Z]=1 and Var(Z)=1/(p−1). A first-order Taylor term cancels in expectation, making second-order errors O(1/p) when the required derivative and tail bounds really hold.

03 / Evidence and replay

What can actually be checked?

An original, standard-library-only checker exhaustively verifies 700 supports: 172 prime-circulant supports (p=2, 3, 5, 7) and 528 supports across C4, C8 and nonabelian D8. It checks affine symmetries and modular congruences, uses independent permutation enumerations for selected cases, and exhibits a failing C6 control outside the theorem’s scope.

Verification limit. Finite integer tests confirm only these finite invariants. They do not prove the asymptotic quadratic bound. The uniform quantitative permanent approximation used by the authors traces to Divoux–Wang, arXiv:2610.23755; a source-by-source independent check of the extracted O(1/p) rate remains pending.

What must be checked before any stronger status?

Read the cited analytic proposition and proof at the version used by the authors; verify all quantifiers for the fixed spectral gap, matrix entries including zeros, the gamma tail and rook-polynomial error. Separately check that O(√p) swaps preserve the fourth-moment control. Only then evaluate a new, explicitly scoped theorem certificate. No automatic promotion follows from passing our finite checker.

How does this inform NIGHTFALL's own work?

Test spectral moment control in phase-retrieval recovery and product-space observability; look for centered error cancellations in quantum-simulation estimates; and use symmetry and modular labels to separate constructions before expensive optimization. These are proposed experiments, not proved transfers.
